What an injection site rotation log should capture

The useful unit is one complete weekly record—not just a body location. Log enough context to separate a site observation from a schedule change or a broader symptom pattern.

  • Date and exact time of the injection
  • General location recorded consistently in your own shorthand
  • Side or region so consecutive entries are easy to compare
  • Skin observation such as no change, tenderness, redness, itching, or bruising
  • Onset and duration of any observation rather than a vague end-of-week note
  • Weekly context including travel, illness, poor sleep, schedule drift, or a changed routine

Separate the site log from the symptom timeline

A local skin note and a whole-week side-effect pattern answer different questions. Keep them connected by timestamp, but tag them separately:

  • Local observation: record what you noticed near the logged location and when it changed.
  • Weekly symptom: record nausea, appetite, energy, bowel, sleep, mood, or wearable changes in the retatrutide side-effect timeline.
  • Schedule disruption: use the retatrutide missed-dose tracker when the weekly anchor moved.
  • Protocol change: tag the change in the retatrutide protocol tracker rather than treating it as an ordinary week.

Review four weeks without over-interpreting one entry

A log documents observations; it does not prove what caused them. When reviewing a month, ask factual questions.

  • Was each weekly location recorded, or are there gaps?
  • Did the same observation repeat, and did its timing repeat too?
  • Was the week affected by a changed injection day, illness, travel, or another confounder?
  • Is the note local to the injection area, or part of a wider week-long symptom pattern?
Make the history reusable: consistent labels are more valuable than elaborate prose. Pick short location tags and symptom tags you will recognize later, then use the same format every week.
