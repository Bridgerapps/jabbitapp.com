Mounjaro week-by-week timeline

Day 0: injection anchor

Record the exact timestamp, dose as prescribed, injection site, and whether this was your usual day and time.

  • Tag poor sleep, dehydration, illness, heavy exercise, travel, alcohol, or unusual meals.
  • If the dose was late or the week was already off schedule, say that immediately.

Day 1-2: onset window

This is often the highest-signal window for side-effect timing and wearable changes.

  • Log first symptom start time, not just the worst point.
  • Separate nausea, reflux, fatigue, appetite suppression, sleep disruption, anxiety, or higher resting HR.

Day 3-4: peak or fade

Note whether the pattern is settling down, lingering, or broadening into a harder recovery week.

  • Track bowel changes, intake, hydration, and whether work stress or poor sleep is taking over the story.

Day 5-7: late-week drift

Late-week notes are what make the next comparison useful instead of blurry.

  • Tag rebound hunger, constipation, low energy, travel, schedule drift, and next-shot timing pressure.
Common mistake: changing too many variables in the same week. If injection timing, travel, restaurant food, sleep, illness, and hydration all changed together, you do not have a clean Mounjaro side effect timeline yet. You have a noisy week.

What to log so the week is readable later

The goal is not a huge diary. The goal is a boring, repeatable structure that lets you compare one week against the next.

Core anchors

Date and time of injection, dose as prescribed, injection site, and whether the shot was on time, late, or part of a schedule reset.

Symptom timing

First symptom onset, peak window, rough severity, total duration, and whether it clustered in Day 1-2 or stretched across the week.

Wearable context

Morning resting heart rate, HRV trend, sleep quality, and exercise tolerance when the week feels wired or depleted.

Confounders

Hydration, food intake, caffeine, alcohol, travel, illness, menstrual cycle, life stress, and any missed-dose noise.
