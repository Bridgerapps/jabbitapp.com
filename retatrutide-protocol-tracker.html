What to track (high-signal, low-effort)

You don’t need perfect data — you need consistency. A protocol tracker is just a timeline that makes patterns obvious.

Fast start: track (1) dose date/time, (2) how you felt day 0–3, and (3) anything that could confound the week (travel, stress, illness).

1) Your schedule (the anchor)

Week Dose date/time Injection site Notes (early/late, travel, stress)
Week 1 YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM Left/right, area Anything unusual that week
Week 2 YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM Rotate site Notes
Week 3 YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM Rotate site Notes

2) Side effects timeline (a simple way to stay honest)

Instead of “I felt awful sometime this week,” capture a few timestamps. This is especially useful when you change dose timing or miss a dose.

Day Time What happened Severity + context
0 (dose day) HH:MM Nausea / reflux / fatigue / headache 0–10 + what you ate/drank, sleep, stress
1 HH:MM Appetite / GI / energy notes 0–10 + context
2–3 HH:MM Anything notable 0–10 + context

3) Missed/late dose notes (don’t lose the reason)

A missed-dose story without the reason is useless later. If you’re early/late, record the “why” and the symptoms that followed.

  • If you missed a dose: capture planned time vs actual time, plus what you noticed afterwards.
  • If you changed dose timing (morning vs night): log the first 2–3 weeks with extra timestamps.
This page does not provide dosing instructions. It’s a tracker template to help you keep a clean log you can review (or share with your clinician if you choose).
