What to log if retatrutide feels like it changes stress or HRV

The pattern usually gets blurred by ordinary life noise. A useful log keeps the dose timestamp and the obvious confounders together so the week is easier to read later.

  • Dose timing: exact date and time, plus whether the week was on schedule, early, or late.
  • HRV or wearable note: low, usual, or improving, plus the time window you noticed it.
  • Stress or anxiety symptoms: what happened and when it started relative to the dose.
  • Sleep and meals: a short note on poor sleep, heavy late meals, alcohol, travel, or illness.
  • Other side effects: nausea, reflux, constipation, or fatigue if they seem to travel with the same pattern.
High-signal rule: “felt off on Tuesday” is weak data. “Low HRV the morning after injection, anxious by afternoon, back toward baseline on Day 3” is a pattern.
