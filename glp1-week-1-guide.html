GLP-1 Week 1 Guide

What to Expect Your First Week on Ozempic, Mounjaro, Wegovy & Other GLP-1s

Starting a GLP-1 medication? Your first week is a journey. Most people experience a range of sensations as their body adjusts—from reduced appetite to mild nausea. This is completely normal.

This guide walks you through day by day what to expect, how to manage side effects, and when you should contact your provider.

Your First Week: Day by Day

Day 1 - Injection Day

Most people feel little to nothing immediately after their first injection. The medication needs time to build up in your system. You may feel a mild sting at the injection site (usually in your abdomen or thigh). Some users report feeling "full" faster at their next meal.

Day 2 - Early Appetite Changes

Appetite reduction typically begins on day 2. You might notice smaller portions satisfy you, or you skip meals without thinking about it. Some people experience mild bloating or gas. Stay hydrated and eat when hungry—even small amounts.

Day 3 - Peak Early Effects

Day 3 is often when early side effects peak. Nausea is most common here, along with possible fatigue. This is your body adjusting to the medication. Take it easy—rest, bland foods, and hydration are your friends.

Day 4-5 - Stabilization Begins

Early side effects usually start subsiding. Your stomach is adapting. Many people report clearer thinking and more energy as their body finds balance. Appetite suppression continues but becomes more manageable.

Day 6-7 - End of Week 1

By day 7, most acute symptoms have settled. You're likely eating regular (but smaller) meals. Some people lose 1-3 pounds this first week, though weight loss varies significantly. You're building a foundation for the weeks ahead.

Common First Week Side Effects

🟢 Very Common (Affected 30-50%)

  • Nausea - usually peaks days 2-4, then improves
  • Reduced appetite - the primary mechanism, expected
  • Fatigue - especially if eating less than usual
  • Bloating/gas - normal digestive adjustment

🟡 Common (Affected 10-30%)

  • Diarrhea - usually resolves within a week
  • Constipation - stay hydrated, consider fiber
  • Headache - often from dehydration or skipped meals
  • Dizziness - usually from low blood sugar or dehydration

🔴 Less Common (Affected <5%)

  • Vomiting - if severe, contact your provider
  • Injection site reactions - minor redness is normal
  • Rapid heartbeat - if persistent, seek medical advice

Week 1 Survival Tips

Eat Small, Eat Often

Even with reduced appetite, your body needs fuel. Focus on:

  • High-protein foods (eggs, Greek yogurt, chicken)
  • Complex carbs (oatmeal, whole grains)
  • Plenty of water (64+ oz daily)
  • Avoid greasy, spicy, or heavy foods this week

Pro tip: Keep crackers, ginger ale, and bland foods handy for nausea moments.

Start Low, Go Slow

Your provider started you on a low dose for a reason. This allows your body to adjust. Don't increase your dose early—follow your prescribed schedule.

Track Your Symptoms

Keep a simple log: what you ate, how you felt, any side effects. This helps you identify patterns and gives your provider useful information for adjustments.

Rest When Needed

Fatigue in week 1 is normal. Don't push yourself with intense workouts this week. Light walking is great—listen to your body.

When to Contact Your Provider

Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:
  • Severe vomiting or inability to keep fluids down for 24+ hours
  • Signs of pancreatitis (severe abdominal pain, back pain)
  • Symptoms of gallbladder problems (right upper quadrant pain, jaundice)
  • Allergic reaction (rash, swelling, difficulty breathing)
  • Thoughts of self-harm or severe depression
Contact your provider within 24-48 hours if:
  • Nausea is severe and not improving
  • Diarrhea persists more than 3 days
  • You're unable to eat for more than 24 hours
  • You have questions about your dose

First Week FAQ

Will I lose weight in week 1?

Possibly 1-3 pounds, mostly water weight. True fat loss takes time. Don't be discouraged if the scale doesn't move much this week—your body is adjusting.

Can I take anti-nausea medication?

Yes! Over-the-counter options like ginger, B6, or Pepto-Bismol can help. Your provider may also prescribe stronger anti-nausea medication if needed.

Should I exercise this week?

Light activity is fine—walking, stretching, gentle yoga. Avoid intense workouts until you feel stable. Your body is already working hard adjusting to the medication.

What if I feel fine?

Great! Not everyone experiences strong side effects. This doesn't mean the medication isn't working—appetite suppression may still occur even without noticeable symptoms.

When does it get easier?

Most people feel significantly better by week 2-3. Side effects typically subside as your body adjusts to the medication.

Can I drink alcohol week 1?

It's best to avoid alcohol your first week, especially if you're experiencing nausea or dizziness. Alcohol can worsen side effects and dehydrate you. Wait until you're feeling stable.

What Comes Next

Week 1 is the foundation. As you continue your GLP-1 journey:

  • Week 2-4: Side effects continue improving, appetite suppression stabilizes
  • Month 2: Typically your first dose increase (if prescribed)
  • Month 3-6: Steady weight loss, most side effects minimal
  • Month 6+: Maintenance discussions with your provider

Remember: You're building a new relationship with food and your body. Be patient—the first week is the hardest part.
