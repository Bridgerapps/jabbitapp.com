What to track every injection week

This page exists for the generic search intent behind “GLP-1 injection tracking guide.” Most people do not actually want an essay. They want a repeatable way to answer: what did I take, when did I take it, what changed after that, and which tracker page should I use if the week got messy.

High-signal rule: the best log is usually shorter than people think. Timestamp, medication, site, dose as prescribed, and one factual note often beats a long diary you will never read again.
  • Injection anchor: date, time, medication, and site.
  • Symptom timing: nausea, constipation, fatigue, stress, anxiety, appetite changes, sleep changes, or any other effect you are trying to compare against the clock.
  • Routine context: travel, unusual meals, poor sleep, illness, training, alcohol, or major schedule drift.
  • Missed-dose context: whether the week was late, skipped, restarted, or otherwise off the normal routine.
  • Protocol note: if the week involved other peptide or injectable workflow changes, keep that in the same timeline instead of splitting it across different apps or note threads.
Boundary: this is tracking guidance only. It does not tell you what dose to take, when to increase, or how to switch medications.

FAQ

Do I need to log every symptom?
No. Log the symptoms or context that help you interpret timing later. A shorter consistent log is usually more useful than an exhaustive one.

What if my question is really about stress, anxiety, HRV, or resting heart rate after injections?
Use the GLP-1 stress, anxiety, and HRV guide or the drug-specific anxiety/HRV pages if the pattern is clearly autonomic or wearable-related.

What if I use this for non-GLP-1 peptides too?
Start with the same timestamp-and-context logic, then move into the peptide protocol tracker or peptide tracking hub when the workflow goes beyond one GLP-1 medication.
