If you’re on a GLP‑1 medication, an injection log can be the difference between “I think I’m doing things consistently” and actually knowing what happened. This page is a practical checklist for what to record each week so you can notice patterns, reduce memory drift, and bring clearer notes to your next appointment — without turning your life into a spreadsheet.

Why an injection log helps

  • Consistency gets easier. It’s harder to accidentally drift a day or two when you can see your last injection timestamp.
  • Symptoms stop feeling random. A simple log can reveal “day 1–2 vs day 5–7” patterns over time.
  • Changes become explainable. If appetite, sleep, or GI symptoms shift, you can check what else changed that week (dose, timing, travel, stress).

The GLP‑1 injection log checklist

Start with these fields — they match what most people end up trying to remember later:

  • Date + time. Record the exact timestamp (not just “Sunday”).
  • Medication name. Useful if you ever switch, pause, or restart.
  • Dose. Whatever unit your prescription uses.
  • Injection site (and side). Example: “left thigh” or “right abdomen”.
  • Timing note. “As scheduled”, “late”, “early”, “travel day”.
  • Quick symptom check‑in. 10‑second ratings work well: nausea (0–10), constipation (0–10), fatigue (0–10), reflux (0–10).
  • One free‑text note. Keep it short: “dose change”, “poor sleep”, “high stress”, “heavy meal”.

A simple tracker template

Copy/paste this into Notes, a spreadsheet, or a journaling app:

Missed dose? What to note

This is educational only — follow your prescriber’s instructions. For tracking purposes, log what happened (instead of guessing what “should” have happened):

  • Planned date/time vs actual date/time (or “skipped”).
  • Reason (travel, pharmacy delay, forgot, side effects, etc.).
  • Any symptoms you noticed during the gap.

For a dedicated missed‑dose template, see Missed a GLP‑1 dose? What to log.

Side‑effect & symptom logging (without overthinking it)

The goal isn’t to diagnose yourself — it’s to notice patterns and reduce “memory drift.” Two easy approaches:

  • Daily 10‑second ratings: pick 3–5 symptoms and rate 0–10.
  • Event notes: only log when something happens (“nausea after dinner”, “constipation day 3”, “reflux at night”).

For a deeper educational overview (and what’s often worth logging), see GLP‑1 side effects guide.
