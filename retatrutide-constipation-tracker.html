What to log (minimum viable constipation tracking)

Most people don’t need more detail — they need consistent detail. Track the same few fields for 2–4 weeks, then adjust.

Rule of thumb: If logging feels annoying, cut it down to “bowel movement + discomfort + one sentence of context.” Consistency beats completeness.

Core fields

  • Date (and optional time)
  • Bowel movement (yes/no)
  • Straining (none / mild / moderate / severe)
  • Discomfort (0–10)
  • Hydration (roughly “low / normal / high” is enough)
  • Food pattern (bigger meals, low fiber, travel day, etc.)
  • Timing relative to injection (same day vs 24–72h later)
  • What helped (movement, routine, smaller meals, etc.)

Patterns worth watching

  • Injection timing (if constipation reliably peaks 24–72h after)
  • Hydration dips (low-fluid days can stack up quietly)
  • Meal size (bigger meals can amplify “slow gut” symptoms)
  • Travel / schedule disruption (routine changes often correlate)
  • Nausea overlap (constipation can make nausea feel worse)
Safety note: Severe belly pain, vomiting, inability to pass stool/gas, blood in stool, or rapidly worsening symptoms can be urgent — seek medical care.
