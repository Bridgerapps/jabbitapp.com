GLP-1 Comparison Guide

Ozempic vs Wegovy vs Mounjaro vs Zepbound — Which is right for you?

Choosing a GLP-1 medication can be overwhelming. Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound all work similarly but have important differences in effectiveness, side effects, cost, and approved uses.

This guide breaks down everything you need to know to make an informed decision.

Active Ingredients: Semaglutide vs Tirzepatide

GLP-1 medications fall into two categories based on their active ingredient:

Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy)

  • Single-action: Mimics only the GLP-1 hormone
  • Well-studied: Longest track record of any GLP-1
  • Available longer: FDA-approved since 2017

Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound)

  • Dual-action: Mimics both GLP-1 and GIP hormones
  • More effective: Studies show 20-25% more weight loss vs semaglutide
  • Newer: FDA-approved since 2022
Key Insight: Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound) tends to produce more weight loss than semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy), but both are highly effective. Individual response varies significantly.

Side Effects Comparison

Black Box Warning: All GLP-1 medications carry a warning about potential risk of thyroid C-cell tumors. Patients with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2) should not use these medications.

Dosing Comparison

Semaglutide (Ozempic/Wegovy)

  • Ozempic: 0.25mg → 0.5mg → 1mg → 2mg (max)
  • Wegovy: 0.25mg → 0.5mg → 1mg → 1.7mg → 2.4mg (max)
  • Injection: Once weekly, any day
  • Titration: 4 weeks minimum per dose

Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound)

  • Mounjaro: 2.5mg → 5mg → 7.5mg → 10mg → 12.5mg → 15mg (max)
  • Zepbound: 2.5mg → 5mg → 7.5mg → 10mg → 12.5mg → 15mg (max)
  • Injection: Once weekly, same day each week
  • Titration: 4 weeks minimum per dose
Pro Tip: Stay at a tolerable dose for at least 4-6 weeks before increasing. Many people find success at lower doses and never need the maximum.

Insurance Coverage

Coverage varies significantly between diabetes and weight loss formulations:

  • Ozempic/Mounjaro (diabetes): More likely covered by insurance with T2D diagnosis
  • Wegovy/Zepbound (weight loss): Coverage varies; many insurers still exclude weight loss medications
  • Medicare: Does NOT cover GLP-1s for weight loss (Wegovy, Zepbound)
  • Eligibility: Typically requires BMI ≥30, or BMI ≥27 with at least one weight-related condition

Which Should You Choose?

Choose Ozempic if:

  • You have Type 2 Diabetes
  • You want the most established option
  • Your insurance covers it for diabetes
  • You prefer starting with lower potency

Choose Wegovy if:

  • Weight loss is your primary goal
  • You want semaglutide specifically for weight loss
  • Your insurance covers Wegovy
  • You want the highest semaglutide dose available

Choose Mounjaro if:

  • You have Type 2 Diabetes
  • You want better glucose control
  • You want more effectiveness than semaglutide
  • Your insurance covers it

Choose Zepbound if:

  • Maximum weight loss is your priority
  • You want the most effective option
  • You've tried other GLP-1s without success
  • Your insurance covers it
Important: Never start, stop, or change your GLP-1 medication without consulting your healthcare provider. All of these medications require a prescription and medical supervision.

Making Your Decision

Consider these factors in consultation with your doctor:

  1. Your primary goal: Diabetes management vs weight loss
  2. Insurance coverage: What will your plan pay for?
  3. Side effect tolerance: Tirzepatide may have more GI side effects
  4. Effectiveness priority: Are you willing to try more options for better results?
  5. Track record: How important is having the longest safety data?

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I switch between GLP-1 medications?

Yes, but under medical supervision. If one isn't working or causing intolerable side effects, your doctor may recommend switching. Generally, you should wait at least 4 weeks after your last dose before starting a new GLP-1.

Q: What if I can't afford these medications?

Check manufacturer savings programs: Novo Nordisk (Ozempic, Wegovy) and Eli Lilly (Mounjaro, Zepbound) offer discount cards that can reduce costs significantly. Some patients also explore compounding pharmacies (where legal) or discuss alternatives with their provider.

Q: How long do I need to stay on GLP-1 medication?

These medications are generally intended for long-term use. Studies show most weight is regained when stopping GLP-1s. Work with your doctor to determine the right duration for your situation.

Q: Can I take GLP-1s with other diabetes medications?

Yes, but your doctor needs to monitor you closely. Combining GLP-1s with insulin or sulfonylureas can increase hypoglycemia risk. Dosage adjustments of other medications are often necessary.

Q: Do I still need to diet and exercise on GLP-1s?

Absolutely. GLP-1s work best combined with reduced calorie intake and increased physical activity. They are not magic pills—they work as tools to help you maintain a caloric deficit while feeling less hungry.
