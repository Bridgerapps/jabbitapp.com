What to record in a retatrutide dose log

The goal is not perfect journaling. The goal is enough structure to answer later: what changed, when did it change, and what followed?

Fast rule: if the entry only captures dose and date, you will still miss most of the useful pattern. Add the actual time, whether the week drifted, and one short note about what happened after.

Core fields that stay high-signal

  • Planned dose day and actual injection timestamp
  • Dose exactly as prescribed, written the same way each week
  • Schedule status: on time, early, late, skipped, or intentionally moved
  • Injection-site note if rotating sites matters to your workflow
  • Short symptom line with timing, such as appetite change, nausea, sleep disruption, low HRV, or unusual stress
  • Context note for travel, alcohol, illness, meals, poor sleep, or other confounders

Simple review workflow

  1. Log the dose as close to the actual event as possible.
  2. Add one sentence if the week included a timing shift, missed dose, or unusual context.
  3. Add symptom timing later only if something changed.
  4. Review a few weeks together instead of treating each dose as an isolated event.

What not to overcomplicate

  • Do not add ten symptom fields if you only ever review two.
  • Do not leave missed weeks blank. Write down the timing change.
  • Do not rely on memory for whether appetite, sleep, or stress changed after dose day.
