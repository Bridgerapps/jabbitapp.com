Insurance Coverage

Does Insurance Cover GLP-1s?

Coverage varies significantly by plan type:

  • Medicare Part D: Covers GLP-1s for Type 2 diabetes. Weight loss coverage varies by plan. As of 2026, some Medicare plans now cover GLP-1s for weight loss with prior authorization.
  • Commercial Insurance: Most cover Ozempic and Mounjaro for diabetes with prior authorization. Weight loss coverage (Wegovy, Zepbound) varies widely—many plans exclude it.
  • Employer Plans: Larger employers are increasingly adding GLP-1 weight loss coverage. Check your specific plan formulary.
  • State Medicaid: Coverage varies by state. Many cover GLP-1s for diabetes; fewer cover for weight loss.

How to Check Your Coverage

Step 1: Call the number on your insurance card and ask: "Is [medication name] covered for [diabetes/weight loss]? What's the prior authorization requirement?"
Step 2: Ask about tier placement, deductible, and copay amounts.
Step 3: Check if your plan requires step therapy (trying other medications first).

Other Discount Options

  • GoodRx: Offers discounted prices (typically $800-1,000/month). Not insurance but can be cheaper than full price.
  • SingleCare: Another prescription discount card with competitive pricing.
  • Pharmacy Comparison: Prices vary between CVS, Walgreens, Walmart, Costco, and independent pharmacies. Always compare.
  • Costco Pharmacy: Often has competitive pricing. No membership needed for prescriptions.

Patient Assistance Programs

Novo Nordisk Patient Assistance Program (PAP)

Free medication for qualifying patients.

  • Income limit: Typically 400-500% of federal poverty level
  • Insurance: Must not have prescription drug coverage
  • Apply: Through your healthcare provider at novocare.com

Eli Lilly Patient Assistance Program

Provides free GLP-1 medications to qualifying patients.

  • Income requirements: Based on household size and income
  • Apply: Through your doctor or at lillycares.com

Other Assistance Options

  • Pan Foundation: Provides financial assistance for medication costs. Income-based.
  • Patient Advocate Foundation: Helps navigate insurance and assistance programs.
  • State pharmaceutical programs: Many states have their own prescription assistance programs.

Compounded GLP-1s: An Alternative?

⚠️ Important Safety Note: Compounded GLP-1s are available but come with risks:
  • Not FDA-approved (though compounded for individual patients is legal)
  • Quality and potency can vary between compounders
  • Shortage list medications may be compounded—this is legal but has oversight challenges
  • Only use reputable 503A/503B compounding pharmacies

Compounded prices range from $200-400/month—significantly cheaper but with considerations.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Ozempic cost without insurance?

Without insurance or discounts, Ozempic costs approximately $968/month ($11,616/year). Use manufacturer coupons or discount cards to reduce this significantly.

Which GLP-1 is cheapest?

With coupons, most GLP-1s can be reduced to $25/month. Without coupons, Trulicity ($886/month) and Rybelsus ($935/month) are on the lower end of list prices. Compounded options can be $200-400/month.

Does Medicare cover GLP-1s for weight loss?

As of 2026, Medicare Part D does NOT cover GLP-1s specifically for weight loss alone. However, some Medicare Advantage plans are beginning to offer coverage. Coverage for Type 2 diabetes is more widely available.

How can I get GLP-1s for $25/month?

Most manufacturer savings cards reduce costs to $25/month for commercially insured patients. Eligibility typically requires having commercial insurance (not Medicare/Medicaid) and meeting certain criteria.

Are Ozempic and Wegovy the same price?

No. Wegovy (for weight loss) costs $1,349/month while Ozempic (for diabetes) costs $968/month. However, with insurance, your actual copay may be similar or the same.

What if my insurance denies GLP-1 coverage?

Options include: (1) Appeal the denial with help from your doctor, (2) Try manufacturer coupons, (3) Apply for patient assistance programs, (4) Consider compounded alternatives, (5) Check if your employer can make an exception.
