What to log every week

The goal is not a diary. The goal is a repeatable timeline you can compare across weeks.

  • Injection timestamp: exact date and time, plus whether the shot was on schedule, early, or late
  • Week label: stable week, travel week, dose-change week, missed-dose week, or illness week
  • Symptoms: nausea, reflux, constipation, diarrhea, fatigue, appetite swings, headache, sleep changes, or mood changes
  • Severity and timing: when it started, when it peaked, and when it faded
  • Context: hydration, meal size, alcohol, stress, poor sleep, travel, illness, or unusually low intake
  • Wearables: resting heart rate, HRV, sleep scores, and any “felt weird” notes that line up with them
Most common mistake: people compare one messy week to another messy week. If the schedule drifted, use the missed-dose tracker. If the signal feels more like stress, anxiety, HRV, or elevated pulse, branch into the dedicated autonomic pages instead of treating it like a generic side-effect week.

FAQ

Do GLP-1 side effects always follow the same day-by-day pattern?
No. Many people are searching for a timeline because the pattern is inconsistent. That is exactly why a simple weekly log is more useful than trying to remember the week afterward.
Should I track anxiety or HRV on the same timeline as nausea or fatigue?
Yes. The timeline is stronger when symptoms and context live together. If the signal looks more autonomic than digestive, branch into the dedicated stress, anxiety, and HRV page.
Do I need a separate tracker for late-dose weeks?
Usually yes. A moved injection can distort the whole week, so it helps to keep those weeks clearly labeled in the missed-dose tracker.
