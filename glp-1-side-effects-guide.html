Week-by-week GLP-1 side effect timeline to build

If you only keep one structure, keep this one. It makes the difference between an anecdote and a usable comparison across several injections.

Window What to capture Useful tags
Baseline / pre-shot How you felt before the injection or before a dose increase. Sleep, hydration, appetite, stress, illness, alcohol, travel.
Injection day Exact shot time, dose, site, and whether the week stayed on schedule. Normal week, dose increase, restart, missed-dose recovery, routine change.
Day 1-2 Whether symptoms start early or stay quiet. Nausea, reflux, appetite drop, low intake, fatigue, stress, anxiety, resting HR.
Day 3-4 Whether the pattern peaks, fades, or changes shape. Constipation, diarrhea, sleep disruption, HRV, workouts feeling worse, recovery lag.
Day 5-7 Whether you return toward baseline or the week stays messy into the next shot window. Appetite rebound, late meals, travel, dehydration, next-shot timing, schedule drift.

What to log every week

  • Dose context: medication or brand, exact timestamp, and whether it was a stable week or a change week.
  • Symptom timing: when it started, when it peaked, whether it faded, and whether the pattern repeated from the prior week.
  • Severity: use the same simple scale every week so you can compare like with like.
  • Confounders: sleep debt, hydration, alcohol, larger meals, travel, illness, caffeine, and heavy exercise can all distort the read.
  • Schedule drift: missed, late, or moved injections should be labeled immediately instead of buried in a note.
  • Wearable context: if you track resting heart rate, HRV, or sleep, note whether the shift started before or after the shot.

FAQ

When do GLP-1 side effects usually show up in the week?
That varies, which is exactly why the timeline matters. Some patterns show up within the first 24 hours, some peak on Days 2-3, and some only make sense once you compare several weeks side by side.
What is most useful to log after a GLP-1 shot?
The highest-signal fields are injection date and time, dose level, symptom onset and severity, hydration, sleep, stress, food intake, schedule drift, and any wearable changes like resting heart rate or HRV.
Should missed-dose weeks stay separate from normal weeks?
Yes. A late, skipped, or moved injection can change the whole pattern. Labeling that week clearly keeps you from drawing the wrong conclusion later.
Is this page treatment advice?
No. This page is educational and tracking-focused only. For treatment decisions, dosing, or urgent symptoms, follow licensed clinical guidance and the medication labeling.
