Concrete logging workflow

If you want a useful side-effect record, log the week the same way each time. That keeps one bad memory from turning into a fake trend.

When to log What to capture
Right after the injection Dose date and time, medication or brand, planned weekday anchor, and whether this was a normal week, increase, restart, or routine-change week.
First 24 hours Early symptoms, appetite shift, hydration, sleep debt, stress load, travel, and anything unusual that could muddy the read.
Days 2 to 3 Whether the symptoms peaked, faded, or changed shape, plus anxiety, HRV, resting heart rate, constipation, reflux, fatigue, or food-noise changes.
End of the week Compare the whole week against the prior one: milder, worse, same, or unclear. Tag whether schedule drift, illness, alcohol, or sleep likely confounded the week.

What to log on a side-effect timeline

  • Dose context: injection date, time, and whether it was a starter week, increase week, restart, or normal repeat.
  • Symptom window: 0 to 24 hours, 1 to 3 days, and the rest of the week.
  • Pattern modifiers: sleep debt, meals, hydration, alcohol, travel, exercise load, stress, or illness.
  • Schedule drift: whether the shot was late, missed, or moved to a different day.
  • Outcome: improved, stable, worse, or unclear compared with the prior week.

Logging fields worth keeping every week

  • Dose anchor: keep the planned weekday and the actual timestamp so late-dose weeks do not blur into normal weeks.
  • Symptom severity: use the same simple scale each week so you can compare like with like.
  • Wearable context: if you track HRV, resting heart rate, or sleep, note whether the change started before or after the injection.
  • Confounders: dehydration, intense exercise, poor sleep, travel, illness, alcohol, or a heavier meal can change the read.
  • Next step for your own notes: keep observing, route to the missed-dose page, or collect questions to raise with a clinician.
