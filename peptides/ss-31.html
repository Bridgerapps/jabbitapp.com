SS-31 (elamipretide): what the research shows

SS-31 (elamipretide, also called MTP-131 or Bendavia) is a four-amino-acid peptide that binds cardiolipin in the inner mitochondrial membrane. It is FDA-approved only as Forzinity, under accelerated approval, to improve muscle strength in Barth syndrome patients weighing at least 30 kg, at 40 mg a day under the skin.[1][3]

What the animal research shows

In very old mice, a single 3 mg/kg injection restored muscle energy output to young levels within an hour, and 8 weeks of daily dosing improved exercise tolerance.[4][5]

Human data

A 2021 trial in 39 older adults found a borderline rise in one hand muscle’s ATP output after a single infusion (P = 0.055 on the raw change), gone by day 7.[6] Across 13 placebo-controlled phase 2 or 3 trials in heart, kidney, muscle and eye disease, none clearly met its main goal, including a 218-person phase 3 in mitochondrial myopathy and a 176-person trial in dry macular degeneration.[7][8][9] FDA’s clinical and statistical reviewers recommended against approval for Barth syndrome; the office director approved it, accepting more uncertainty for a disease affecting about 150 Americans.[2]

Safety

Injection-site reactions occurred in every Barth patient on the drug in the randomized trial. The label warns of hypersensitivity reactions, eosinophilia, benzyl alcohol in the formulation and a metformin transporter interaction, and notes that carcinogenicity studies have not been done.[1][3]

What is not known

No trial has tested SS-31 for aging. The only registered aging study is a 30-person open-label safety study.[10] A phase 3 in dry macular degeneration reads out in the second half of 2027.[11] For the full paper trail, see our teardown below.
