Tirzepatide: approvals and key trials

Tirzepatide is a once-weekly agonist of the GIP and GLP-1 receptors. FDA approved it as Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes on May 13, 2022, and as Zepbound for chronic weight management on November 8, 2023.[1][2]

Approved uses

Mounjaro is approved as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes.[1] Zepbound is approved for chronic weight management in adults with a BMI of 30 or more, or 27 or more with at least one weight-related condition.[2] In December 2024 FDA approved Zepbound for moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity, the first medication approved for that condition.[3]

SURMOUNT-1: weight loss

In adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes, mean weight change at week 72 was −15.0% (5 mg), −19.5% (10 mg), and −20.9% (15 mg), versus −3.1% with placebo.[4] For a critical read of the design, safety, sponsor role, denominators, and weight regain after stopping, see the SURMOUNT-1 study teardown.

SURPASS-2: versus semaglutide in type 2 diabetes

All three tirzepatide doses lowered HbA1c more than semaglutide 1 mg (−2.01, −2.24, and −2.30 points versus −1.86) and were noninferior and superior to it. Weight reductions were also greater. Gastrointestinal events were the most common adverse events in both groups (nausea 17–22% versus 18%).[5]

Compounded versions
