Ipamorelin: what the research shows

Ipamorelin is a synthetic pentapeptide that stimulates growth hormone release through the ghrelin (GHRP) receptor. It is not an FDA-approved drug.[1][2]

Preclinical profile

Novo Nordisk researchers described ipamorelin in 1998 as the first selective GH secretagogue. In rats and swine it released GH with potency similar to GHRP-6, and in swine it did not raise ACTH or cortisol beyond the levels seen with GHRH, even at doses more than 200-fold above the ED50 for GH release.[1] These are animal findings.

Human trial

A multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 2 trial (NCT00672074) gave intravenous ipamorelin 0.03 mg/kg twice daily after bowel resection to treat postoperative ileus. Among 114 analyzed patients, it was well tolerated, but there were no significant differences from placebo in the key or secondary efficacy outcomes (median time to first tolerated meal 25.3 vs 32.6 hours, p = 0.15).[3]

Regulatory status

No FDA-approved drug product contains ipamorelin; a Drugs@FDA search returns no approved application.[2] Ipamorelin acetate is currently in category 2 under FDA’s 503B (outsourcing facility) interim policy, added September 29, 2023, and also appears among withdrawn 503A/503B nominations. FDA cites aggregation and impurity risk, unnatural amino acids that complicate characterization, and a published report of serious adverse events including death when ipamorelin was given intravenously for gastric motility.[4]

What is not known

There are no positive efficacy trials in humans and no long-term safety data. A 2026 review groups ipamorelin with unapproved peptides that have scarce rigorous human safety data.[5]
