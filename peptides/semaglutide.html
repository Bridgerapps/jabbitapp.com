Semaglutide: approvals and key trials

Semaglutide is a once-weekly GLP-1 receptor agonist approved by the FDA as Ozempic for type 2 diabetes (2017) and as Wegovy 2.4 mg for chronic weight management (June 4, 2021).[1][2]

Approved use for weight management

FDA approved Wegovy as an adjunct to a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity for chronic weight management in adults with a BMI of 30 or more, or 27 or more with at least one weight-related condition such as hypertension, type 2 diabetes, or dyslipidemia.[1]

STEP 1: weight loss

In STEP 1, adults with overweight or obesity and without diabetes had a mean body-weight change of −14.9% with semaglutide 2.4 mg versus −2.4% with placebo at week 68. Half of the semaglutide group (50.5%) lost 15% or more. Nausea and diarrhea were the most common adverse events.[3] For a critical read of the design, dropout, sponsor role, and weight regain after stopping, see the How much weight will you lose on Wegovy? (STEP 1 teardown).

SELECT: cardiovascular outcomes

SELECT enrolled 17,604 adults with established cardiovascular disease and overweight or obesity but no diabetes. Over a mean follow-up of 39.8 months, the primary endpoint (cardiovascular death, nonfatal heart attack, or nonfatal stroke) occurred in 6.5% on semaglutide versus 8.0% on placebo (hazard ratio 0.80; 95% CI 0.72–0.90). Adverse events led to stopping treatment in 16.6% versus 8.2%.[4] For a critical read of absolute risk, hierarchy, population limits, and safety, see the SELECT study teardown.

Compounded and unapproved versions

Compounded semaglutide is not the FDA-approved product. See the compounding enforcement explainer for FDA’s current position.
