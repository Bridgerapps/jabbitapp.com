Semax: what the research shows

Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro) is a synthetic analog of the adrenocorticotropin fragment ACTH(4-10), studied mainly in Russia and given intranasally in published work. It is not an FDA-approved drug, and its clinical evidence comes almost entirely from Russian studies.[1][2]

Russian clinical studies in stroke

A 2018 study of 110 patients after ischemic stroke, split into early and late rehabilitation groups with and without semax (two 10-day courses of 6,000 mcg/day), reported higher plasma BDNF and better Barthel index recovery in the semax subgroups.[3] An earlier 1997 study gave semax to 30 patients in the acute period of hemispheric ischemic stroke, compared them with 80 conventionally treated controls, and reported “some influence” on the rate of neurological recovery.[4] These were published in a Russian neurology journal by Russian groups, and the 2018 study does not describe blinding or placebo control in its abstract.[3] We read the full Russian text in our semax stroke teardown.

Animal data

In rats, a single intranasal dose of semax (50 mcg/kg) increased hippocampal BDNF protein about 1.4-fold and trkB activation about 1.6-fold, alongside improved conditioned avoidance learning.[1]

Regulatory status

No FDA-approved drug product contains semax; a Drugs@FDA search returns no approved application.[2] FDA lists semax (heptapeptide) among substances previously placed in compounding category 2 whose nominations were withdrawn, citing aggregation and impurity risk and stating it has “no, or limited, safety-related information for proposed routes of administration.”[5]

What is not known

There are no independent, placebo-controlled trials outside Russia and no Western regulatory review of efficacy or safety.[3][5]
