Selank: what the research shows

Selank is a synthetic peptide studied in Russia as an anxiolytic. It is not an FDA-approved drug, and nearly all of the clinical evidence comes from Russian research groups publishing in Russian.[1][2]

Russian clinical studies

  • Selank vs phenazepam (2014). A comparative study in 60 patients with anxiety and somatoform disorders reported anxiolytic and mild nootropic effects of selank, lasting a week after the last dose.[1]
  • Selank added to phenazepam (2015). In 70 patients (30 on phenazepam alone, 40 on phenazepam plus selank), the combination was reported to speed the response on HDRS and reduce several phenazepam side effects.[3]

Both studies come from the same Moscow author group, which includes researchers at the Institute of Molecular Genetics, Russian Academy of Sciences.[1][3] Independent replication outside Russia has not been published. We read both papers in Russian in our selank anxiety teardown.

Animal data

In rats under chronic mild stress, selank reduced anxiety measures in the elevated plus maze and changed the effect of diazepam when the two were combined.[4]

Regulatory status

No FDA-approved drug product contains selank; a Drugs@FDA search returns no approved application.[2] FDA lists selank acetate (TP-7) among substances previously placed in compounding category 2 whose nominations were withdrawn, citing aggregation and impurity risk, and states that it “lacks important information regarding any safety issues raised by selank acetate administered to humans.”[5]

What is not known

There are no large, independent, placebo-controlled trials, and there are no Western regulatory reviews of efficacy or safety.[1][5]
