Retatrutide: what the trials show

Retatrutide is an investigational once-weekly agonist of the GIP, GLP-1, and glucagon receptors, developed by Eli Lilly. It is not FDA approved; a Drugs@FDA search returns no approved application.[1][2]

Phase 2 in obesity

In a 48-week phase 2 trial of 338 adults with obesity, mean weight change was −8.7% (1 mg), −17.1% (4 mg), −22.8% (8 mg), and −24.2% (12 mg), versus −2.1% with placebo. Gastrointestinal adverse events were the most common and were dose-related.[2]

Phase 2 in type 2 diabetes

A randomized, double-blind phase 2 trial in 281 people with type 2 diabetes compared retatrutide with placebo and dulaglutide 1.5 mg and reported dose-related HbA1c reductions at 24 weeks.[3]

Phase 3: TRANSCEND-T2D-1

The first published phase 3 trial randomized 537 adults with type 2 diabetes managed by diet and exercise alone. At 40 weeks, HbA1c fell by 1.69–1.94 points with retatrutide 4–12 mg versus 0.81 with placebo, and body weight fell by 11.5–15.3% versus 2.6%. Discontinuations due to adverse events were 2–5% with retatrutide. The trial was funded by Eli Lilly.[4]

What is not known

Long-term safety and the results of the remaining phase 3 programs are still pending. Products sold online as “retatrutide” are not the investigational drug used in these trials and are unregulated.[1]
