We don’t treat “peptide” as one credibility bucket

Some peptides are FDA-approved medicines with large trial programs. Others are compounds with almost no human data that happen to be popular online. Those are completely different evidence situations, so every card below carries its own status. Browsing by class helps you find a compound; the badge tells you how much to trust what you read next.

Healing & recovery

Tissue, tendon, and gut repair. Heavily discussed in sport and recovery circles, but the human evidence is thin.

Longevity & skin

Anti-aging and cosmetic peptides. Interesting biology and big claims — the claims usually run well ahead of the human data.

Claims > evidence
Epitalon Research only

Pineal tetrapeptide

Telomerase and lifespan claims rest largely on one research group’s work.
GHK-Cu Research only

Copper tripeptide

Genuine cosmetic and wound-healing research topically; injectable use is unproven.

What the evidence supports

  • GLP-1 medicines (semaglutide, tirzepatide) have large randomized trials and labeled indications.
  • Some secretagogues have real, published pharmacology even without an approved product.
  • Approval status and evidence quality are not the same thing — we show both.
  • Compounding or easy online availability does not equal FDA approval.

What we won’t fake certainty about

  • Whether animal-only signals (BPC-157, TB-500) will hold up in humans.
  • Whether single-group longevity claims (epitalon) replicate independently.
  • Whether peptides approved abroad (semax, selank) meet FDA-grade evidence.
  • Long-term safety of research compounds used off-label without monitoring.

How to check any claim yourself

For approved drugs

Start with the FDA label, pivotal trial publications, and official safety communications.

For research compounds

Ask whether the literature is animal-only, uncontrolled, early-stage, or absent in humans.

For regulatory context

Use enforcement letters, shortage notices, and formal policy updates instead of vendor claims.
