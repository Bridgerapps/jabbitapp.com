KPV: what the research shows

KPV (lysine-proline-valine) is the last three amino acids of the hormone alpha-MSH. It is not an FDA-approved drug, and FDA reports no human exposure data for it by any route.[1][2]

What the animal research shows

KPV has anti-inflammatory effects in rodents from several independent labs. In mice, KPV in drinking water reduced chemically induced colitis, apparently after being carried into gut cells by the PepT1 transporter.[3][4][5] It does not appear to act through melanocortin receptors, and FDA says its molecular targets remain unknown.[6][1]

Human data

No published trial has given KPV to people.[1] A related dimer, CZEN-002, was tested as a vaginal gel in an open-label study of 20 women with yeast infections in 2003–2004; no journal publication followed.[7] Plain KPV does not pass through intact human skin in lab tests.[8]

Regulatory status

KPV was nominated to FDA as a 0.1% topical cream for wounds and inflammatory skin conditions; the nomination was withdrawn and FDA reviewed it on its own initiative, recommending against the compounding list. In July 2026 FDA’s advisory committee voted 8 to 6, with 1 abstention, in favor.[1][9] An FDA peptide compounding policy went to the White House for review on October 14, 2026.[10]

What is not known

There are no human pharmacokinetic data, no standard toxicity studies and no established human dose.[1] For the full paper trail, including the 1991 patent and dose math, see our teardown below.
