CJC-1295: what the research shows

CJC-1295 is a long-acting analog of growth hormone–releasing hormone (GHRH). It reached early human trials but is not an FDA-approved drug.[1][2]

Human trial data

Two randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind ascending-dose trials (28 and 49 days) in healthy adults aged 21–61 gave CJC-1295 subcutaneously. A single injection raised mean GH 2- to 10-fold for 6 days or more and IGF-I 1.5- to 3-fold for 9–11 days. The estimated half-life was 5.8–8.1 days, and with repeated doses IGF-I stayed above baseline for up to 28 days. No serious adverse reactions were reported in those trials.[1]

A follow-up study found that pulsatile GH secretion persisted during continuous stimulation with CJC-1295.[3]

Regulatory status and safety signals

No FDA-approved drug product contains CJC-1295; a Drugs@FDA search returns no approved application.[2] FDA lists CJC-1295 among substances previously placed in compounding category 2 whose nominations were withdrawn. FDA states it “has identified serious adverse events associated with CJC-1295 including increased heart rate and systemic vasodilatory reaction” and that “available clinical data are limited,” along with immunogenicity and impurity concerns.[4]

What is not known

The published human data are short trials in healthy volunteers measuring hormone levels, not clinical outcomes. There are no long-term safety data, and gray-market products are not the material used in those trials.[1][5]
