What this page is (and isn’t)

This is a logging template. It doesn’t tell you what to take, when to change dose, or how to treat symptoms. It helps you capture a clean, consistent timeline so you can spot patterns (or confirm there aren’t any).

What to log each day

  • Symptom (nausea, reflux/heartburn, constipation, diarrhea, fatigue, headache, mood, sleep)
  • Severity (0–10) and duration
  • Injection timestamp (and the day-of-week rhythm)
  • Food + hydration notes (big changes only)
  • Other changes (travel, illness, missed dose, new meds/supplements, alcohol)
  • What helped (if anything) — purely observational

Side effect log vs timeline: use the right view

A side effect log captures each symptom event. A timeline compares where those events fall across the injection week. Start here when you need a simple daily record, then move into the narrower page that matches the pattern you are seeing.

How to spot “timeline” patterns (without overfitting)

Many once-weekly meds have a weekly rhythm. Logging helps you answer questions like:

  • Do symptoms cluster 0–48h after injection?
  • Do symptoms show up on day 3–5 instead?
  • Are symptoms worse after dose changes or a missed dose?
  • Do they line up with travel, stress, or sleep disruption?

A weekly review that takes two minutes

At the end of the injection week, reduce the daily entries to a short factual summary. This makes the log easier to compare later and more useful if you choose to share it with a clinician.

  • Most noticeable symptom: name one, plus its peak severity.
  • Timing: note the day it started, peaked, and eased.
  • Week type: label it stable dose, dose change, late dose, travel, illness, or disrupted routine.
  • Context: record only meaningful changes in sleep, meals, hydration, alcohol, stress, or other medications.
  • Next comparison: identify the one pattern you want to watch next week without drawing a conclusion from a single entry.
Stress or wearable pattern? If the main change is anxiety, lower HRV, disrupted sleep, or a higher morning pulse, use the Ozempic anxiety + HRV tracker or Ozempic resting-heart-rate timeline instead of burying that pattern in a general symptom note.

Quick checklist

  • Log daily (even “0” days).
  • Write down big changes (travel, illness, missed dose).
  • Keep notes short and factual.
  • Bring the log to appointments.

When to treat it as urgent

If you have severe symptoms, signs of dehydration, fainting, chest pain, trouble breathing, or anything that feels dangerous, treat that as urgent and seek appropriate care.
