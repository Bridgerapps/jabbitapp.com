FDA warning letters on telehealth marketing of compounded GLP-1s

On March 3, 2026, FDA announced 30 warning letters to telehealth companies for false or misleading claims about compounded GLP-1 products on their websites.[1]

What the letters targeted

FDA said the main violations were claims implying that compounded products are the same as FDA-approved products, and branding that implied the telehealth company was the compounder.[1] Its guidance for telehealth companies also lists calling a compounded drug a generic, claiming FDA approval, claiming it is clinically proven to match the approved drug, and claiming it comes from an “FDA-approved” pharmacy, since FDA does not approve or license facilities.[2]

Context

FDA described this as the second group of telehealth letters since a September 2025 crackdown on misleading direct-to-consumer drug ads, and said it had sent thousands of such letters in six months.[1] A warning letter is a regulatory notice to a company. It is not a recall and is not a ruling about any individual’s treatment.
