Alcohol and GLP-1 medicines: what the evidence shows

The semaglutide and tirzepatide labels do not include a specific alcohol interaction, but several of their warnings overlap with effects of drinking. A small 2025 randomized trial is the best human evidence so far on GLP-1 medicines and drinking.[1][2][3]

The 2025 semaglutide trial

In a 9-week phase 2 trial, 48 non-treatment-seeking adults with alcohol use disorder received low-dose semaglutide (up to 1.0 mg) or placebo. Semaglutide reduced alcohol consumed in a laboratory drinking session, drinks per drinking day, and weekly craving. It did not change average drinks per day or the number of drinking days. The authors called this initial evidence that justifies larger trials.[1]

Label warnings that overlap with drinking

  • Gastrointestinal effects: nausea was reported in 44% of Wegovy patients versus 16% on placebo, and vomiting in 24% versus 6%.[2]
  • Kidney injury from dehydration: both labels warn about acute kidney injury, and Wegovy’s describes reports mostly in patients whose GI reactions led to dehydration.[2][3]
  • Hypoglycemia: the Wegovy label says people with diabetes taking it with insulin or an insulin secretagogue such as a sulfonylurea may have a higher risk of low blood sugar, including severe episodes.[2]

What is not known

The trial was small and short and did not test alcohol safety in people taking these drugs for weight loss.[1] Questions about drinking while on a specific medicine belong with the prescriber.
