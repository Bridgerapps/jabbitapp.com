GLP-1 Hair Loss Guide

Why it happens, how to prevent it, and proven recovery strategies

Hair loss while on GLP-1 medications like Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound, or tirzepatide is remarkably common. If you're experiencing thinning hair, you're not alone—and it's usually temporary and treatable.

Why GLP-1 Medications Cause Hair Loss

Hair loss on GLP-1 drugs is almost always telogen effluvium—a temporary shedding condition triggered by physiological stress on the body. This isn't your genes; it's your body's response to rapid changes.

Primary Causes:

  • Rapid weight loss – The body shifts resources away from non-essential functions like hair growth
  • Caloric restriction – Reduced nutrient intake can trigger starvation responses
  • Protein deficiency – Hair is made of protein; inadequate intake affects growth cycles
  • Micronutrient depletion – Zinc, iron, vitamin D, and B vitamins are crucial for hair health
  • Metabolic stress – Significant changes in blood sugar and metabolism affect hair follicles
Good news: Telogen effluvium is almost always reversible. Once the trigger is identified and addressed, hair typically grows back within 3-6 months.

Prevention Strategies

1. Prioritize Protein Intake

GLP-1 medications reduce appetite, which can lead to insufficient protein consumption. Hair requires approximately 50-80 grams of protein daily for optimal growth.

  • Aim for 1.2-1.6g of protein per kg of body weight daily
  • Include protein at every meal
  • Consider protein supplements if struggling to meet targets
  • Best sources: eggs, fish, poultry, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, legumes

2. Maintain Essential Micronutrients

3. Gradual Weight Loss

If possible, aim for 1-2 pounds per week rather than rapid weight loss. Slower weight loss reduces the physiological stress that triggers telogen effluvium.

Recovery Strategies

If You're Already Experiencing Hair Loss:

  1. Don't panic – Stress worsens hair loss
  2. Check your labs – Request iron, ferritin, vitamin D, zinc, and thyroid panels
  3. Increase protein – Even if eating less, prioritize protein density
  4. Consider supplements – A quality multivitamin can help fill gaps
  5. Be patient – New growth typically appears in 3-6 months

Evidence-Based Supplements

Note: Always consult your healthcare provider before starting any supplement, especially if on other medications.
  • Multivitamin – Covers baseline micronutrient needs
  • Collagen peptides – Some evidence supports hair/skin benefits
  • Omega-3 fatty acids – Support scalp health and reduce inflammation
  • Saw palmetto – May help with androgen-related thinning (limited evidence)

Peptide Support for Hair Health

Some peptides have shown promise for hair growth in research settings:

  • BPC-157 – May support tissue repair including hair follicles
  • GHK-Cu – Shown in some studies to promote hair follicle function
  • PT-157 – Similar to BPC-157, potential tissue repair benefits
Research note: Peptide research for hair growth is preliminary. Most evidence comes from animal studies or in vitro research. Human clinical trials are limited.

When to See a Doctor

Consult a healthcare provider if:

  • Hair loss persists beyond 6 months
  • You notice bald patches (not just general thinning)
  • You have symptoms of thyroid dysfunction
  • Your bloodwork reveals deficiencies
  • Hair loss significantly impacts your quality of life

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my hair grow back?
Yes. In nearly all cases of GLP-1-induced telogen effluvium, hair grows back once the trigger (usually rapid weight loss or nutrient deficiency) is addressed. Full recovery typically takes 3-6 months.
How long does hair loss last?
The active shedding phase typically lasts 2-4 months. New hair growth becomes visible around month 3-4, with significant improvement by month 6.
Does dose matter?
There's no clear evidence that specific doses cause more hair loss. It appears to be more related to the rate of weight loss and individual nutritional status rather than medication dose.
Should I stop my GLP-1 medication?
Generally no. Hair loss is usually temporary and reversible. Work with your provider to address nutritional gaps rather than stopping effective treatment. The benefits of the medication often outweigh the temporary hair loss.
Do compounds cause more hair loss?
There's no evidence that compounded tirzepatide or semaglutide causes more hair loss than brand-name versions. The mechanism (telogen effluvium from rapid weight loss) is the same.
