How to Get Compounded Tirzepatide Safely

With shortages of name-brand tirzepatide and prices hovering around $1000/month, compounded versions are increasingly popular. But not all compounding is equal.

Understanding Compounding

Compounding pharmacies create custom versions of FDA-approved medications when:

  • There's a shortage (current situation)
  • Patient needs a specific dose not commercially available
  • Patient has allergies to inactive ingredients

503A vs 503B Pharmacies

503A Pharmacies

  • Compound for individual prescriptions
  • State-board regulated
  • Must use FDA-registered facilities
  • Generally more personalized service

503B Outsourcing Facilities

  • Can compound in bulk
  • FDA inspected
  • Higher volume, often lower prices
  • Must meet stricter quality standards

Red Flags to Avoid

Walk away if:

  • No prescription required
  • Prices too good to be true (under $200/month is suspicious)
  • No pharmacy contact information
  • Can't verify licensing
  • No third-party testing available
  • Pressure to buy quickly

Green Flags to Look For

  • 503A or 503B compliant (ask for verification)
  • Third-party lab testing (COAs available)
  • Transparent pricing
  • Licensed pharmacist available for questions
  • Good online reviews
  • Requires valid prescription

Questions to Ask

  1. Are you a 503A or 503B pharmacy?
  2. Can you provide a certificate of analysis (COA)?
  3. Who is your API supplier?
  4. How do you handle quality control?
  5. What is your return policy?

The Cheat Sheet

Legitimate compounded tirzepatide:

  • Costs $250-400/month
  • Requires prescription
  • Comes from US-based pharmacy
  • Has verifiable testing
  • Has licensed pharmacist available

Track Everything

Once you have your source, track your protocol in an app. Document which pharmacy, lot number, dose, and your results. This creates your personal data and helps identify what works.
