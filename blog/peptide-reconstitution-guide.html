How to Reconstitute Peptides: Complete Guide

Getting the math wrong means either wasting expensive peptides or accidentally taking too much. Here's how to do it right.

What You'll Need

  • Your peptide vial (lyophilized powder)
  • Bacteriostatic water (most common)
  • Needle and syringe
  • Calculator (or Jabbit app)

Common Ratios

1:1 Ratio (Most Common)

1mg peptide + 1ml water = 1mg/ml solution

1:2 Ratio

1mg peptide + 2ml water = 0.5mg/ml solution

2:1 Ratio

2mg peptide + 1ml water = 2mg/ml solution

Example: Semaglutide

You have a 5mg vial of semaglutide and want a 1mg/ml solution:

Add 5ml of bacteriostatic water

Now when you draw 0.1ml, you're getting 0.1mg.

The Formula

Water (ml) = Peptide (mg) ÷ Desired Concentration (mg/ml)

Example: 5mg peptide, want 2mg/ml:

5 ÷ 2 = 2.5ml water

Storage After Mixing

  • Refrigerate - Most peptides last 30-60 days refrigerated
  • Freeze - Some peptides can be frozen in single-use aliquots
  • Avoid light - Store in dark place
  • Check stability - Different peptides have different shelf lives

Common Mistakes

  1. Using regular water - causes pH issues, peptides degrade
  2. Adding water too fast - causes foaming
  3. Not waiting - let peptide dissolve naturally
  4. Shaking vigorously - damages peptide structure
  5. Wrong calculation - double-check your math
