GLP-1 Side Effects: Nausea, GI Issues, and How to Manage

GLP-1 medications like semaglutide and tirzepatide are incredibly effective, but side effects are common. Here's how to manage them.

Why Do GLP-1s Cause Side Effects?

GLP-1s slow gastric emptying - food stays in your stomach longer. This is what creates the feeling of fullness, but it also causes nausea and GI issues.

Most Common Side Effects

  • Nausea - Most common, usually in first weeks
  • Vomiting - Less common, usually from overeating
  • Diarrhea - Often resolves over time
  • Constipation - Less common than diarrhea
  • Abdominal pain - Usually mild
  • Acid reflux - Can worsen with higher doses

Timeline

  • Week 1-2: Worst nausea typically
  • Week 3-4: Symptoms often improve
  • Month 2+: Usually subsides significantly

Proven Management Strategies

1. Start Low, Go Slow

Don't rush to maintenance dose. Stay at each dose for 4+ weeks. If symptoms are bad, go back down.

2. Eat Smaller Meals

Overeating on GLP-1s is the #1 cause of vomiting. Eat 3 small meals + 2 snacks. Stop when you feel 70% full.

3. Avoid Trigger Foods

  • Greasy/fried foods
  • High-fat foods
  • Spicy foods
  • Alcohol
  • Large portions

4. Stay Upright

Don't lie down after eating. Wait 2-3 hours before lying down.

5. Ginger Helps

Ginger tea, ginger candies, or ginger supplements can reduce nausea.

6. OTC Medications

  • Pepto-Bismol
  • Antacids
  • Motion sickness medication (Bonine)

7. Stay Hydrated

Small sips throughout the day. Dehydration worsens nausea.

When to Lower Your Dose

  • Vomiting more than 2x per week
  • Can't keep food down
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Symptoms last beyond 4 weeks at each dose

When to See Your Doctor

  • Severe, persistent vomiting
  • Signs of dehydration (dizziness, dark urine)
  • Abdominal pain that doesn't go away
  • Gallbladder symptoms
  • Pancreatitis symptoms (severe pain, fever)

The Good News

For most people, side effects improve significantly after the first month. The key is patience, starting low, and not rushing to higher doses.
