GLP-1 Surgery Guide

Pre-operative and post-operative considerations for patients on GLP-1 medications

Understanding GLP-1 Medications and Surgery

If you're taking Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound, or other GLP-1 medications and have upcoming surgery, it's essential to understand how these medications interact with surgical procedures, anesthesia, and the recovery process.

Key Takeaway: GLP-1 agonists slow gastric emptying, which increases the risk of aspiration during anesthesia. Most surgeons require patients to pause GLP-1 medications before elective surgery.

Why GLP-1s Require Special Surgical Planning

GLP-1 receptor agonists work by:

  • Slowing gastric emptying: Food stays in your stomach longer than normal
  • Reducing appetite: This can affect nutrition before and after surgery
  • Delaying absorption: Oral medications may not absorb as expected
Aspiration Risk: During general anesthesia, patients cannot protect their airway. A full stomach increases the risk of stomach contents entering the lungs (aspiration), which can cause serious complications including pneumonia.

Pre-Operative Guidelines

Important: These are general guidelines. Always follow your specific surgeon's and anesthesiologist's instructions. The hold time may vary based on your specific situation, the type of surgery, and your dose.

What to Tell Your Surgical Team

Before surgery, make sure your surgical team knows:

  • Which GLP-1 medication you're taking
  • Your current dose
  • When you last took your medication
  • Any other diabetes medications (especially insulin or sulfonylureas)
  • Your blood sugar patterns, especially if you experience hypoglycemia

Blood Sugar Management During the Hold

When you pause your GLP-1 medication before surgery:

  • Monitor blood sugar more closely: You may notice increases as the medication leaves your system
  • Adjust other medications if needed: Your doctor may temporarily modify insulin doses
  • Stay hydrated: This helps manage blood sugar and supports recovery
  • Resume normal eating: Your appetite may return, which is normal

Day of Surgery Instructions

General Rule: For morning surgery, don't take your GLP-1 dose. For evening surgery, skip the dose the day before. Your anesthesiologist will provide specific instructions.

Post-Operative Considerations

Resuming GLP-1 Medications After Surgery

After your procedure:

  • Wait for clear liquids: Most surgeons want you eating normally before resuming
  • Start with your normal dose: Don't double up to "make up" for missed doses
  • Monitor for nausea: Post-op nausea is common; GLP-1s can contribute
  • Stay hydrated: Important for recovery and to prevent hypoglycemia
Recovery Tip: Start with bland, easy-to-digest foods (crackers, toast, rice) for the first day after resuming your GLP-1. This helps your stomach readjust.

Nutrition for Post-Surgical Recovery

While recovering from surgery on GLP-1 medications:

  • Prioritize protein: Essential for tissue repair
  • Stay hydrated: Aim for 8+ glasses daily
  • Eat smaller, more frequent meals: Your stomach capacity may be reduced
  • Avoid heavy or fatty foods initially: They may cause discomfort
  • Consider protein supplements: If eating enough is difficult

Signs to Watch For

Contact your healthcare provider if you experience:

  • Severe nausea or vomiting that won't stop
  • Blood sugar readings consistently above 250 mg/dL
  • Signs of infection at the surgical site
  • Difficulty swallowing
  • Severe abdominal pain

Special Considerations

Bariatric Surgery Patients

If you've had or are planning bariatric surgery:

  • Coordinate closely between your bariatric surgeon and surgical team
  • Nutrient absorption may already be compromised
  • Protein requirements are higher
  • Vitamin supplementation is likely needed

Emergency Surgery

If you need emergency surgery:

  • Tell medical personnel about your GLP-1 use immediately
  • They will manage aspiration risk accordingly
  • Blood sugar may be harder to control initially
  • Post-op monitoring will be more intensive

Working with Your Healthcare Team

The key to safe surgery while on GLP-1 medications is communication:

  1. Tell all your doctors: Not just your surgeon, but your primary care and any specialists
  2. Ask questions: If you're unclear about any instructions, ask
  3. Bring your medication list: Include GLP-1 and all other medications
  4. Have a backup plan: Know what to do if surgery is rescheduled
  5. Monitor after: Track blood sugar and symptoms post-operatively

Frequently Asked Questions

How long before surgery should I stop Ozempic/Wegovy?

For most weekly GLP-1s, the recommendation is to stop at least 1 week before elective surgery. However, this can vary based on your dose, the type of surgery, and your individual risk factors. Always follow your surgeon's specific instructions.

What if I accidentally took my GLP-1 before surgery?

Tell your surgical team immediately. They may need to delay the procedure, especially if you took it within 24 hours of surgery. The risk is primarily related to delayed gastric emptying increasing aspiration risk.

Will my blood sugar be high after stopping GLP-1 for surgery?

You may see some increase in blood sugar as the medication leaves your system, especially if you're on higher doses. This is typically temporary. Your healthcare team can help you manage this with other medications if needed.

Can I take my GLP-1 after minor procedures like dental work?

For minor procedures that don't require general anesthesia or deep sedation, you may be able to continue your GLP-1. However, always confirm with your healthcare provider, as local anesthesia protocols vary.

What if I'm on GLP-1 for diabetes and need surgery?

Work closely with your endocrinologist and surgical team. You may need to temporarily switch to other diabetes medications that have less impact on gastric emptying. Blood sugar monitoring will be important before and after surgery.
