Home / Research / Muscle loss on GLP-1s

Evidence review · Ozempic, Wegovy and Rybelsus (semaglutide), Zepbound and Mounjaro (tirzepatide) · DXA and MRI body-composition studies, muscle-drug trials, protein and creatine evidence, guidelines, ClinicalTrials.gov review · Sources checked October 19, 2026

Published October 19, 2026 · Updated October 21, 2026

Muscle loss on Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound or Mounjaro: about a quarter to a third of the weight lost is lean mass. Lifting and protein are what guidelines back; creatine and the new muscle drugs are unproven on these drugs.

In four recent trials, about 29% to 34% of the weight people lost on semaglutide or tirzepatide alone was lean mass on a DXA scan.[6][5][2][4] That’s in the same range as diet and bariatric surgery, and “lean” on DXA includes water and organs as well as muscle.[4][10] Direct MRI scans show thigh muscle shrinking in line with the weight lost, while the fat inside the muscle goes down.[12][11] Strength and function mostly hold up in the trials that tested them. The clearest warning signs come from older people.[11][13][6][14] Experimental muscle drugs cut the lean share to roughly 8–18%, but none is approved, and one combination had serious safety problems.[1][3] Guidelines back resistance training and enough protein.[15][16] We read the ClinicalTrials.gov records: of 70 registered studies on muscle during GLP-1 treatment, 3 have posted results, and the only creatine trial is still recruiting.[26]

Why this matters now

On October 1, Regeneron presented one-year COURAGE results at the European diabetes meeting (EASD). It said its antibody trevogrumab preserved about 70% of the thigh muscle that semaglutide alone took off, and that the paper is in press at The Lancet. We found no published paper as of October 19.[1] In September, a Texas A&M study reporting that creatine added lean tissue in middle-aged adults even without exercise went from a university release to ScienceDaily.[23][22] On X, posts tying GLP-1 drugs to muscle loss ran at 13 to 29 a day last week, a small but steady stream.[25] The same audience is arguing about cardio versus lifting on these drugs and about testosterone as a muscle fix. For anyone over 40 on a weekly shot, the question is practical: how much muscle am I losing, and what actually protects it?

The short answer

  • Some lean loss is normal for any big weight loss. On these drugs it runs about a quarter to a third of the weight lost, similar to diet or surgery.[4][15]
  • Lean mass isn’t all muscle. DXA lumps in water, organs and bone. MRI shows thigh muscle shrinking about as much as expected for the weight lost, with less fat inside it.[10][12]
  • Function mostly holds in trials, but older people are the concern. Chair stands and walking distance held or improved in two trials. In one company-reported trial of people over 60, 42.6% on semaglutide alone lost at least a tenth of their stair-climb power in 16 weeks.[11][13][6]
  • Lifting and protein are what guidelines recommend. No randomized trial on these drugs has published results for either.[15][16][26]
  • Creatine works with lifting in older adults, but it’s untested on GLP-1s. The one trial is due in 2027.[18][26]
  • The muscle drugs aren’t available and aren’t proven to help strength. Of the four with published or presented results, none registered a strength or function outcome.[26]

How much of the weight lost is lean mass?

The big approval trials measured body composition by DXA in small subgroups. In STEP 1, 140 people on semaglutide had DXA scans. Fat and lean mass both fell in kilograms, but fat fell more, so lean mass made up a bigger share of what was left.[8] In SURMOUNT-1, fat mass fell 33.9% on tirzepatide versus 8.2% on placebo, and the fat-to-lean ratio dropped from 0.93 to 0.70.[9] The Wegovy label puts it plainly: semaglutide “lowers body weight with greater fat mass loss than lean mass loss.”[14]

The newer muscle-drug trials report the share directly, because that’s what they’re trying to change. In their GLP-1-plus-placebo groups, lean mass was 31.9% of weight lost in QUALITY (median, people over 60, 16 weeks), 30.2% in EMBRAZE (24 weeks), 34% in COURAGE (26 weeks) and 28.9% in BELIEVE (48 weeks, our arithmetic from the published fat loss index of 71.1%).[6][5][2][4] The BELIEVE authors write that with calorie restriction of any kind, including lifestyle change, incretin drugs and bariatric surgery, lean mass makes up roughly 25% to 40% of weight lost.[4] The U.S. nutrition advisory likewise says the losses are similar to bariatric surgery and very-low-calorie diets. It cites modeling that puts actual muscle at 10–15% of weight lost in women and 20–25% in men without strength training.[15]

Lean mass is not the same as muscle

DXA splits the body into fat, bone and “lean,” and lean “includes not only muscle but also organs, bone, fluids, and water in fat tissue,” as a 2024 review put it.[10] When fat tissue shrinks, the water in it goes too, and DXA counts that as lean loss. MRI measures muscle directly:

  • SURPASS-3 MRI (tirzepatide, 246 people with type 2 diabetes, 52 weeks): thigh muscle volume fell 0.64 L. That was almost exactly what UK Biobank data predict for that much weight loss (a difference of 0.04 L, not significant). Fat inside the muscle fell more than expected. The study was Lilly-funded and post hoc.[12]
  • STEP UP MRI (semaglutide 7.2 or 2.4 mg, 72 weeks): thigh muscle volume was 1.1 L lower than placebo, but the confidence interval crossed zero (p=0.07). Muscle fat infiltration fell 0.92 points. Only 55 of 1,407 participants had usable scans, and just 6 of them were on placebo.[11]
  • COURAGE MRI (semaglutide 2.4 mg, 52 weeks): fat-free thigh muscle fell 1.03 L on semaglutide alone in a 75-person substudy, versus about 0.3 L with trevogrumab added.[1][2]

The 2024 review’s reading is that muscle changes on these drugs “appear to be adaptive”: about what you’d expect for the weight lost, with better muscle quality. It adds that older age and more severe disease may change who is a good candidate.[10]

Does it matter for strength and function?

The reassuring data. In the STEP UP MRI substudy, people did a mean of 13 chair stands in 30 seconds at the start and 15 at week 72. That figure pooled the semaglutide and placebo groups.[11] In STEP-HFpEF, 529 people with heart failure and obesity walked 21.5 m farther in six minutes on semaglutide and 1.2 m farther on placebo.[13] Less fat inside muscle, seen in SURPASS-3 and STEP UP, is generally a sign of better muscle quality.[12][11][10] In EMBRAZE, apitegromab saved 1.9 kg of lean mass. The exploratory strength and chair-stand tests showed “no notable differences” between groups, so the extra lean mass didn’t translate into measurable function over 24 weeks.[5]

The worrying data. QUALITY enrolled people over 60. After 16 weeks on Wegovy with placebo, 42.6% had lost at least 10% of their stair-climb power, compared with 19.4% with enobosarm added. That comes from a company press release, not a peer-reviewed paper.[6] The Wegovy label reports hip and pelvis fractures in 2.4% of people 75 and older on the drug in the cardiovascular outcomes trial, versus 0.6% on placebo. The label doesn’t say why, and nothing shows muscle loss caused them.[14] In one of only three registered muscle studies with posted results, 10 adults over 65 on semaglutide plus lifestyle counseling lost 1.73 kg of lean mass in 20 weeks (95% CI 0.40 to 3.06 kg). Ten on counseling alone lost 0.42 kg.[26]

The trials that showed a drug could save lean mass mostly didn’t test whether people got stronger. On ClinicalTrials.gov, none of BELIEVE, QUALITY, EMBRAZE or COURAGE lists a strength or physical-function outcome. QUALITY’s stair-climb result was a key secondary endpoint in Veru’s release, but it isn’t on the registry record.[26][6]

What preserves muscle

Resistance training

Every guideline we checked puts strength training first. The 2025 advisory from the American College of Lifestyle Medicine, American Society for Nutrition, Obesity Medicine Association and The Obesity Society says GLP-1s “should be prescribed together with a structured exercise program, aiming for regular strength training at least 3 times weekly plus at least 150 min of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise weekly.”[15] An international expert consensus says “a high protein intake alone does not increase muscle mass.” It advises moderate-to-high-intensity resistance training, and 2–3 sessions a week alongside aerobic activity.[16] That consensus was funded by Nestlé Health Science, which sells protein products. It also says its statements rest mainly on indirect evidence.[16]

The direct evidence on these drugs is thin. We found 15 registered trials adding resistance or structured exercise to a GLP-1 drug. 11 of them measure strength or function, and none has posted results.[26] We covered the cardio side, including the liraglutide-plus-exercise trial, in our cardio teardown.

Protein

No trial in GLP-1 users has set a protein target. The numbers come from general weight-loss and aging research:

  • U.S. advisory (2025): the general RDA is 0.8 g per kg of body weight a day. “Higher targets, such as 1.2–1.6 g/kg/d, have also been proposed during active weight reduction.” For people with obesity, it says “it is unclear” whether to base the math on actual, adjusted or ideal weight, or fat-free mass, and that actual weight “can significantly overestimate protein requirements.” An absolute 80–120 g a day is an alternative. Avoid long-term intake at or above 2 g/kg.[15]
  • Expert consensus (2025, Nestlé-funded): 1.2–1.5 g/kg of actual body weight a day while losing weight, an expert-opinion statement, and at least 0.8 g/kg during maintenance.[16]
  • Older adults (PROT-AGE, 2013): at least 1.0–1.2 g/kg a day over age 65, at least 1.2 g/kg if active, and 1.2–1.5 g/kg with acute or chronic illness. People with severe kidney disease (eGFR under 30, not on dialysis) may need less.[17]

Ten registered trials are testing protein or nutrition products on GLP-1 drugs, including whey, yeast protein, a leucine blend, HMB and a high-protein diabetes formula. Two are industry-sponsored. None has results.[26]

Creatine

Creatine has the best supplement evidence for muscle in older adults, but only when combined with lifting. A 2017 meta-analysis pooled 22 randomized trials with 721 people aged 57 to 70 on average, training 2–3 days a week. It found 1.37 kg more lean tissue with creatine than placebo, plus small gains in chest press and leg press strength.[18] An earlier meta-analysis also found better 30-second chair-stand scores, but no difference in knee-extension strength.[19] A 2026 meta-analysis in postmenopausal women found a smaller gain of 0.37 kg of lean mass and 7.5 kg on the leg press. The benefit showed up at 5 g a day or more with resistance training, and trials using 3 g a day or less without training found nothing.[21]

The September Texas A&M study gave 10 g a day to 64 adults aged 45 to 65. Those who didn’t exercise gained 1.1 kg of lean tissue (95% CI 0.2–2.0) in 12 weeks. The groups were small (13–20 people), participants chose whether to exercise, and the senior author chairs the scientific advisory board of Alzchem, which makes creatine and supplied it.[22] Two cautions apply here. Creatine pulls water into muscle: early loading studies saw about 0.5–1.0 L of fluid retention, roughly matching the weight gained. DXA counts that water as lean mass.[24] Several of the meta-analysis authors also disclose creatine-industry ties.[20][21]

On GLP-1 drugs specifically, there’s no result. The only registered trial is NCT07625202 at the University of Saskatchewan. It gives 40 people starting a GLP-1 drug either 10 g a day of creatine or placebo during 12 weeks of resistance training. Its primary outcome is chair-stand performance, it is still recruiting, and its primary completion date is May 2027.[26] Neither the U.S. advisory nor the expert consensus mentions creatine.[15][16]

The new muscle drugs

Four drugmakers have reported results from adding a muscle drug to semaglutide or tirzepatide. All four trials were company-funded and phase 2, and none of the drugs is approved.[1][4][5][6]

  • Trevogrumab (Regeneron, blocks myostatin, weekly injection). In COURAGE’s lower-dose part, lean mass fell 7.3% over a year on semaglutide alone, versus 5.8% and 4.2% with trevogrumab. Weight loss didn’t meaningfully change. Adverse events were similar, 77% versus 82% on placebo.[1] After people stopped semaglutide in the higher-dose part, lean mass returned to baseline in the trevogrumab arms.[2]
  • Trevogrumab plus garetosmab (blocks activin A), the “triplet.” It saved the most lean mass, but at the June 2025 interim, 28.3% stopped for side effects (versus 4.6% on semaglutide alone), and 6.7% had a serious adverse event (versus 0.7%). There were two deaths, one of undetermined cause and one cardiac arrest in a person with heart disease. Regeneron said it found no causal link.[3] An echocardiography abstract found no harmful heart-muscle changes at 26 weeks.[2]
  • Bimagrumab (Eli Lilly, blocks activin type II receptors, IV every 12 weeks). In BELIEVE (507 people, 48 weeks), adding it to semaglutide 2.4 mg raised weight loss to 17.8 kg from 14.2 kg, and the fat loss index rose to 92.3% from 71.1%. Muscle spasms, diarrhea and acne were common. Between 14.0% and 21.4% stopped for adverse events on bimagrumab alone, and up to 12.5% in the combination groups.[4]
  • Apitegromab (Scholar Rock, blocks myostatin activation). In EMBRAZE (102 people, 24 weeks on tirzepatide), lean loss was 1.6 kg versus 3.5 kg, with similar weight loss. Side effects were similar to placebo, and function tests showed no notable difference.[5]
  • Enobosarm (Veru, a selective androgen receptor modulator also called ostarine, daily pill). In QUALITY (168 people over 60, 16 weeks), the median lean share of weight lost was 9.4% versus 31.9%, with less stair-climb decline. Liver enzyme (ALT) rises occurred in 11% at 6 mg versus none on placebo. Results so far come from company press releases.[6][7]

The registry shows 13 muscle-drug add-on studies. 10 are industry-run and only 1 (BELIEVE) has posted results. Just 3 register any strength or function test.[26]

What we counted on ClinicalTrials.gov

We pulled 2,751 studies that list semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, orforglipron or liraglutide, or pair GLP-1 drug names with muscle, protein, creatine, muscle-drug or physical-function terms. Then we read the 605 keyword hits by hand. We kept 70 that make muscle, lean mass, strength or function an aim or primary outcome, or that add something to the GLP-1 drug and measure muscle.[26]

  • 3 of 70 have posted results. They are BELIEVE and two pilots in people over 65 that enrolled 16 and 20 people. 54 are recruiting or haven’t started.[26]
  • 38 register a strength or function test, but only 16 make it a primary outcome. Among the 13 muscle-drug studies, it is 3.[26]
  • 10 enroll only people 60 or older, the group with the most to lose. That includes 6 of the 45 add-on trials.[26]
  • Creatine: 1 trial, the 40-person Saskatchewan study.[26]
  • Retatrutide and orforglipron: only one kept study names either drug, a Massachusetts General Hospital smartwatch-guided exercise trial open to people on several GLP-1 drugs.[26]

Every record, the excluded ones and our reasons are in the source file. These counts are a lower bound, since a study that doesn’t use these words in its record would be missed.[26]

What the evidence supports

  • Expect some lean loss. About a quarter to a third of weight lost on these drugs is DXA lean mass, similar to other ways of losing a lot of weight.[4][15]
  • Strength-train. The U.S. advisory says at least 3 times a week, plus 150 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise. The expert consensus says 2–3 resistance sessions. Neither target has been tested in a GLP-1 trial yet.[15][16][26]
  • Get enough protein. Guideline ranges run from 0.8 g/kg up to the 1.2–1.6 g/kg proposed during active weight loss, and at least 1.0–1.2 g/kg over 65. Ask about your kidneys first if you have kidney disease.[15][16][17]
  • Creatine is plausible, not proven here. It helps older adults who lift. On GLP-1 drugs the first trial isn’t done, and no GLP-1 guidance we checked recommends it.[18][26][15][16]
  • Measure function, not just weight. The U.S. advisory lists checking muscle strength, function and body composition before starting. Chair stands, grip and stair climbs are what the trials use.[15][26]
  • Muscle drugs: wait. None is approved, the strength benefit is untested, and one combination had serious safety problems.[1][3][5]

Strengths and limits

Strengths. The lean-share figures come from randomized, placebo-controlled groups using the same DXA method, and the effect of adding a muscle drug points the same way across four companies and three drug classes.[4][5][2][6] MRI studies measure muscle directly and include a check against population data.[12][11]

Limits. DXA lean mass is a stand-in for muscle, and creatine’s water effect makes it worse.[10][24] The MRI substudies were small or post hoc, and STEP UP had only 6 people on placebo.[11][12] Every muscle-drug trial was funded by the drug’s maker. COURAGE isn’t published yet, and QUALITY exists only as press releases.[1][6] The protein and exercise guidance is mostly expert opinion and indirect evidence.[16] And the people most at risk, older adults with low muscle, are a minority of trial participants.[26]

Bottom line

Muscle loss on Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound or Mounjaro is real but mostly proportional. About a quarter to a third of the weight lost is lean mass, like any big weight loss. MRI suggests the muscle that remains carries less fat.[4][12] For a middle-aged person with plenty of muscle, the trial data on strength and function are mostly reassuring. For someone over 65 or already frail, the signals on stair-climb power and fractures are reasons to plan for muscle from the start.[11][13][6][14] What the guidelines support is resistance training and adequate protein.[15][16] Creatine is a reasonable thing to ask your clinician about if you lift, but its first GLP-1 trial won’t finish until 2027.[18][26] The new muscle drugs look like they save lean mass on a scan. Nobody has shown they make people stronger, and none is approved.[5][26]

The trials that will settle this track the same things you can: dose, weeks on the drug, and how many chair stands or how much grip you have now versus when you started. Jabbit keeps a private, dated log of your shots on your iPhone so you can line those up.

Frequently asked questions

How much muscle do you lose on Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound or Mounjaro?
In trials, roughly a quarter to a third of the weight lost on these drugs was lean mass measured by a DXA scan: 28.9% to 34% in the semaglutide or tirzepatide control groups of four recent trials. That is in the same 25–40% range seen with diet and bariatric surgery. Lean mass is not the same as muscle. It also counts water, organs and bone. MRI studies that measure thigh muscle directly found smaller losses that were about what you’d expect for the amount of weight lost, and the fat inside the muscle went down. [2][4][5][6][10][11][12]
Does the muscle loss from GLP-1 drugs actually matter?
It depends on how much muscle you start with. In the trials that tested it, strength and function mostly held up or improved: chair-stand counts rose in a small STEP UP substudy (drug and placebo groups pooled), and walking distance rose about 20 meters in the STEP-HFpEF heart-failure trial. The worry is older people. In one company-reported trial of people over 60, 42.6% of those on semaglutide alone lost at least 10% of their stair-climb power in 16 weeks. The Wegovy label also reports more hip and pelvis fractures in people 75 and older. Neither result shows that muscle loss was the cause. [6][11][13][14]
How much protein should I eat on a GLP-1?
Guidance varies, and none of it comes from trials in GLP-1 users. A 2025 advisory from four U.S. nutrition and obesity societies cites the general RDA of 0.8 g per kg of body weight a day. It says targets of 1.2–1.6 g/kg have been proposed during active weight loss, that using actual body weight in people with obesity can overestimate needs, and that an absolute 80–120 g a day is an alternative. It also says to avoid long-term intake at or above 2 g/kg. A Nestlé-funded expert consensus suggests 1.2–1.5 g/kg of actual body weight while losing weight. For people over 65, the PROT-AGE group recommends at least 1.0–1.2 g/kg. People with kidney disease need their own advice. [15][16][17]
Does creatine help preserve muscle on Ozempic or Zepbound?
Nobody knows yet. In older adults who lift weights, creatine adds about 1.4 kg of lean mass and modest strength gains on average, according to a 2017 meta-analysis of 22 trials. DXA counts the extra water creatine pulls into muscle as lean mass, and much of this research comes from creatine-industry-linked scientists. The only trial of creatine in people starting a GLP-1 drug is a 40-person University of Saskatchewan study that is still recruiting, with a primary completion date of May 2027. Neither GLP-1 nutrition guidance document we checked mentions creatine. [15][16][18][20][21][24][26]
What are trevogrumab, bimagrumab, apitegromab and enobosarm, and can I get them?
They are experimental drugs meant to block muscle-loss signals (myostatin or activin) or, for enobosarm, to act on the androgen (testosterone) receptor. In company-funded phase 2 trials with semaglutide or tirzepatide, each cut the share of weight lost as lean mass, roughly from 30% to between 8% and 18%. None is approved. One combination, trevogrumab plus garetosmab, had 28.3% of people stop for side effects and two deaths in an interim readout, and Regeneron said it found no causal link to the drugs. [2][3][4][5][6]
Does lifting weights on a GLP-1 prevent muscle loss?
It is what every guideline recommends, but there’s no published randomized trial testing it on these drugs. The U.S. advisory says GLP-1s should come with strength training at least three times a week plus 150 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise. We found 15 registered trials adding resistance or structured exercise to a GLP-1 drug. None has posted results yet. [15][26]
