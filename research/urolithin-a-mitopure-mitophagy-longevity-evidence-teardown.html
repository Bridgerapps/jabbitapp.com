Study teardown · Urolithin A (Mitopure; sold under the Timeline brand per its maker’s disclosure) · FDA’s GRAS letter, EU, UK and Swiss novel-food records, the animal lifespan studies, every randomized trial of urolithin A taken by mouth that we found, two systematic reviews, registry records and PubMed counts · Sources checked October 25, 2026

Published October 25, 2026 · Updated October 25, 2026

Urolithin A (Mitopure): does the mitochondria longevity supplement work, and is it safe? It extended lifespan in worms and in one mouse study, but no human trial has tested lifespan; the human trials are small and short, most were funded by the ingredient’s maker, several missed their main goals, and the newest independent trial, published October 9, 2026, missed its main goal too. Short-term tolerability looks good.

Urolithin A is a compound your gut bacteria can make from ellagitannins in pomegranates, some berries and nuts, and a lab-made version is sold as a supplement ingredient branded Mitopure.[10][2][13] Does it work? It extended lifespan in worms, and one 2023 mouse study reported longer life in normal aging mice, but no human trial has tested lifespan.[5][6][23] In people, the seven randomized trials in our chart enrolled 10 to 88 people for 4 weeks to 4 months; four were funded by the ingredient’s maker, Amazentis SA, and several missed their main goals while reporting gains in secondary measures such as strength, endurance or immune cells.[12][11][13][15][14][17][16] The newest, an independent trial published October 9, 2026, did not improve blood-vessel function, its main goal.[14] Is it safe? Short trials report it was well tolerated, and the FDA had “no questions” about its maker’s conclusion that it is safe as a food ingredient, but there are no long-term data.[11][14][1][19]

Why this matters now

On October 9, 2026, JAMA Network Open published a University of Oklahoma trial in 50 middle-aged adults with obesity; its authors report no conflicts of interest, and the study was supported by the American Heart Association, among others.[14] Four weeks of urolithin A did not improve blood-vessel function, the main measure; some brain blood-flow responses changed while performance on the tasks did not.[14] A 2026 meta-analysis of five trials called the walking-test result “statistically inconclusive” with low certainty.[18] Meanwhile the maker has registered more trials, including a 650-person study listed as finished enrolling with no results posted, and the ingredient is still awaiting a decision in the UK.[21][3]

What urolithin A is

Urolithins are made by gut bacteria from ellagitannins and ellagic acid. In one maker-sponsored study, only 12% of healthy adults had detectable urolithin A in their blood at the start, and about 40% made meaningful amounts after drinking pomegranate juice, which is the main argument for taking it directly.[10] The supplement ingredient is made in a lab: the FDA letter says it “is chemically synthesized,” and the EU application calls it identical in structure to the compound the body makes.[1][2] It is said to work by boosting mitophagy, the recycling of worn-out mitochondria.[5][9] Amazentis SA filed the FDA and EU applications, and a 2026 paper discloses that it “commercializes its products under the brand Timeline.”[1][2][20]

The evidence, rung by rung

  • Animals. Urolithin A extended lifespan in worms in the 2016 paper and in a 2025 study by an independent team.[5][7] A Buck Institute study that mainly tested an Alzheimer’s mouse model also reported that it extended lifespan in normal aging mice; we read the abstract only.[6] Rodents showed better exercise capacity.[5]
  • People: lifespan. We found no human trial that measured whether it extends life.[23]
  • Muscle and walking. In 88 middle-aged adults, it did not improve leg power (the main goal) but strength rose about 12%; in 66 older adults, walking distance rose in both groups with no significant difference, while some muscle-endurance tests improved.[12][11] The Amazentis-funded 2022 JAMA Network Open paper states the funder had a role in the study’s design, analysis and the decision to publish, and that the original primary end points were replaced after the study started.[11] A 2026 meta-analysis of five trials and 236 people rated the walking-test evidence low certainty.[18]
  • Athletes. In 42 elite runners, race time did not improve, though muscle-damage markers fell; in 20 strength athletes, max-lift gains were not significant, while two other muscle measures improved.[15][16]
  • Heart, vessels and brain. A 10-person heart failure trial found no effect on heart scans.[17] The October 2026 obesity trial found no blood-vessel benefit; brain blood-flow responses to tasks changed, but task performance did not.[14]
  • Immune aging and skin. A 4-week maker-funded trial reported a small rise in “naive-like” immune T cells; maker- and L’Oréal-affiliated authors report fewer fine wrinkles around the eyes with a topical form.[13][20]
  • Mitochondria. A 2024 review found it did not change maximal energy (ATP) production or mitochondrial biogenesis in healthy people, though early trials reported changes in mitochondria-related genes and blood markers.[19][9]

The key trials, in one chart

Our analysis: the positive results are mostly secondary measures, such as strength, endurance tests, immune-cell types and blood markers, while the main goals the trials set for themselves were mostly missed.[12][11][14][15][17] The two independent reviews agree the evidence is limited; the 2026 authors call it “not reproducible evidence of efficacy” and ask for “larger, longer” trials.[18][19]

The case for urolithin A, in fairness

The biology is coherent: it triggers mitophagy, and a 2025 worm study by a team reporting no conflicts also found lifespan effects.[5][7] A Buck Institute mouse study, whose authors declare no competing interests, also reported longer life.[6] Strength gained about 12% in one trial and muscle endurance improved in another, and a trial with no reported conflicts found some strength gains in athletes.[12][11][16] Side effects in the trials were mild or moderate, and no more common than on placebo in the 2022 older-adult trial; the FDA did not question the maker’s safety conclusion for food use.[11][19][1] Our analysis: these are reasons to keep testing, which the NIA, NCI and others are doing, not proof that it slows human aging.[21]

Safety

  • Short-term tolerability. The first human trial reported a favorable safety profile; the 2022 older-adult trial found no difference in adverse events vs placebo; the 2024 review found adverse events were mild or moderate and unrelated; the 2026 obesity trial called it well tolerated.[9][11][19][14]
  • Animal toxicology. Rat studies of up to 90 days found no toxic effects or target organs, and lab tests found no genotoxicity; these studies were by maker-affiliated authors.[8]
  • What is unknown. None of the trials in our chart lasted longer than 4 months, and most enrolled healthy adults; one 4-month trial’s participants were all White and mostly women.[11][19] There are no published data we found on long-term use, pregnancy, or people with serious illness beyond the 10-person heart failure trial.[17][23]
  • Not a drug. The FDA letter is “not an affirmation” that it is GRAS, and any health claims on labels are subject to labeling rules; it does not mean the FDA reviewed it as a medicine.[1]

Purity

The immune-aging trial used the maker’s Mitopure, and the FDA letter covers the maker’s lab-synthesized ingredient for food use.[13][1] Our analysis: other products labeled “urolithin A,” or pomegranate extracts sold as urolithin boosters, are different products, and the trial results do not automatically apply to them. We do not name or rate brands or sellers.

Legal status

In the US, urolithin A is sold as a food and supplement ingredient after the FDA had no questions about its maker’s GRAS notice; it is not an approved drug for any condition.[1] In the EU, an application was submitted in 2018, and we did not find an authorisation or EFSA opinion.[2] In the UK, the application is listed as in progress at the risk-assessment stage.[3] Switzerland classified it in 2025 as a novel food that needs authorisation.[4]

What a careful reader should notice

These points are our analysis of the sources cited.

  1. “Extends lifespan” is a worm result, plus one mouse abstract. No human trial has measured lifespan.[5][6][23]
  2. Look for the main goal. Many headlines report secondary findings from trials whose main goals were missed.[12][11][14]
  3. Look at who paid. Four of the seven trials in our chart were maker-funded, as the papers disclose; that does not make the results wrong, but independent replication matters, and the newest independent trial was negative on its main goal.[11][12][13][15][14]
  4. Small and short. The trials enrolled 10 to 88 people for 4 weeks to 4 months.[17][12][14][11]
  5. GRAS is not approval. The FDA’s letter says it is not an affirmation of safety status, and it is not a review of health benefits.[1]

Frequently asked questions

Does urolithin A extend lifespan?
In worms, yes, and one 2023 mouse study reported longer life. No human trial has measured lifespan.[5][6][23]
Is urolithin A (Mitopure) safe?
Trials of up to 4 months report it was well tolerated, with mild or moderate side effects. Long-term safety is unknown.[11][19][14]
Is urolithin A FDA approved?
No. It is not a drug. The FDA had no questions about its maker’s conclusion that it is GRAS for use in certain foods, which is not an approval or an endorsement of health benefits.[1]
Does urolithin A build muscle or strength?
One maker-funded trial reported about 12% more strength but missed its main goal, leg power; a 2026 meta-analysis called the walking-test effect inconclusive.[12][18]
What did the October 2026 JAMA Network Open trial find?
In 50 adults with obesity, 4 weeks of urolithin A did not improve blood-vessel function, the main goal. Brain blood-flow responses changed, but task performance did not.[14]
Can I get urolithin A from pomegranate?
Only if your gut bacteria make it. In one maker-sponsored study, about 40% of people converted pomegranate juice compounds into meaningful amounts.[10]
Who funds urolithin A research?
Amazentis SA funded four of the seven trials in our chart, as the papers disclose, and leads 13 of 38 registry records. The NIA, NCI and university teams also run trials.[11][12][13][15][22][21]

Bottom line

Urolithin A has a plausible mechanism and lifespan results in worms and one mouse study.[5][7][6] In people, the trials are small, short and mostly maker-funded; several missed their main goals while reporting secondary signals, and the newest independent trial was negative on its main goal.[12][11][14][18] It appears well tolerated over a few months, and larger trials are under way.[19][21]
