Home / Research / GLP-1 drugs and bone

Evidence review · Ozempic, Wegovy and Rybelsus (semaglutide), Zepbound and Mounjaro (tirzepatide) · Randomized trials, conference posters, records studies, meta-analyses, 9 US drug labels, ClinicalTrials.gov review · Sources checked October 20, 2026

Published October 20, 2026 · Updated October 21, 2026

Do Ozempic, Wegovy, Zepbound or Mounjaro weaken your bones? Hip bone density drops a little as the weight comes off, fracture data are mixed, and tirzepatide has the newest warning signs and the least testing.

On semaglutide or liraglutide, hip bone density falls a little more than with placebo or exercise, roughly in line with the weight lost. In the one trial that added exercise to the drug, bone density stayed at placebo levels.[10][11][12] Fracture data are mixed. Pooled diabetes trials show no harm or even fewer fractures, but Wegovy’s label reports more hip and pelvis fractures in women and in people 75 and older.[14][15][3] The newest warning signs involve tirzepatide (Zepbound, Mounjaro): a 3.2% hip density drop in six months in a small trial, and more fractures in pooled trials, from a conference abstract.[6][5] We also checked the labels and the trial registry. Of 9 US GLP-1 labels, only Wegovy’s mentions fractures, and none mentions bone density.[4] Of 30 registered studies that measure bone on these drugs, 2 have posted results, none tests tirzepatide against placebo, and Eli Lilly runs none.[21]

Why this matters now

Today, October 20, is World Osteoporosis Day.[1] Over the past week, English posts on X tying GLP-1 drugs to bone ran at 8 to 18 a day. They ranged from claims that the drugs “cause massive bone density loss” to a peptide seller promising better “bone density.”[2] The research is moving too. A 60-study meta-analysis came out in Drugs in September, and Novo Nordisk presented CagriSema bone-marker data at the European diabetes meeting the same month. Records studies on tirzepatide keep landing.[15][19][8][7] Hip fractures are among the most serious injuries of aging. For anyone in their 50s, 60s or 70s on a weekly shot, the practical question is whether they’re trading fat for fragile bones, and what they can do about it.

The short answer

  • Bone density dips at the hip, mostly in proportion to weight loss. On semaglutide, a year cost about 0.02 g/cm² of hip density more than placebo in a trial of people at higher fracture risk.[10]
  • Exercise erased the drop in the one trial that tested it. Liraglutide plus exercise kept hip and spine density at placebo levels despite the biggest weight loss.[11]
  • Fracture data conflict. Diabetes trial pools find no harm or fewer fractures, but Wegovy’s label flags more hip and pelvis fractures in women and in people 75 and older.[14][15][3]
  • Tirzepatide has the newest warning signs and the least testing. The evidence is a small trial, a conference abstract and conflicting records studies. No registered trial compares it with placebo on bone.[6][5][7][8][21]
  • The labels are mostly silent. Only Wegovy’s label mentions fractures. Ozempic’s, Zepbound’s and Mounjaro’s don’t.[4]

What happens to bone density

Bone responds to load. Carry less weight, and the skeleton tends to shed some density, especially at the hip. That happened with diet and exercise alone in the Look AHEAD trial.[18][16] The question is whether GLP-1 drugs cause more loss than the weight loss alone would, and whether it matters.

  • Semaglutide, randomized (Hansen 2024, Denmark). 64 adults with low bone density or a recent fracture, mean age 63, mostly postmenopausal women, took semaglutide 1.0 mg or placebo for a year. The trial’s main question was whether the drug would increase bone formation, and it didn’t (P1NP, p=0.418). Instead, the bone-breakdown marker CTX rose, and total hip density ended 0.020 g/cm² lower than placebo (p=0.001). Spine density was lower too. People lost 6.8 kg more on the drug.[10] The registry record still lists the trial’s status as unknown, with no results posted.[21]
  • Liraglutide vs exercise, randomized (S-LiTE, Denmark). 195 adults aged 18 to 65 lost weight on an 800-calorie diet, then spent a year on placebo, exercise, liraglutide 3.0 mg, or both. Liraglutide alone lowered hip density 0.013 g/cm² more than exercise alone (p=0.03) despite similar weight loss. The combination group lost the most weight, 16.9 kg, yet its hip and spine density was no different from placebo.[11]
  • Older adults, randomized pilot (Dinkla 2025). 20 adults with a mean age of 72.7 took semaglutide 1.0 mg plus counseling or counseling alone for 20 weeks. There was no significant difference in whole-body density or bone markers, though the authors note the semaglutide group had “consistently lower BMD and higher bone turnover.”[13]
  • Real-world scans (Liu 2026, JCEM). 255 semaglutide or tirzepatide users at higher fracture risk, 92% women, mean age 64, were compared with 255 matched nonusers. Over a median 17 months, both groups lost hip density at similar rates. In people without diabetes, users lost more (−1% vs −0.6%, P=.04), and the more weight people lost, the more hip bone they lost.[12]
  • Tirzepatide, randomized (TABFAT, Slovenia). 34 premenopausal women with obesity took tirzepatide or placebo for six months. The tirzepatide group lost a median 13.5% of body weight, 3.2% of total hip density and 4.4% at the femoral neck. Both drops were greater than placebo. Spine density rose 2.1%, bone breakdown (CTX) rose 84%, and bone formation (PINP) fell 16.8%.[6] That is a large drop for six months in women under 50. It is a poster, not a paper, and the trial’s registry record lists brown-fat outcomes, not bone.[6]

The pooled view is calmer. A 2026 meta-analysis in Drugs of 60 studies and over 1.25 million people found no significant effect on bone density or fractures in its most-adjusted models. It rated the certainty low.[15] A 2025 review in Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism called GLP-1 effects on bone markers and density “neutral or negative” but not clinically significant, and noted that bone data on dual and triple agonists are mostly from animals.[16]

Fractures: what the trials and labels say

Bone density is a stand-in. Fractures are what matter, and no trial of these drugs was built to count them.

The reassuring data. A 2025 meta-analysis of 44 randomized diabetes trials (47,823 people, 292 fractures) found fewer fractures on GLP-1 drugs than on placebo or other diabetes drugs (RR 0.77). The benefit came from liraglutide (RR 0.42) and treatment longer than 78 weeks. Other GLP-1 drugs showed no benefit.[14]

The worrying data. The Wegovy label reports that in SELECT, the large heart-outcomes trial, “more fractures of the hip and pelvis were reported on WEGOVY than on placebo in female patients: 1% (24/2,448) vs. 0.2% (5/2,424), and in patients ages 75 years and older: 2.4% (17/703) vs. 0.6% (4/663).” In Wegovy’s liver (MASH) trial, where the median age was 61, fractures occurred in 4.4% on the drug versus 3.3% on placebo.[3] These are subgroup counts, and the label gives no p-values for them. They are also the only fracture warnings on any of the nine GLP-1 labels we checked.[3][4]

The label gap. We read the current prescribing information for Wegovy, Ozempic, Rybelsus, Zepbound, Mounjaro, Saxenda, Victoza, Foundayo and Trulicity. Only Wegovy’s mentions fractures, and none mentions bone density.[4] The SELECT and MASH findings are on Wegovy’s label because those were Wegovy trials. Ozempic is the same molecule at up to 2 mg a week, but its label is silent. Zepbound’s and Mounjaro’s labels say nothing about fractures, even though an ADA abstract counted 37 fractures on tirzepatide versus 13 on control across 11 of its trials.[4][5]

Weight loss itself has a fracture cost in older adults. In Look AHEAD, 5,145 adults aged 45 to 76 with type 2 diabetes were followed for a median of 11.3 years. The lifestyle group lost more weight (6.0% vs 3.5%). Total and hip fracture rates didn’t differ, but frailty fractures were 39% more common in the weight-loss group (HR 1.39, 95% CI 1.02–1.89).[17] Men in the lifestyle group also lost more hip bone over 8 to 16 years.[18] That trial used no drugs, and the GLP-1 drugs produce bigger weight losses.

Tirzepatide: the conflicting evidence

Zepbound and Mounjaro also act on a second hormone receptor (GIP), and researchers have flagged it as a possible extra risk for bone.[6] The human data so far split:

Three of the five point to harm, but they are a small trial, a conference abstract and a records study comparing users with people on other GLP-1 drugs.[6][5][7] The records study that found fewer fractures compared older adults with people on DPP-4 diabetes pills, which don’t cause weight loss. Comparisons like that can mix up drug effects with who gets prescribed what.[8] The abstract’s authors note “there were a few fractures in total,” 50 across all 11 trials.[5] Lilly’s own pooled analysis of people 65 and older found no meaningful difference in falls or fractures.[9] None of the 5 registered tirzepatide bone studies is randomized against placebo, and Lilly sponsors none of them.[21]

What we counted on ClinicalTrials.gov

We pulled 1,712 studies that list semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, orforglipron, liraglutide or cagrilintide, or pair GLP-1 drug names with bone terms, and read the 189 keyword hits by hand. We kept 30 that give one of these drugs and register a bone outcome: bone density, bone markers, bone microstructure or fracture.[21]

  • 2 of 30 have posted results, and both stopped early, with 14 and 5 people. The Danish semaglutide trial was published in 2024 but hasn’t posted results to the registry.[21][10]
  • 1 registers fractures as an outcome. It is a Boehringer Ingelheim claims study of its own diabetes drug, with GLP-1 users as a comparison group.[21]
  • No tirzepatide trial is randomized against placebo on bone. The closest are a tirzepatide-vs-semaglutide trial in Taiwan and a 40-person single-arm study of people 65 and older at Wake Forest.[21]
  • Drugmakers are barely looking. The only Novo Nordisk or Eli Lilly study with a bone outcome is Novo’s 144-woman cagrilintide trial, due in May 2028. It is designed to test whether cagrilintide “can reduce the decline in bone mass associated to weight loss.” None of the 86 registered retatrutide or orforglipron studies measures bone.[21]

CagriSema and the next drugs

Novo’s CagriSema adds cagrilintide, an amylin drug, to semaglutide, and Novo is now testing whether cagrilintide can reduce the bone loss that comes with weight loss.[21] In a Novo-funded post hoc look at the REIMAGINE 2 diabetes trial, bone breakdown marker CTX rose about the same on CagriSema 2.4/2.4 mg (+152.6 pg/mL) as on semaglutide 2.4 mg (+151.1), versus +59.8 on placebo, after 68 weeks and 14.2% vs 10.2% weight loss. The authors called it “preliminary reassurance,” but the trial measured no bone density.[19] For a full look at the drug, see our CagriSema teardown.

What the evidence supports

  • Expect a small hip density dip. It mostly tracks how much weight you lose.[10][12][6]
  • Exercise is the one tested protection. In S-LiTE, a GLP-1 drug plus exercise kept bone density at placebo levels. That was liraglutide in people under 65.[11]
  • Know your risk group. The fracture signals show up in women, people 75 and older and older adults losing weight. Spain’s menopause society says GLP-1 treatment in menopause should account for fracture risk, resistance exercise and protein.[3][17][20]
  • Ask about bone if you’re at risk. If you’re older, postmenopausal, or have had a fracture or osteoporosis, ask your clinician whether a DXA scan or other fracture-risk check makes sense before or during treatment. None of the sources we checked sets a GLP-1-specific scanning schedule.[20][16]
  • Tirzepatide: watch the data. The signals are early, mixed and mostly unpublished, and the trials that could settle them won’t report until 2027 or later.[6][5][8][21]

Strengths and limits

Strengths. The semaglutide and liraglutide density findings come from randomized, placebo-controlled trials using DXA.[10][11] The fracture meta-analyses pool tens of thousands of trial participants.[14][15] The label and registry checks are direct reads of primary records.[4][21]

Limits. No trial of these drugs was built to count fractures, so the fracture numbers are small subgroups or pooled side-effect reports.[3][5] The tirzepatide bone data are a poster, an abstract and records studies, which can’t rule out confounding.[6][5][7][8] TABFAT’s rising spine density alongside falling hip density shows that one site can’t stand in for the whole skeleton.[6] Most participants were middle-aged.[10][11][12] The people with the most to lose, frail adults over 75, are few in these studies.[3][13]

Bottom line

For most people, GLP-1 drugs don’t look like bone killers. Hip density slips a little with the weight, pooled fracture data show no clear harm, and exercise blunted the loss in the one trial that tested it.[10][15][14][11] The concern is concentrated where it is for any big weight loss: older adults, postmenopausal women and people who already have weak bones. That is where Wegovy’s label and Look AHEAD show fracture signals.[3][17] Tirzepatide is the open question. The signals are early and conflicting, and nobody, including Lilly, has registered a placebo-controlled bone trial to settle it.[6][5][8][21]

Frequently asked questions

Do Ozempic and Wegovy cause bone loss?
Some, mostly at the hip, and mostly in step with the weight lost. In a 64-person Danish trial in adults at higher fracture risk, a year of semaglutide 1.0 mg (the Ozempic dose range) left total hip bone density 0.020 g/cm² lower than placebo, and a bone-breakdown marker rose. Spine density was lower too. Bone formation didn’t change. A 20-person pilot in adults averaging 73 found no significant change, though the numbers leaned the same way. A 2026 meta-analysis of 60 studies found no significant effect on bone density or fractures overall, with low certainty. [10][13][15]
Do Zepbound and Mounjaro (tirzepatide) cause bone loss or fractures?
The data are thin and point in different directions. In TABFAT, a 34-woman Slovenian trial presented as a poster, six months of tirzepatide cut total hip bone density 3.2% and femoral neck density 4.4%, more than placebo. A conference abstract pooling 11 tirzepatide trials counted 37 fractures among 6,357 people on the drug versus 13 among 3,883 controls (odds ratio 1.84). One large records study linked tirzepatide to more osteoporosis or fragility fractures than other GLP-1 drugs (HR 1.44). Another found fewer femoral fractures than with DPP-4 diabetes pills in people over 65. Lilly’s own analysis of older adults found no meaningful difference in fractures. No registered trial compares tirzepatide with placebo on bone. [5][6][7][8][9][21]
Do GLP-1 drugs raise your fracture risk?
Not clearly, and it may depend on who you are. A 2025 meta-analysis of 44 diabetes trials found 23% fewer fractures on GLP-1 drugs, driven by liraglutide and longer treatment. The Wegovy label, though, reports more hip and pelvis fractures on the drug in women (1% vs 0.2%) and in people 75 and older (2.4% vs 0.6%) in its heart-outcomes trial. None of these trials was designed to count fractures. Among the drug labels we checked, Wegovy’s is the only one that mentions fractures. [3][4][14]
Who should think most about bone on a GLP-1?
The groups where the signals show up: people 75 and older, postmenopausal women, people who already have osteoporosis or a past fracture, and anyone losing a lot of weight fast. In the Look AHEAD diet-and-exercise trial, intentional weight loss in older adults with diabetes didn’t raise total or hip fractures but raised frailty fractures by 39%. Spain’s menopause society says GLP-1 treatment in menopause should take fracture risk into account. Whether you need a bone density (DXA) scan is a question for your clinician. [3][17][20]
Can exercise protect your bones on a GLP-1?
In the one randomized trial that tested it, yes. In S-LiTE, adults who took liraglutide 3.0 mg and exercised for a year lost 16.9 kg, the most of any group, and their hip and spine bone density was no different from placebo. Liraglutide alone lowered hip and spine density more than exercise alone, though both groups lost similar weight. The trial used liraglutide, not semaglutide or tirzepatide, and enrolled people 18 to 65. [11]
What about CagriSema, retatrutide and orforglipron (Foundayo)?
There’s almost nothing on bone yet. Novo’s CagriSema poster from September found bone breakdown markers rose about as much on CagriSema as on semaglutide, but measured no bone density. Novo is running a 144-woman trial of cagrilintide and bone density that isn’t due until 2028. We found no registered retatrutide or orforglipron study with any bone outcome, out of 86. [19][21]
