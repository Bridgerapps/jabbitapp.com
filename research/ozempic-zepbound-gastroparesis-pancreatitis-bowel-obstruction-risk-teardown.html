Do Ozempic, Wegovy and Zepbound cause gastroparesis, pancreatitis or bowel obstruction?

This week a GLP-1 safety story trended on X, and widely shared posts repeated the same numbers: roughly 9 times the pancreatitis risk, 4 times the bowel obstruction risk and 3.6 times the gastroparesis risk, drawn from 16 million records. Those figures come from a 2023 JAMA research letter, and the 9x pancreatitis figure rests on 2 cases in the semaglutide group and 1 case in the comparison group.[1] Our plain answer: these drugs do slow the stomach, and some newer large studies link them to more gastroparesis diagnoses. But the extra risk in absolute terms looks like about 1% or less a year, and randomized trials have not shown a clear rise in pancreatitis or bowel obstruction.[11][4][5][7] The best-supported serious digestive risk is gallbladder disease.[2][12]

Why this matters now

New research keeps arriving. In October 2026 a review in the Journal of Internal Medicine concluded that, for acute pancreatitis and intestinal obstruction, “evidence regarding causality remains uncertain.”[2] A five-year records study of 78,277 GLP-1 users was published the same month, and a Chinese study posted as a preprint (not yet peer-reviewed) reported a doubled gastroparesis risk.[3][13] Meanwhile, the federal court panel that tracks mass lawsuits listed 4,025 pending cases in the combined GLP-1 product-liability litigation as of October 1, 2026.[17] Those are allegations, not findings, and we come back to them below.

Where the “9x pancreatitis” number comes from

The study used a random sample of 16 million patients from a US insurance-claims database, covering 2006 to 2020. But it compared only 613 semaglutide users and 4,144 liraglutide users with 654 people taking bupropion-naltrexone, another weight-loss drug.[1] With groups that small, each case moves the result a lot:

  • Pancreatitis: 2 cases on semaglutide and 71 on liraglutide, against 1 case on bupropion-naltrexone. The adjusted hazard ratio was 9.09, but the 95% confidence interval ran from 1.25 to 66.[1]
  • Bowel obstruction: 0 cases on semaglutide. The 4.22 ratio came from liraglutide (73 cases) against 2 comparator cases (interval 1.02 to 17.4).[1]
  • Gastroparesis: 4 cases on semaglutide and 66 on liraglutide, against 3 comparator cases. The ratio was 3.67 (1.15 to 11.9).[1]

The groups also differed: 82.4% of bupropion-naltrexone users were men, compared with 55.8% of semaglutide users and 61.0% of liraglutide users. The authors also wrote that whether the GLP-1 drugs “were all used for weight loss is uncertain.”[1] In our analysis, this was a reasonable early safety signal, which is what a research letter is for. It was never a precise estimate of how much more likely you are to get pancreatitis. Viral posts that quote “9x” without the case counts or the interval leave out the most important context.

What randomized trials show

Randomized trials are the best test of cause, because they compare similar people assigned at random. A 2025 meta-analysis of 55 placebo-controlled trials with 106,395 participants found more gallstones (2 more cases per 1,000 people) and more reflux (4 more per 1,000). It concluded the drugs “probably have little or no effect” on other GI or biliary events, a list that included pancreatitis, intestinal obstruction, ileus and gastroparesis.[7] A 2026 network meta-analysis of 50 trials found no GLP-1 drug that significantly raised obstruction risk.[10]

The FDA labels give the pancreatitis counts from the makers’ own trials. The absolute rates are low in both the drug and comparison groups:[14][15][16]

Trials also catch severe GI side effects in general, mostly severe nausea, vomiting or diarrhea. The Wegovy label reports 4.1% compared with 0.9% on placebo. For Zepbound the figures are 1.7% to 3.1% by dose arm compared with 1%, and for Ozempic 0.4% to 0.8% compared with 0%.[14][16][15] The catch is that trials last one to two years and enroll selected patients, so rare problems may not show up.

What the newer real-world studies show

Large records studies can pick up rare events that trials miss, but they cannot rule out other explanations. Their results point in different directions:

  • A 2026 Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism study compared 313,342 matched pairs of GLP-1 users with users of SGLT-2 inhibitors, another diabetes drug class. A combined outcome of severe constipation, gastroparesis or obstruction occurred in 1.02 compared with 0.75 per 100 person-years (HR 1.37). In Jabbit’s arithmetic, that difference of 0.27 is about 1 extra event per 370 person-years of treatment.[5]
  • A 2026 Digestive Diseases and Sciences study (164,987 people per group) linked GLP-1s to more gastroparesis (HR 1.57) and more coded drug-induced pancreatitis (HR 2.85). But bowel obstruction was lower (HR 0.86), as were ER visits and hospital admissions.[4]
  • The Shanghai preprint (2,656 GLP-1 users compared with 26,560 DPP-4 inhibitor users) found gastroparesis HR 1.99, with no significant change in pancreatitis (1.31) or obstruction (1.02). It has not been peer-reviewed.[13]
  • Meta-analyses: pooled pancreatitis OR 1.29 (0.91 to 1.84, not significant).[8] A 2026 meta-analysis of obstruction found a non-significant OR of 1.95 with very high heterogeneity (I² 94%).[9]

Gastroparesis: the most consistent signal, with a caveat

Slower stomach emptying is part of how these drugs work. A 2024 meta-analysis found an average delay of 36 minutes on scintigraphy, the gold-standard scan.[11] So it is not surprising that records studies find more gastroparesis diagnoses.[4][5][13] The caveat: in a 2026 study of 78,277 GLP-1 users, 696 people with symptoms were sent for a gastric emptying scan, and only 34.6% of them actually had delayed emptying.[3] In our analysis, this means a “gastroparesis” code in a database can include people with nausea or fullness whose stomach empties normally. Some studies may count symptoms as the disease.

All three labels say the drug is “not recommended in patients with severe gastroparesis.”[14][15][16] Slower emptying is also why the Wegovy label notes “rare postmarketing reports of pulmonary aspiration” during surgery and sedation. If you have a procedure planned, tell the care team you take a GLP-1.[14]

Bowel obstruction: conflicting evidence

The labels list “ileus, intestinal obstruction, severe constipation including fecal impaction” under postmarketing reports. For those reports the labels say it is “not always possible to reliably estimate their frequency or establish a causal relationship to drug exposure.”[14][15][16] The study evidence conflicts. One 2026 meta-analysis found liraglutide linked to more obstruction (OR 3.0), but a network meta-analysis of randomized trials found it linked to less (OR 0.44).[9][10] The largest new cohort found obstruction slightly lower on GLP-1s.[4] Other medicines matter too. In people with diabetes on a GLP-1 who started an opioid painkiller, the 30-day risk of a motility-related GI event was 0.51% with oxycodone, compared with 0.33% with tramadol.[6]

The risk with the strongest evidence: gallbladder

Experts disagree about pancreatitis and obstruction, but gallbladder disease has stronger evidence. A 2022 meta-analysis of 76 trials found a 37% higher relative risk of gallbladder or biliary disease, more than doubled (RR 2.29) in weight-loss trials.[12] The October 2026 review called it the outcome with “the most consistent evidence supporting a causal association.”[2] In the Wegovy weight trials, gallstones were reported in 1.6% compared with 0.7% on placebo.[14]

What about the lawsuits?

The Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation report dated October 1, 2026 lists MDL 3094, “In re: Glucagon-like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonists (GLP-1 RAs) Products Liability Litigation,” in the Eastern District of Pennsylvania with 4,025 actions pending (4,139 filed in total).[17] A lawsuit count tells you how many claims were filed, not whether the claims are right. It is not evidence that any drug caused any person’s injury, and the report says nothing about the merits.

The longevity question

For people thinking long-term, the useful question is not “is there any risk?” but “how big is it compared with what the drug does for me?” The October 2026 review makes that point: because these events are rare, “even substantial relative risk increases generally correspond to small absolute risk increases in broad populations but may become relevant in patients with high baseline risk.”[2] In practice, that means people with prior pancreatitis, gallstones, existing gastroparesis or bowel surgery, or those taking opioids, have the most reason to go over these risks with a prescriber.[2][6][14]

What a careful reader should notice

  • A hazard ratio built on 1 comparator case can be very large and very uncertain at the same time (interval 1.25 to 66).[1]
  • A relative risk is not an absolute risk. The best-measured combined GI-obstruction difference was 0.27 events per 100 person-years.[5]
  • Records studies depend on diagnosis codes. Only about a third of symptomatic users who were tested had delayed emptying.[3]
  • Different comparison drugs (bupropion-naltrexone, SGLT-2 inhibitors, DPP-4 inhibitors or no GLP-1) give different answers.[1][5][13][4]
  • Preprints and lawsuit counts are not settled evidence.[13][17]

FAQ

Does Ozempic cause gastroparesis?
Semaglutide slows stomach emptying, by about 36 minutes on average on scans, and large 2026 records studies link GLP-1 drugs to more gastroparesis diagnoses (HR about 1.6 to 2). The absolute increase appears small, and only about a third of symptomatic users who were tested had truly delayed emptying.[11][4][13][3]
Is it true GLP-1s raise pancreatitis risk 9 times?
That figure comes from a 2023 claims study in which the comparison group had a single pancreatitis case. Its 95% interval ran from 1.25 to 66. Randomized-trial meta-analyses and the Zepbound and Wegovy labels show small or no differences.[1][7][8][16][14]
Can Wegovy or Zepbound cause a bowel obstruction?
The labels list ileus and intestinal obstruction among postmarketing reports, where cause and frequency cannot be reliably established. The studies conflict, and the largest 2026 cohort found obstruction slightly lower on GLP-1s.[14][16][4][10]
How common are serious stomach side effects on GLP-1s?
In label trials, severe GI side effects occurred in 4.1% on Wegovy compared with 0.9% on placebo, and 1.7% to 3.1% on Zepbound compared with 1%. These are mostly severe nausea, vomiting or diarrhea, not pancreatitis or obstruction.[14][16]
Can I take a GLP-1 if I already have gastroparesis?
The Wegovy, Ozempic and Zepbound labels say the drugs are not recommended in severe gastroparesis. This is a decision to make with your prescriber.[14][15][16]
What symptoms should prompt a call to a doctor?
The Wegovy label describes pancreatitis symptoms as persistent or severe abdominal pain, sometimes spreading to the back, with or without nausea or vomiting. Severe or persistent belly pain, repeated vomiting or being unable to pass stool or gas should be checked promptly.[14]

Bottom line

GLP-1 drugs reliably cause stomach symptoms, slow stomach emptying and raise gallbladder risk.[14][11][12] Newer real-world data suggest a modest rise in gastroparesis diagnoses, though some of those may be symptoms rather than the disease.[4][3] For pancreatitis and bowel obstruction, causation is still uncertain and the absolute risks look small.[2][7] The viral “9x” figure is real, but it comes from very few cases.[1]
