Who is Eli Lilly suing over retatrutide, and what does it mean if you bought “reta” online? Lilly has filed 11 federal lawsuits against online sellers, med spas, a clinic and a compounding pharmacy. Three ended with prejudice, eight are open, no court has ruled on the merits, and no buyer has been sued

Retatrutide is Eli Lilly’s experimental weekly shot for obesity. It is still in trials and, in Lilly’s words, “No medicine containing retatrutide has yet been approved for human use by any regulatory agency in the world.”[3] That has not stopped vials labeled “reta” from being sold online and through clinics. The reader’s question: who is Lilly suing, how are the cases going, and should buyers worry? Our plain answer: Lilly has sued 11 sellers and clinics. Three cases ended with prejudice, eight are open, and no court has decided whether Lilly’s claims are right. The suits target sellers, not people who bought the product. The bigger risk for buyers is not legal but medical: nobody can verify what is in an unapproved vial.[1][17][18]

Why this matters now

On October 26, 2026, Bloomberg Law reported that Lilly “has filed at least 11 federal lawsuits against a range of online vendors and wellness clinics” over copycat retatrutide.[1] In the two weeks before that, Lilly dropped two of the cases with prejudice, a court set a November 30 conference in the case where a seller is fighting back, and a compounding pharmacy’s response came due October 30.[9][10][6][8] Meanwhile, poison center reports involving retatrutide have kept rising through 2026.[19]

What Lilly is claiming

Lilly does not hold an approved retatrutide product to protect, so the suits rest on unfair competition and false advertising claims, not on drug approval itself. The seller and pharmacy cases rely on state unfair-competition and consumer-protection laws. The Striker Pharmacy and American Peptides complaints each plead eight causes of action, under seven states’ statutes and Texas common law.[8][11] The med spa and clinic cases filed in October add federal false advertising claims under the Lanham Act. In them, Lilly alleges the businesses compared their products to approved weight-loss drugs and leaned on Lilly’s own trial data, and asks for court-ordered corrective advertising.[14][15]

The complaints repeat one line: “Retatrutide cannot legally be sold for human use to anyone, anywhere, for any reason.”[8] In the Striker case, Lilly alleges that a Texas compounding pharmacy sold the products to med spas and shipped them directly to consumers.[8] FDA has separately said retatrutide “cannot be used in compounding under federal law.”[17]

The other side: a seller fights back

Only one defendant has filed a full response so far. Legendary Peptides’ motion to dismiss argues that Lilly lacks standing because “Lilly does not sell retatrutide, and on its own allegations it cannot.” It sums up: “That is the whole case: Lilly seeks damages for lost sales due to a product it does not and cannot lawfully sell.”[7] It also argues that Texas law does not allow an unfair-competition claim built on a state drug-law violation, “as two courts have already held in Lilly’s own cases.”[7] The court has not ruled. It gave Lilly 44 days from service of the motion to respond and set a case management conference for November 30.[6]

Jabbit analysis: this standing question is the legal crux. If courts accept it, Lilly’s private suits may do less than its public referrals to regulators. If courts reject it, the October Lanham Act cases against clinics could become the template.

Why three cases ended

Lilly ended the Aesthetic Envy, Texas Peptides and Lone Star Peptide cases by filing notices of voluntary dismissal with prejudice.[4][9][10] Under federal rules, a plaintiff can do that without a court order before the defendant answers. “With prejudice” means the claims cannot be refiled.[16] The notices themselves give no reason and no terms, so the court record does not say whether there was a settlement.[9][10] In Astra, Lilly filed a dismissal notice on September 2 but then asked the court to let it take discovery to identify the seller, and the docket remains open.[5]

What this means if you bought “reta”

  • The lawsuits name sellers, clinics and a pharmacy, not buyers. We found no case against a person for buying or using the product.[20]
  • There is no approved retatrutide anywhere. FDA says it “has not been found safe and effective for any condition.”[17][3]
  • Contents are unverified. Victoria’s health department linked six liver injury cases to products labeled retatrutide and warned that “all such products are at risk.”[18] CBS News found two death reports to FDA. It notes the details are unverified and that it is unclear whether the drug was to blame.[19]
  • Poison center reports are climbing. Exposures logged in 2026 rose from 249 in the first quarter to 862 in the third, per CBS News.[19]

If you have used a product sold as retatrutide and feel unwell, especially with yellowing skin or eyes, dark urine, severe belly pain or vomiting, contact a doctor or poison control (1-800-222-1222 in the U.S.). Tell them exactly what you took. For what the trials of the real drug show, see our retatrutide trial summary.

What a careful reader should notice

  1. “11 lawsuits” is a filing count, not a scorecard. No court has found any defendant liable.[1][20]
  2. The three ended cases were closed by Lilly’s own notices, which state no terms.[4][9][10]
  3. The strongest public argument against the suits comes from a defendant, so it is advocacy, not a ruling.[7]
  4. Lilly’s release has at least one detail that differs from the court record: it lists the Aesthetic Envy case in the Northern District of California, while the filings are in the Eastern District.[3][4]
  5. The safety signals, from Victoria’s liver cases, death reports and poison center counts, are real but uncontrolled. None proves what was in a given vial.[18][19]

FAQ

Who has Eli Lilly sued over retatrutide?
Eleven defendants in federal courts in Texas, California, Louisiana and Arizona. Six were sued on August 12, 2026, including online sellers, a compounding pharmacy and a med spa. Five more were sued on October 8, including a med spa and a weight-loss clinic. The table above lists each by court and case number.
Is it illegal to buy retatrutide online?
Lilly’s lawsuits target sellers, not buyers, and we found no case against a buyer. Retatrutide is not approved anywhere, and FDA says it cannot be used in compounding. Anything sold as retatrutide is an unapproved product of unverified content, whatever the label says.
Did Lilly win any of the lawsuits?
Not on the merits. Lilly dropped three cases with prejudice in September and October 2026, and the notices state no terms. Eight cases are open, and no court has ruled on whether Lilly’s claims are valid.
What happens next in the retatrutide cases?
Striker Pharmacy’s response to Lilly’s complaint is due October 30, 2026. In the Legendary Peptides case, Lilly must respond to a pending motion to dismiss, and a case management conference is set for November 30. In the Striker case, discovery runs to August 10, 2027.
Are “research use only” peptides legal to sell?
Lilly alleges that some sellers use a “research use only” label on products that are really meant for people. That is Lilly’s allegation, and courts have not decided it in these cases. FDA has warned companies for marketing unapproved retatrutide directly to consumers.
Is black-market retatrutide dangerous?
It can be. Victoria, Australia, reported six liver injury cases possibly linked to a contaminant in products labeled retatrutide. CBS News found two unverified death reports to FDA, and U.S. poison center exposures reached 862 in the third quarter of 2026. If you feel unwell after using it, see a doctor.

Bottom line

Lilly’s 11 lawsuits are a legal campaign against sellers and clinics, not a verdict. Three cases are closed on Lilly’s own notices, eight are open, and the main defense argument, that Lilly cannot claim lost sales on a drug it cannot sell, is still before a judge. For anyone who bought “reta,” the court fight matters less than the fact that no regulator has approved the drug and no buyer can verify what is in the vial.
