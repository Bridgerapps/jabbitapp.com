Study teardown · Cerebrolysin (FPF-1070; discussed online as a “nootropic peptide”) · From a 1998 study in healthy men to 2026: the stroke, brain-injury and dementia trials, two Cochrane reviews, a European guideline, the Austrian label, the maker’s US notice, an FDA warning letter, WADA, registry, FAERS and PubMed counts · Sources checked October 25, 2026

Published October 25, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

Cerebrolysin: does the pig-brain “nootropic peptide” work, and is it safe? Its largest stroke trial missed its main goal, a Cochrane review found no effect on death and a possible rise in serious side effects, the positive trials are mostly small, it has barely been tested in healthy people, and it is not approved in the US.

Cerebrolysin is an injectable mixture of small peptides and amino acids made from pig brain protein.[1][22] It is widely used for stroke in Russia, Eastern Europe, China and other parts of Asia, and in Austria it is a prescription drug for dementia and for recovery after stroke or brain injury.[1][22] Its record is weaker than its reputation. The largest stroke trial, 1,070 patients, found no difference on its main goal, and a 2023 Cochrane review found no effect on death and a possible increase in non-fatal serious adverse events.[2][1] Smaller trials, some with authors from the manufacturer, report benefits.[3][4][14][10] We found one study in healthy people, from 1998, and it measured brain waves, not everyday thinking.[21] Its maker says it is not approved for sale in the United States.[23]

Why this matters now

New trials keep arriving: a 2025 aphasia trial, a 2025 meta-analysis of 14 trials, a 2026 analysis of patients treated with clot removal, and nine studies on ClinicalTrials.gov that are recruiting, about to start or still running.[10][9][12][30] In the US, FDA’s new peptide compounding policy cleared White House review on October 27, 2026, and the version HHS made public on October 28 lists six peptides; Cerebrolysin is not among them.[27] Cerebrolysin is not on FDA’s May 2026 list of nominated compounding ingredients, and in 2020 FDA told a pharmacy it was not eligible for compounding and had not been nominated.[25][24] Our analysis: that leaves it outside the group of peptides the policy debate has focused on. Our FDA peptide compounding status tracker follows the ones that are on FDA’s lists.

What cerebrolysin is

The Austrian label describes a “proteolytic peptide fraction from pig brain protein,” in our translation, made of short peptides similar or identical to ones the body makes, which is why, the label says, its levels in the body cannot be measured directly.[22] It is a solution given by injection into a muscle or a vein or by drip, in courses of treatment, and the label does not recommend it for anyone under 18 because there are not enough data.[22] It is not a single molecule like most of the peptides we cover, so “a peptide” here means a mixture of many short peptides rather than one defined molecule.[22][1] EVER Neuro Pharma holds the Austrian authorization, and its product site tells US visitors that Cerebrolysin “is not registered with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and is not approved for sale or distribution in the United States.”[22][23]

The evidence, rung by rung

  • Death after stroke. Across six trials and 1,689 people, the Cochrane review found no effect on death from any cause (risk ratio 0.96), rated moderate-certainty evidence, and none of the trials reported poor functional outcome as Cochrane defines it.[1]
  • Recovery after stroke. The largest trial, CASTA, found no significant difference on its main outcome; a benefit in more severe patients was found only after the fact, and its authors said it needed confirming.[2] A 2017 meta-analysis of six trials and 1,649 patients found no effect on function at day 90.[5] Meta-analyses that pooled early stroke-scale scores report benefits, including one of nine trials and 1,879 patients and a 2025 analysis of 14 trials that found only a non-significant trend on functional independence.[6][9] In 2021, a European neurology guideline recommended cerebrolysin for early rehabilitation after ischaemic stroke.[7]
  • Rehabilitation. In CARS, patients in a rehabilitation program had much better arm function at day 90 on cerebrolysin, but its authors called the trial exploratory and too small to be conclusive.[3] In ESCAS, 132 patients with aphasia who all had speech therapy scored better on a language test with cerebrolysin, and the authors called for larger trials.[10]
  • Brain injury. The two CAPTAIN trials together enrolled 188 people and analyzed 185; the smaller one just missed its main goal in the main analysis, and the pooled result was a small-to-medium effect on a combined score of many scales.[13][14][15] A broader 2023 meta-analysis of ten studies and 8,749 patients, including retrospective ones, found no effect on death or length of hospital stay.[16]
  • Dementia. Trials were short: four weeks in the Korean Alzheimer’s trial, with daily living unchanged.[20] An Alzheimer’s meta-analysis of six trials reported better cognition at 4 weeks, not significantly at 6 months.[19] For vascular dementia, Cochrane rated the cognitive benefit very low-quality evidence and said the data “are not definitive.”[17][18]
  • Healthy people. A 1998 study gave cerebrolysin or placebo to 48 healthy men for 10 days. It reported EEG changes; on short-term memory it found only a trend, and that trend came from the placebo group doing worse.[21] We found no trial testing whether it improves focus, memory or learning in healthy people.[31][30]

The key trials, in one chart

Our analysis: the largest of these trials, with more people than the next seven combined, was the one that found no difference on its main goal.[2] The positive results in the chart come from trials of 53 to 142 people, often measured on combined scores, test scores or early stroke scales rather than death or long-term disability.[20][4][14][10][6] At least two positive stroke trials list authors from the manufacturer.[3][4] Cochrane noted that the manufacturer supported three of the multicentre stroke studies it reviewed.[1] Company support does not make a trial wrong, but it is a reason to want independent replication, which the trial authors themselves called for.[2][3][10]

Safety

  • Serious adverse events. The 2023 Cochrane review found a possible increase in non-fatal serious adverse events (risk ratio 2.39, three trials, 1,335 people, moderate certainty), while it found probably little to no difference in the total number of people with serious adverse events (risk ratio 1.16).[1] A 2021 meta-analysis of 12 trials and 2,202 patients found no significant differences from placebo.[8] Our analysis: the two reviews pooled different trials and outcomes, so the question is open.
  • Seizures. The Austrian label lists status epilepticus as a reason not to use it, warns that seizure frequency may rise in people with epilepsy, and lists isolated grand mal seizures among very rare side effects.[22]
  • Allergy and kidneys. The label lists allergic reactions, very rarely up to a shock-like state, and does not allow use with severe kidney impairment or known hypersensitivity.[22]
  • Pregnancy and children. The label says it should not be used in pregnancy unless the woman’s condition requires it, and it is not recommended under 18.[22]
  • Reports. FAERS holds 80 reports naming cerebrolysin; such reports are unverified and do not show cause.[29]
  • Outside medical supervision. Its maker describes it as a prescription medicine for use under a health professional’s supervision.[23] Our analysis: in the US, where no approved product exists, there is no FDA-reviewed source and no US label.[29][23]

Legal status

No cerebrolysin product appears in Drugs@FDA or the NDC directory, and its maker says it is not approved for sale or distribution in the US.[29][23] In an April 2020 warning letter, FDA said cerebrolysin was not eligible for the compounding exemptions because it has no USP or NF monograph, is not part of an approved drug and is not on the 503A bulks list, and that it had not been nominated for that list.[24] It does not appear on FDA’s May 2026 list of nominated substances or on its compounding safety-risk list.[25][26] FDA’s interim peptide policy, made public by HHS on October 28, does not list Cerebrolysin.[27] WADA’s 2026 Prohibited List does not name cerebrolysin; whether any general category applies is a question for anti-doping authorities, and we make no call on it.[28]

What a careful reader should notice

These points are our analysis of the sources cited.

  1. The biggest trial is the most neutral one. CASTA enrolled more patients than the other trials in our chart combined, and it missed its main goal.[2]
  2. Outcomes matter. Benefits show up most on early stroke scales and combined scores; on death, Cochrane found none, and a 2017 meta-analysis found no day-90 functional effect.[6][1][5]
  3. Experts disagree. A European guideline recommends it for early stroke rehabilitation, while a 2017 meta-analysis said routine use “cannot be supported by the available evidence from RCTs.”[7][5]
  4. “Nootropic” is a leap. Every trial with real outcomes was in sick people, and the one healthy-person study we found measured brain waves.[21]
  5. It is a mixture from animal tissue, given by injection, with no US label, so people outside licensed settings have no US-reviewed information on what they are getting.[22][23]

Frequently asked questions

Does cerebrolysin work?
For stroke, the evidence is mixed: the largest trial found no difference on its main outcome, Cochrane found no effect on death, and smaller trials report better early recovery scores. For brain injury and dementia, the trials are small or short. We found no good evidence that it improves thinking in healthy people.[2][1][6][15][17][21]
Is cerebrolysin safe?
A 2023 Cochrane review found a possible increase in non-fatal serious adverse events after stroke, while another meta-analysis found no difference from placebo. The Austrian label warns about seizures and allergic reactions and rules it out in severe kidney impairment.[1][8][22]
What is cerebrolysin made from?
It is a mixture of small peptides and amino acids made by breaking down pig brain protein, given by injection or drip.[1][22]
Is cerebrolysin FDA approved?
No. Its maker says it is not registered with FDA or approved for sale in the US, and no product appears in FDA’s databases.[23][29]
Can a US pharmacy compound cerebrolysin?
In 2020, FDA said it was not eligible for compounding and had not been nominated for the 503A bulks list, and it is not on FDA’s May 2026 list. FDA’s new interim peptide policy, made public on October 28, does not list it.[24][25][27]
Is cerebrolysin a nootropic?
Some papers call it one, but the only placebo-controlled study in healthy people we found was a 10-day EEG study in 48 men from 1998, which found brain-wave changes and only a trend on memory.[21]
Is cerebrolysin banned in sport?
WADA’s 2026 Prohibited List does not name it. Athletes should ask their anti-doping organization about any unapproved substance.[28]

Bottom line

Cerebrolysin is a real medicine in parts of Europe and Asia, with decades of trials behind it.[1][22] Those trials do not add up to a clear benefit: the largest stroke trial was neutral, Cochrane found no effect on death and flagged a possible rise in serious side effects, and the positive results come mostly from smaller trials.[2][1][3][14] Its use as a brain booster for healthy people rests on almost nothing.[21] In the US it is not approved, and FDA said in 2020 that it cannot be compounded.[23][24]
