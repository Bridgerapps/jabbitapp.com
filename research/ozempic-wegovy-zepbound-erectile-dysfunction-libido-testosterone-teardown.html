Do Ozempic, Wegovy and Zepbound cause erectile dysfunction or low libido?

A GLP-1 safety story trending on X this week summed up posts citing “studies linking them to” higher risks of several problems, including “erectile dysfunction in non-diabetics.”[1] The study behind that line found that young men with obesity who were prescribed semaglutide were 4.5 times as likely to get a new ED diagnosis or ED-pill prescription. In absolute terms that was 1.47% compared with 0.32%.[2] Our plain answer: the evidence is mixed and mostly observational. A large randomized trial of a related drug found slightly less new ED, weight loss itself tends to help erections, and GLP-1 drugs usually raise low testosterone in men with obesity.[4][17][11][12] But a few studies do report more ED or more ED reports, and no trial has yet made sexual function its main endpoint for Wegovy or Zepbound.[2][3][7]

Why this matters now

On October 19, 2026, two authors from Stanford’s urology department published a comment in the International Journal of Impotence Research on a March 2026 perspective on this question. They wrote that the observational signals “should not be viewed as definitive evidence of harm but do warrant further investigation.”[10][9] An FDA adverse-event analysis, a large US records study and a meta-analysis, all published in 2026, also point in different directions.[7][3][11] With more younger, healthier men now taking these drugs, the question is coming up more often.[9]

Where the “4.5 times” figure comes from

The 2024 study used TriNetX, a network of US health records. It matched 3,094 men aged 18 to 50 with obesity and no diabetes who were prescribed semaglutide against 3,094 similar men who were never prescribed it. The average man was 37.8 years old with a BMI of 38.6.[2] The outcome was a new ED diagnosis or a new prescription for an ED pill such as sildenafil. It occurred in 1.47% of the semaglutide group compared with 0.32% of the comparison group (risk ratio 4.5, 95% CI 2.3 to 9.0). A new testosterone-deficiency diagnosis occurred in 1.53% compared with 0.80%.[2]

In Jabbit’s arithmetic, the difference is about 1.2 percentage points, or roughly 1 extra man with the outcome for every 87 prescribed semaglutide. The numbers are small, and the design has limits. Men who see a doctor for a weight-loss prescription may also be more likely to bring up and be treated for other problems. A prescription for an ED pill is not the same as a diagnosis. And the study cannot tell whether men actually took the drug.[2][9] The March 2026 perspective lists “confounding, detection bias, or rare idiosyncratic effects” as possible explanations for signals like this.[9]

What the randomized evidence shows

No randomized trial of Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound or Mounjaro has made erectile function its main outcome. The closest evidence comes from a related GLP-1 drug, dulaglutide (Trulicity):

  • REWIND was a large placebo-controlled heart trial. In an exploratory analysis of 3,725 men with type 2 diabetes, average age 65.5, more than half (56.5%) already had moderate or severe ED at the start. New moderate or severe ED occurred at 21.3 per 100 person-years on dulaglutide compared with 22.0 on placebo (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.85 to 0.99). The analysis was exploratory, and the abstract lists Eli Lilly, which makes dulaglutide, as the funder.[4]
  • A small crossover trial gave healthy men aged 18 to 50 four weeks of dulaglutide or placebo. Sexual desire scores did not change, and hormones and sperm stayed normal. Only 24 men completed it, and four weeks is short.[5]
  • A randomized open-label trial in 25 men with diabetes, obesity and low testosterone compared semaglutide with testosterone therapy for 24 weeks. Both raised testosterone, but erectile-function scores improved significantly only with testosterone.[15]

What the newer real-world studies show

Health-record studies are large, but they cannot fully separate the drug from the kind of person who takes it. Their results point in different directions:[3][6][7][8]

  • eClinicalMedicine, 2026: In about 10,400 men with type 2 diabetes, new ED occurred at 35.2 per 1,000 person-years in GLP-1 starters compared with 28.0 in DPP-4 inhibitor starters (HR 1.26). After the authors calibrated for hidden bias using a “negative control” outcome, the link was no longer statistically significant. They wrote that the findings “may reflect residual or selection bias and do not establish causation.”[3]
  • Journal of Diabetes and its Complications, 2025: In men with type 2 diabetes, tirzepatide was linked to lower ED risk than sitagliptin (RR 0.70), injectable semaglutide (0.67) and dulaglutide (0.55). The authors called for randomized trials to confirm it.[6]
  • FDA adverse-event reports: A 2026 analysis found a reporting signal for semaglutide (ROR 1.53, from 49 reports), but also for the non-GLP-1 diabetes drugs sitagliptin and dapagliflozin, and none for dulaglutide or metformin.[7] A 2025 analysis of all GLP-1s found 182 male sexual-dysfunction cases and a low overall ratio (0.41), with exenatide the most reported drug.[8] Report databases show what people chose to report, not how often something happens.

Testosterone: usually up, not down

Obesity lowers testosterone, and losing weight tends to bring it back up. Studies of GLP-1 drugs mostly show the same pattern.[12][11][13] In 215 men on incretin drugs, average total testosterone rose from 332 to 399 ng/dL with about 5% weight loss, and the share with normal levels went from 67% to 86%. Men who lost the most weight gained the most.[12] A 2026 meta-analysis of 8 studies (375 men) found GLP-1 drugs raised total and free testosterone and improved erectile function.[11] A smaller 2025 meta-analysis found rises in bioavailable testosterone but no significant change in free testosterone.[13] A small 2-month study in men with obesity and low testosterone, which was not randomized, reported larger rises in testosterone and erectile-function scores with tirzepatide plus diet than with diet alone.[14]

Researchers disagree about why. The meta-analysis authors suggest the testosterone effect may be partly independent of weight loss.[11] The October 2026 comment says the benefits are “most plausibly indirect” and that there is “no evidence to support a direct effect of GLP-1 receptor activation” on testosterone in men whose levels are already normal.[10] That matters for men who start with normal testosterone: they should not expect a boost.

Libido and desire: a theory, little data

GLP-1 drugs act on reward pathways in the brain, which is one reason they reduce cravings. A 2026 review proposed that the same effect could lower sexual desire through serotonin signaling. The authors called this a theoretical model and said trials rarely measure desire at all.[18] The October 2026 comment adds that “rapid weight loss and nutritional deficits could influence libido, energy levels, or sexual performance,” and that muscle loss may also play a part.[10] So far, the only placebo-controlled test of desire, in healthy men over four weeks, found no change.[5]

What the labels say

We searched the current US prescribing information for Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound and Mounjaro on October 28, 2026. None mentions erectile dysfunction, sexual dysfunction, libido or impotence.[20] The 2024 study’s abstract says package inserts “include sexual dysfunction as a side effect.” We could not find that in the US labels we checked, though labels in other countries or other versions may differ.[2][20] The Zepbound label reports that in rat studies tirzepatide had no effect on “sperm morphology, mating, fertility, and conception.”[20] A 2026 counseling review notes that labels mostly cover genital safety and animal reproductive data, and it says these drugs “should be selected for metabolic indications, not as treatments for ED or infertility.”[19]

The longevity question

ED is often an early sign of blood-vessel disease, because the penile arteries are small and show damage early. That is why weight loss helps. In a 2004 randomized trial, 17 of 55 men with obesity and ED who lost weight and exercised more (about 31%) reached normal erectile scores, compared with 3 of 55 controls.[16] A meta-analysis of weight-loss trials found an average improvement of about 2 points on the erectile function scale.[17] In our analysis, for most men with obesity, the larger effect of a GLP-1 drug on erections is likely to come through weight, blood sugar and blood vessels, which on average should help rather than hurt.[9][10][4] The open question is whether a minority of men, perhaps younger men without diabetes, have a different experience. The current data cannot answer that.[2][10]

What a careful reader should notice

  • A 4.5 times relative risk on a 0.32% baseline is about a 1-point absolute difference.[2]
  • The best randomized evidence is for dulaglutide in older men with diabetes, not for semaglutide or tirzepatide in younger men.[4]
  • Comparison drugs change the answer: GLP-1 vs no GLP-1, vs DPP-4 inhibitors or between GLP-1s give different results.[2][3][6]
  • Adverse-event report ratios also flagged non-GLP-1 diabetes drugs, which suggests the reports may reflect diabetes itself or reporting habits.[7]
  • Testosterone rises in men with obesity-related low levels. That is not evidence of a boost in men with normal levels.[12][10]

FAQ

Does Ozempic cause erectile dysfunction?
It has not been shown to. One 2024 records study linked semaglutide to more new ED diagnoses or ED-pill prescriptions in men 18 to 50 without diabetes (1.47% compared with 0.32%). Other studies found no increase or a decrease, and a randomized trial of a related drug found slightly fewer new cases.[2][4][6]
Does Zepbound or Mounjaro cause ED?
We found no trial testing this directly. In a 2025 records study of men with type 2 diabetes, tirzepatide was linked to lower ED risk than sitagliptin, injectable semaglutide or dulaglutide. That is an association, not proof.[6]
Do GLP-1 drugs lower testosterone?
In men with obesity, studies mostly show testosterone going up, not down, with larger rises in men who lose more weight. One study found average total testosterone rose from 332 to 399 ng/dL.[12][11][13]
Can Wegovy lower sex drive?
A 2026 review proposed this as a theory through brain reward pathways, and rapid weight loss could affect energy and libido. The only placebo-controlled test, a four-week trial in healthy men, found no change in desire.[18][10][5]
Is erectile dysfunction listed on the Ozempic or Zepbound label?
Not in the US labels we checked on October 28, 2026. We found no mention of erectile dysfunction, sexual dysfunction, libido or impotence in the Wegovy, Ozempic, Zepbound or Mounjaro prescribing information.[20]
What should I do if I notice ED on a GLP-1?
Tell your prescriber. ED can be an early sign of heart or blood-vessel disease and can also be linked to low testosterone, diabetes, mood or other medicines, so it is worth checking out rather than assuming the cause. GLP-1 drugs are not approved as ED treatments.[16][19][10]

Bottom line

The claim that GLP-1 drugs cause erectile dysfunction rests mainly on one 2024 records study with small absolute numbers, plus mixed adverse-event data.[2][7][8] The larger and randomized evidence points the other way on average: less new ED with dulaglutide, rising testosterone in men with obesity, and better erections with weight loss.[4][11][16] Researchers agree on one thing: trials need to measure sexual function directly, especially in younger men.[10][18]
