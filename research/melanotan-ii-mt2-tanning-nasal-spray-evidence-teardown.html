Study teardown · Melanotan II (MT-II, MT2, “Melanotan 2,” tanning nasal sprays and injections) · TGA and FDA documents, 1990s trials, 17 case reports, a lab study, FAERS and registry counts, and the approved relative Scenesse · Sources checked October 24, 2026

Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026

Melanotan II (MT2, the “tanning peptide” sold as nasal sprays and injections): is it safe? It does darken skin, but it has never been approved anywhere, it won’t protect you from the sun, lab tests found products far off their labels, and doctors have published reports of melanoma, priapism, kidney injury and brain swelling in users.

Melanotan II is a lab-made, ring-shaped copy of α-MSH, the body’s hormone that tells skin cells to make melanin. It switches on several melanocortin receptors at once, so it darkens skin and can also cause erections, nausea and loss of appetite.[30][7][8] University of Arizona researchers tested it in small studies in the 1990s, but it was never developed into an approved medicine.[10] FDA has no approved melanotan product, Australia’s TGA lists it as prescription-only with no approved products, and the only approved drug in its family for skin is a different molecule, afamelanotide (Scenesse), for a rare light-sensitivity disease.[40][1][13] The TGA says melanotan “will not protect against UV exposure the way a suitable sunscreen will.”[2]

Why this matters now

On August 17, 2026, the TGA reported that 5 seized melanotan II nasal sprays, all with the same label, held an estimated 22 to 54 mg against a labelled 30 mg, and that it had issued 27 infringement notices totalling A$101,412 for alleged unlawful supply.[1] In the US, FDA says its Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee will discuss melanotan II, with four other peptides, at a meeting before the end of February 2027, as a candidate for the list of substances pharmacies may compound.[5] FDA’s separate peptide compounding policy, under White House review since October 14, 2026, has no public list, so whether it covers melanotan II is unknown.[6] Dermatologists have also documented melanotan promotion on TikTok and other social media, where it is nicknamed the “Barbie drug.”[36] We covered TikTok’s October 2026 peptide search block here.

What the studies actually showed

The case for melanotan II rests on a handful of small studies from one university group, all more than 25 years old:

  • It darkens skin. In the first human study, 2 of 3 men developed more pigment on the face and upper body.[7] That’s the effect sellers advertise.
  • It causes erections. In placebo-controlled crossover studies, erections followed melanotan II in 8 of 10 men with psychogenic erectile dysfunction and 12 of 19 with organic causes.[8][9] That line of research led to PT-141 (bremelanotide), now approved as Vyleesi for low sexual desire in premenopausal women; we cover it here.[10]
  • Side effects showed up right away. Nausea, stretching and yawning, and loss of appetite were more common than on placebo, and 4 of 19 injections in the 2000 study caused severe nausea.[8][9]
  • Nothing since. We found no registered melanotan II trial on ClinicalTrials.gov; the one record matching “melanotan” is an example entry.[38] PubMed holds 280 records mentioning melanotan, many of them case reports and reviews.[39]

These studies were small and short, and none was designed to test long-term safety or skin cancer risk.[7][8][9]

Does a melanotan tan protect you from the sun?

No regulator says it does. The TGA says melanotan “will not protect against UV exposure the way a suitable sunscreen will,” and that “no tan, fake or real, will protect skin against damage from sun exposure.”[2] In 2007, FDA’s drug center director called melanotan II “mislabeled, marketed and sold illegally as a preventative against skin cancer and as a tanning agent.”[3]

The closest evidence comes from melanotan I, a different molecule. In a 2004 study, 3 of 4 people given it tanned and had 47% fewer sunburn cells at one test site.[12] That molecule became afamelanotide, which is approved only to help people with EPP tolerate more light, not for tanning or preventing skin cancer.[13][14] Those results don’t transfer to melanotan II bought online.

What’s actually in the bottle

  • Nasal sprays, 2026. The TGA’s estimate of 22 to 54 mg against a 30 mg label means some bottles held about 73% of the label and others about 180%, a 2.5-fold spread between bottles with identical labels (our arithmetic).[1]
  • Injectable vials, 2015. Vials from three online shops claimed 10 mg and held 4.32 to 8.84 mg, with impurities of 4.1% to 5.9% in products from two shops.[33]
  • Why that matters. FDA says compounded melanotan II may pose an immune-reaction risk because of aggregation or peptide-related impurities.[4] The UK medicines regulator has also warned about impurities and about shared needles spreading blood-borne viruses.[34]

Safety: what doctors have reported

Melanotan II has no large safety trials, so most of what’s known about harm comes from case reports: published accounts of individual users seen by doctors. We read 17 of them.

  • Moles and melanoma. Doctors have reported new or rapidly darkening moles, including within 24 hours of a single injection.[16][17][18][19] Melanoma or melanoma in situ has been reported in users, including a 20-year-old woman who also used sunbeds, a 22-year-old woman who developed melanoma inside her mouth after using a nasal spray, and a patient with five melanomas in situ who also used tanning beds and anabolic hormones.[20][21][22][23][24] A 2017 review said “conclusive evidence linking these phenomena is lacking,” and many users also tan with UV, which itself causes melanoma.[15]
  • Priapism. Painful, lasting erections needing emergency treatment have been reported; one man had not regained erectile function a month later, and another needed surgery.[27][28][29]
  • Kidneys and muscles. One user had sympathomimetic toxicity (racing heart, agitation, sweating), muscle breakdown and kidney injury and spent 3 days in intensive care; the substance was confirmed as melanotan II by lab testing.[25] Swedish doctors reported a kidney infarction they judged “most likely attributed” to melanotan II.[30]
  • Brain swelling. A 2013 report described posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES), a type of brain swelling, in a melanotan user.[26] The TGA and FDA both cite brain swelling among reported harms.[2][4]
  • Other skin and mouth changes. Ulcers at injection sites (pyoderma gangrenosum) and dark gum patches that persisted 3 months after stopping have been reported.[31][32]
  • FDA’s database. FAERS holds 10 reports mentioning melanotan, 9 coded serious and none as death. Five describe a renal artery clot and also list a testosterone product; three describe a kidney infarct in Sweden. Several look like duplicates of the same case, and none proves cause.[37]

The TGA’s list of common effects is headache, nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite and facial redness, and it calls the risk of serious skin cancers “the most concerning.”[2]

The approved relative: afamelanotide (Scenesse)

Afamelanotide, once called melanotan I, is the only approved drug in this family for skin.[15][13] It shows what a properly tested version looks like:

  • Narrow use. FDA approved it in 2019 for adults with erythropoietic protoporphyria (EPP), a rare disease in which light causes severe pain. The EU approved it in 2014 under “exceptional circumstances.”[13][14]
  • Measured benefit. In one trial, patients spent a median 64.1 hours in direct sunlight without pain over 6 months, vs 40.5 hours on a dummy implant.[13]
  • Controlled delivery. It is a small implant placed under the skin by a trained clinician, not a self-injected powder or spray.[13]
  • Skin checks built in. The label warns it may darken moles and freckles and calls for a full-body skin exam twice a year. Darker skin patches occurred in 4% vs 0% and new moles in 4% vs 2%.[13]
  • Still studied. Its maker, Clinuvel, sponsors all 23 afamelanotide records on ClinicalTrials.gov, including an active vitiligo trial.[38]

Is melanotan II legal?

  • United States. No melanotan product is FDA approved.[40] FDA warned a seller in 2007 that claims like tanning and skin cancer protection made it an unapproved new drug.[3] An earlier nomination to let pharmacies compound it was withdrawn, and FDA’s advisory committee is due to discuss it again by February 2027.[4][5]
  • Australia. It is prescription-only with no approved products, and the TGA says supplying it without a prescription or advertising it to the public, including by influencers, is illegal.[1][2] Australian dermatologists have called its unregulated use a public health concern.[35]
  • UK and EU. UK clinicians describe it as unlicensed and say its sale is illegal there and in many other countries.[23]

This is a summary of public sources, not legal advice.

What a careful reader should notice

These points are our analysis of the cited sources.

  1. The tan is real, the safety data aren’t there. Small studies show melanotan II darkens skin.[7] No study has followed users long enough to measure skin cancer risk.[7][8][9][38]
  2. Case reports can’t give a rate. They show what can happen, not how often. Many users also use sunbeds or other drugs, which makes cause hard to pin down.[15][24][37]
  3. Doses printed on labels can’t be trusted. Two independent tests a decade apart found products well below or well above their labels.[1][33]
  4. Melanotan I and II aren’t the same drug. Evidence for afamelanotide, a different molecule given as a regulated implant to EPP patients, doesn’t carry over to melanotan II sprays or vials.[13][10]
  5. A committee discussion isn’t an approval. FDA’s plan to discuss melanotan II for compounding means it is under consideration, nothing more.[5]

Frequently asked questions

Is melanotan II safe?
Its safety has never been tested in large trials. Small 1990s studies found nausea, yawning and loss of appetite, and doctors have since published case reports of changing moles, melanoma, priapism, kidney injury and brain swelling in users. Lab tests also found products far off their labels.[9][15][1][33]
Is melanotan II legal in the US?
No melanotan product is FDA approved. FDA has warned a seller that marketing it for tanning or skin cancer protection made it an unapproved new drug, and an FDA advisory committee is due to discuss it for pharmacy compounding by February 2027.[40][3][5]
Is melanotan legal in Australia?
Melanotan II is prescription-only in Australia, with no approved products. The TGA says supplying it without a prescription or advertising it to the public is illegal, and it issued A$101,412 in infringement notices in 2026 over alleged unlawful supply.[1][2]
Does a melanotan tan protect against sunburn or skin cancer?
No regulator says so. The TGA says it will not protect against UV the way sunscreen does, and FDA called skin cancer prevention claims for melanotan II illegal.[2][3]
What is the difference between melanotan I and melanotan II?
Melanotan I (afamelanotide) is a linear peptide that became Scenesse, an implant approved for the rare disease EPP. Melanotan II is a smaller ring-shaped peptide that also causes erections and nausea, and it has never been approved.[10][13]
Is melanotan II the same as PT-141?
No. PT-141 (bremelanotide) is a close analog of melanotan II that was developed into Vyleesi, approved for low sexual desire in premenopausal women.[10]
Are melanotan nasal sprays safer than injections?
There is no evidence that they are. TGA testing of seized nasal sprays found inconsistent amounts between bottles, and one published case of melanoma inside the mouth followed nasal spray use.[1][23]

Bottom line

Melanotan II does what tanning forums say it does: it darkens skin, along with causing nausea and erections.[7][8] But it has never been approved, it won’t protect against UV, products tested by labs didn’t contain what their labels said, and doctors keep reporting serious harms in users.[40][2][1][33][15] The one tested and approved relative, afamelanotide, is a different molecule given as a supervised implant for a rare disease.[13]
