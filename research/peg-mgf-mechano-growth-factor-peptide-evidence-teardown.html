Home / Research / PEG-MGF teardown

Study teardown · PEG-MGF (pegylated mechano growth factor, sold online as a muscle-recovery peptide) · FDA documents, the MGF lab and animal studies and the challenges to them, doping-lab findings, registry, FAERS and PubMed counts, the patents · Sources checked October 24, 2026

Published October 24, 2026 · Updated October 24, 2026

PEG-MGF (pegylated mechano growth factor): does it build muscle, and is it safe? Nobody has published a study of PEG-MGF itself, the MGF peptide’s lab effects are disputed, FDA says it has no human exposure data, and anti-doping rules have banned MGF since 2005.

PEG-MGF is a synthetic peptide, a short fragment linked to the muscle growth hormone IGF-1, with polyethylene glycol (PEG) attached so it lasts longer in the body.[5][22][23] We found no published study of PEG-MGF itself in animals or people, and no registered human trial.[26][27] FDA says it “has not identified any human exposure data” and lacks the information to know whether it would harm people.[1] The unpegylated MGF peptide it is based on showed muscle-cell effects in early lab work, but researchers at two drug companies could not reproduce them, and a natural MGF peptide has never been found in the body.[6][12][5] The honest answer is that nobody knows whether it builds muscle or whether it is safe.

Why this matters now

FDA says its Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee will discuss PEG-MGF at a meeting before the end of February 2027, together with LL-37, GHK-Cu, dihexa acetate and melanotan II. All five are being considered for the list of substances pharmacies may compound.[2] No date or briefing materials have been posted. FDA’s separate interim peptide compounding policy has been under White House review since October 14, 2026, and because its list of peptides is not public, it is unknown whether it covers PEG-MGF.[3] Meanwhile PEG-MGF is promoted online for muscle growth and recovery. This page sets out what the record actually contains before that meeting. For the wider policy, see our FDA peptide compounding explainer.

What PEG-MGF is

The IGF-1 gene can be spliced in different ways. One version, called IGF-1Ec in humans and IGF-1Eb in rodents, carries a distinct tail region (the E domain), and its message rises in muscle that is stretched or damaged. Its discoverers named it mechano growth factor.[4][8][5] The “MGF” sold as a peptide is not that whole protein. It is a synthetic copy of the last 24 amino acids of the tail, often with changes to make it more stable.[5][19] PEG-MGF adds a polyethylene glycol chain, an approach described in patent applications filed in 2005 that are now listed as ceased or abandoned.[22] A 2010 review in Endocrinology pointed out a basic gap: “no analogous peptide product of the Igf1 gene has been identified in or isolated from cultured cells, their conditioned medium, or in vivo animal tissues or biological fluids.”[5] In other words, the peptide sold as MGF has not been shown to exist naturally in the form that is sold.

PEG-MGF has no approved use anywhere we found, and no FDA-approved product.[28] PubMed has 194 papers on mechano growth factor, but only one record mentions PEG-MGF, and that is the 2026 review saying human studies are missing.[26][23]

The evidence, rung by rung

  • The gene message responds to exercise and injury: in rats, MGF messenger RNA rose quickly after muscle damage and fell within days, and older rats made much less after overload.[8][7] In a human study of 8 young and 7 older adults, one bout of heavy leg exercise raised MGF messenger RNA in the young but not the older participants.[9] In older men, weeks of resistance training raised it, more so when growth hormone was added.[10] But a small randomized crossover trial in 7 young men found no change after either exercise or growth hormone.[11] These studies measured the body’s own gene activity. None gave anyone MGF.[26]
  • Synthetic MGF in muscle cells, contested: a 2002 cell study found the E-domain peptide increased muscle precursor cell growth and delayed their maturation.[6] In 2014, scientists at Novartis and, per the paper, a second pharmaceutical company tested native and stabilized MGF peptides on mouse and human muscle cells and on primary muscle stem cells and found no effect. They wrote: “These results call in to question whether there is a physiological role for MGF.”[12]
  • Synthetic MGF at the IGF-1 receptor: in receptor assays, the short human MGF peptide and a stabilized analog produced no IGF-1 receptor activation. A much larger “full-length MGF” did activate the IGF-1 receptor and insulin receptors.[13] The cell study above had proposed that MGF works through a different receptor, which has not been identified in the work we reviewed (our analysis).[6][13]
  • Heart studies in animals, the most positive data: in sheep after a heart attack, the E-domain peptide preserved heart function, with 35% less compromised heart muscle.[14] In mice, it reduced cell death and preserved pumping function, and a stabilized version delayed heart failure but did not prevent harmful heart enlargement.[15][16] These used the unpegylated peptide in heart attack models, not muscle building, and have not been tested in people.[27]
  • PEG-MGF itself: we found no published study in cells, animals or people, and no registered trial.[26][27] The 2026 review says the pegylated product’s half-life is “not reported” and that effects of the native peptide “cannot be assumed to apply to PEG-conjugated constructs.”[23]

Safety: what is and isn’t known

  • FDA’s view: FDA says compounded PEG-MGF “may pose significant risk for immunogenicity for certain routes of administration” and may have problems with “peptide-related impurities and API characterization.” It says it “has not identified any human exposure data” for any route and lacks information on “whether it would cause harm if administered to humans.”[1]
  • No human safety data at all: with no trials, there is no measured rate of side effects to report. FAERS has no reports naming PEG-MGF or MGF, which reflects how rarely unapproved products are reported, not safety.[27][28]
  • Growth signaling and cancer: the 2026 review lists a “theoretical proliferative concern.”[23] In 83 prostate cancer specimens, higher IGF-1Ec levels were associated with more advanced tumors, and a 2020 review discusses IGF-1 isoforms including MGF in several human cancers.[24][25] These are associations involving the body’s own protein, not evidence that the peptide causes cancer. Whether added MGF could feed existing tumors has not been studied (our analysis).
  • Not knowing what is in the vial: doping laboratories have found an unidentified MGF analogue in two black-market products (later shown to be a chemically modified, amidated form), a “full-length MGF” sold through illicit channels, and a different MGF variant in confiscated vials.[18][19][20] The one MGF bulk listing we found in FDA’s NDC directory codes its active ingredient as mecasermin, which is the name for full-length IGF-1, a different molecule (our analysis).[29] The full-length form can activate insulin receptors in the lab.[13] Buyers cannot easily tell which of these molecules they have.
  • Athletes: the group that described MGF flagged its potential for misuse in doping as early as 2005.[17] MGFs are on the World Anti-Doping Agency’s 2026 Prohibited List under growth factors, banned at all times, and doping labs have built tests to detect them.[21][19][18]

Legal status

No PEG-MGF or MGF product is approved by FDA.[28] FDA lists PEG-MGF among bulk substances whose compounding nominations were withdrawn, with the safety summary quoted above, and its advisory committee is due to discuss it by February 2027.[1][2] Whether the pending interim peptide policy covers PEG-MGF is not public.[3] In sport, MGFs are prohibited at all times.[21]

What a careful reader should notice

These points are our analysis of the cited sources.

  1. “Your muscles make MGF” describes a gene message measured in biopsies. The short peptide sold as MGF has not been isolated from the body.[9][5]
  2. The key muscle-cell result did not hold up when researchers at two drug companies tried to repeat it.[6][12]
  3. The most encouraging data are from heart attack models in sheep and mice, not muscle growth in healthy people.[14][15][16]
  4. Adding PEG changes how a peptide behaves, so even the MGF lab data do not carry over to PEG-MGF, a point the 2026 review makes directly.[23]
  5. Every human study we found measured the body’s own MGF message. We found no study of anyone taking MGF or PEG-MGF.[26][27]
  6. FDA’s committee will be weighing a substance for which FDA says it has found no human exposure data at all.[1][2]

Frequently asked questions

Does PEG-MGF build muscle?
There is no evidence that it does in people. No published study has tested PEG-MGF, and no human trial of MGF or PEG-MGF is registered. The unpegylated MGF peptide showed muscle-cell effects in a 2002 lab study, but researchers at two drug companies could not reproduce them in 2014.[26][27][6][12]
Is PEG-MGF safe?
No one knows. FDA says it has not identified any human exposure data and lacks the information to know whether PEG-MGF would cause harm, and it flags immunogenicity and impurity concerns. There are no trials and no adverse event reports to go on.[1][27][28]
What is the difference between MGF and PEG-MGF?
MGF, as sold, is a synthetic 24-amino-acid copy of the tail region of one IGF-1 variant. PEG-MGF has a polyethylene glycol chain attached to make it last longer. A 2026 review says results from the unpegylated peptide cannot be assumed to apply to the pegylated version.[5][22][23]
Is PEG-MGF FDA approved or legal to compound?
No PEG-MGF product is FDA approved. FDA lists it among withdrawn compounding nominations with safety concerns, and its advisory committee is due to discuss it before the end of February 2027.[28][1][2]
Is MGF banned in sport?
Yes. Mechano growth factors are on the World Anti-Doping Agency’s 2026 Prohibited List and are banned at all times. They have been considered prohibited since 2005, and doping labs can detect several versions.[21][19][18][20]
Can PEG-MGF cause cancer?
There is no evidence either way in people. Higher levels of the body’s own IGF-1Ec have been associated with more advanced prostate cancer, and a 2026 review lists a theoretical proliferative concern. That is an association, not proof of harm.[24][25][23]
Does exercise raise MGF naturally?
In some studies, yes. Heavy exercise raised MGF messenger RNA in young adults and resistance training raised it in older men, but another small trial in young men found no change. These studies measured gene activity, not a circulating peptide.[9][10][11][5]

Bottom line

MGF began as an interesting finding about how stretched and damaged muscle switches on the IGF-1 gene, and its tail peptide has shown benefit in animal heart attack models.[4][14][15] But the muscle-cell effects behind the bodybuilding claims were not reproduced, the peptide has never been found in the body in the form that is sold, and PEG-MGF itself has never been studied.[12][5][26] FDA says it has no human exposure data, black-market versions have turned out to be different molecules, and the substance is banned in sport.[1][18][19][21] Anyone using it would be relying on lab and animal data about a different molecule.
