Published October 26, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026

Petrelintide, eloralintide and cagrilintide: what are amylin weight-loss drugs, and are they better than Wegovy or Zepbound? So far, amylin drugs alone have produced about 9% to 12% weight loss in most trials, and up to 20% in one eloralintide trial. Nausea is still the main side effect. None is approved for weight loss; CagriSema, which pairs cagrilintide with semaglutide, has an FDA decision expected by the end of 2026.

Amylin is a hormone your pancreas releases with insulin after meals. It slows stomach emptying and helps end a meal.[20] Drugmakers are now testing long-acting versions as weight-loss shots: petrelintide (Zealand Pharma and Roche), eloralintide (Eli Lilly) and cagrilintide (Novo Nordisk), which Novo also pairs with semaglutide as CagriSema.[5][7][13] Do they work as well as Wegovy or Zepbound? Not on their own, in most trials so far. Petrelintide and cagrilintide alone produced about 9% to 12% weight loss, while Wegovy produced 14.9% and Zepbound up to 20.9% in their main trials.[1][4][11][12][15][16] The exception is eloralintide, whose best arms reached about 20% in a 263-person phase 2 trial.[7] Are they gentler on the stomach? Nausea is still the most common side effect; petrelintide’s rates look lower than the GLP-1 labels, but no trial has compared them head to head.[1][17][18] None is approved for weight loss. The only approved amylin drug, pramlintide (Symlin), is for diabetes, and its label carries a boxed warning for severe low blood sugar with insulin.[19] CagriSema is under FDA review, and Novo expects a decision in Q4 2026.[13][14]

Why this matters now

A run of amylin results landed in the past month. On September 22, Zealand said it had started its phase 3 ZUPREME program for petrelintide, and on September 29 the full ZUPREME 1 results were published in The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology.[5][1] On September 30, Lilly reported phase 2 results combining eloralintide with tirzepatide, and Novo said it expects an FDA decision on CagriSema in Q4 2026.[10][13] On October 7, Zealand reported topline results of a second phase 2 trial, in people with type 2 diabetes.[4] On October 19, two new phase 3 trials of eloralintide plus tirzepatide appeared on ClinicalTrials.gov.[23] Optum Rx, a pharmacy benefit manager, lists December 18, 2026 as the expected FDA decision date for CagriSema.[14] We covered CagriSema and amycretin, Novo’s single-molecule amylin and GLP-1 drug, in separate teardowns linked below; this page covers the amylin drugs themselves.

What amylin drugs are

Amylin is made in the same pancreatic cells as insulin. It slows gastric emptying, suppresses glucagon and “promotes meal termination,” a 2026 review says.[20] Wegovy and Zepbound work mainly through a different gut hormone, GLP-1 (Zepbound also acts on GIP). That is why companies are testing amylin drugs both alone and paired with GLP-1 drugs.[20][13]

  • Pramlintide (Symlin). The first approved amylin drug. It is approved only as an add-on for people with diabetes who use mealtime insulin, not for weight loss.[19] The same review says it “was limited by modest efficacy and frequent dosing.”[20]
  • Cagrilintide (Novo Nordisk). A long-acting, once-weekly amylin drug. It is tested alone (RENEW trials) and with semaglutide as CagriSema (REDEFINE trials).[24][13]
  • Eloralintide (Eli Lilly). A once-weekly drug that targets amylin receptors selectively. It is tested alone (ENLIGHTEN) and with tirzepatide (AMPLIFY).[7][23]
  • Petrelintide (Zealand Pharma and Roche). A once-weekly amylin drug with a half-life of about 10 days in phase 1.[6] Zealand and Roche are co-developing it under a 2025 agreement.[5]

Where each drug stands

Weight loss: how they compare with Wegovy and Zepbound

  • Petrelintide. In ZUPREME 1 (485 people dosed), weight fell 7.9% to 9.8% at 28 weeks across arms vs 1.7% on placebo, and up to 10.7% by week 42.[1] In ZUPREME-2, in 220 people with type 2 diabetes, Zealand reports up to 9.2% at 28 weeks vs 2.0%; these are topline figures that have not been peer reviewed.[4]
  • Eloralintide. In the 48-week phase 2 trial of 263 people, weight fell 9% to 20% across arms vs 0.4% on placebo.[7] In Lilly’s newer type 2 diabetes trial, eloralintide alone reached up to 12.3% vs 3.0%, by Lilly’s report.[10]
  • Cagrilintide. 6.0% to 10.8% at 26 weeks in phase 2 vs 3.0%. The highest dose beat liraglutide (Saxenda’s drug), 10.8% vs 9.0%.[11] In the 68-week REDEFINE 1 trial, cagrilintide alone produced 11.5%, CagriSema 20.4%, semaglutide alone 14.9% and placebo 3.0%.[12]
  • For scale. Wegovy produced 14.9% vs 2.4% at 68 weeks in STEP 1, and Zepbound 15.0% to 20.9% vs 3.1% at 72 weeks in SURMOUNT-1.[15][16]

Our analysis: the only time an amylin drug alone and semaglutide were tested in the same trial, semaglutide did better (14.9% vs 11.5%), and the combination beat both.[12] Eloralintide’s phase 2 result is the strongest for an amylin drug alone, but it comes from one 48-week trial of 263 people, 78% of them women, using the efficacy estimand. That estimand counts what happens to people who stay on treatment, which usually gives bigger numbers.[7]

Are they gentler on the stomach?

That is the main pitch, and the data are mixed.

Sources: [1][7][11][17][18][19]

  • Petrelintide. The ZUPREME 1 authors report “similar gastrointestinal tolerability to that of placebo apart from predominantly mild nausea during dose escalation.” Vomiting was 3% vs 6% on placebo.[1] Zealand reports that 4.8% in the arm it calls maximally effective stopped for adverse events, vs 4.9% on placebo, and that in ZUPREME-2 1.9% stopped for stomach side effects vs 1.7%.[3][4]
  • Eloralintide. Nausea reached 64% in one arm, and fatigue reached 46% vs 12% on placebo.[7] Lilly says these were “seen more frequently in the higher dose arms.”[9] In Lilly’s type 2 diabetes trial, 0% to 10.8% stopped eloralintide alone for adverse events, vs 16.7% on placebo, by Lilly’s report.[10]
  • Cagrilintide. Stomach side effects hit 41% to 63% vs 32% on placebo in phase 2.[11] In REDEFINE 1 they hit 54.0% on cagrilintide alone, vs 73.8% on semaglutide alone and 79.6% on CagriSema.[12]

Our analysis: petrelintide’s numbers look gentler than the Wegovy and Zepbound labels, and cagrilintide alone was gentler than semaglutide in REDEFINE 1. Those drugs also produced less weight loss, so part of the difference may simply be a weaker effect. Eloralintide’s higher arms were not gentle. A 2026 review argues that how these drugs engage the related calcitonin receptor may shape whether eating less reflects normal fullness or “aversive signaling,” which is unresolved.[21]

Safety signals

  • Low blood sugar with insulin. Symlin’s boxed warning: “SYMLIN use with insulin increases the risk of severe hypoglycemia, particularly in patients with type 1 diabetes.”[19] The newer drugs have mostly been tested in people not on insulin, so this risk is not well characterized for them.
  • Fatigue. Common with eloralintide at higher arms (up to 46% vs 12%).[7] Zealand reports “no unexpected safety signals” with petrelintide, “including for alopecia, fatigue, and neuropsychiatric adverse events.”[3]
  • Injection-site reactions. In REDEFINE 1, 16.9% on cagrilintide alone vs 3.0% on placebo.[12]
  • Pancreatitis. Four people (0.2%) on CagriSema in REDEFINE 1.[12]
  • Serious events and deaths. “No deaths were reported” in ZUPREME 1; the eloralintide phase 1 trial reported no deaths and one serious adverse event, judged unrelated to the drug.[1][8]
  • Long term. The longest placebo-controlled amylin data run 68 weeks, and no outcomes trial of an amylin drug has reported.[12][22] Rare side effects may not show up until much larger groups are treated.

When could any be approved?

  • CagriSema (cagrilintide plus semaglutide): filed with the FDA in December 2025; Novo expects a decision in Q4 2026, and Optum lists December 18.[13][14] It would be the first approved weight-loss drug containing an amylin drug, but it is a combination, not an amylin drug alone.
  • Cagrilintide alone: RENEW 1 and 2 have estimated primary completion in May 2027.[24]
  • Eloralintide: seven phase 3 trials are registered; the earliest estimated primary completion is January 2028.[23]
  • Petrelintide: phase 3 began in September 2026, with 64-week endpoints; ZUPREME-3 and -4 have estimated primary completion in December and October 2028.[5][22]

Our analysis: registry dates are sponsor estimates and often slip, and a filing and FDA review typically follow results. Barring a change in plans, a stand-alone amylin weight-loss drug looks unlikely to be approved before 2028.

The case for amylin drugs, in fairness

Amylin drugs work through a different hormone from GLP-1 drugs, so they could suit people who don’t tolerate GLP-1 drugs, and they add to GLP-1 drugs in combination.[20][12] Petrelintide reported placebo-like stopping rates in two trials, and eloralintide’s best phase 2 arms approached Zepbound’s pivotal result.[3][4][7][16] Large phase 3 programs are running, with about 7,000 people planned for petrelintide alone.[5][23][24] Our analysis: the phase 3 trials will show whether the early tolerability holds in bigger, longer trials.

Legal status

In the US, no amylin drug is approved for weight loss. Pramlintide is approved only for diabetes alongside mealtime insulin, and petrelintide, eloralintide and cagrilintide are investigational.[19][13][22][23] We do not name or rate sellers, pharmacies or clinics.

What a careful reader should notice

These points are our analysis of the sources cited.

  1. Most amylin trials so far are phase 2: tens to a few hundred people, for 26 to 48 weeks. Wegovy and Zepbound were judged on phase 3 trials of about 2,000 to 2,500 people.[1][7][11][15][16]
  2. Two of the newest results are company toplines (ZUPREME-2 and Lilly’s EloraTZP trial), not peer-reviewed papers.[4][10]
  3. Check the estimand. Petrelintide and eloralintide headline numbers use the efficacy estimand; STEP 1 and REDEFINE 1 headline the treatment-policy estimand, which usually gives smaller numbers.[1][7][15][12]
  4. Check who was enrolled. Eloralintide’s phase 2 trial was 78% women and petrelintide’s 53%, and Zealand says women lost “considerably more weight” than men.[7][1][3]
  5. Placebo comparisons can mislead. In Lilly’s type 2 diabetes trial, 16.7% on placebo stopped for adverse events, more than on several active arms.[10]
  6. Read the disclosures. ZUPREME 1 was funded by Zealand, the eloralintide trials by Lilly and the cagrilintide trials by Novo, and many authors disclose ties to these or other drugmakers. That does not make the results wrong.[1][7][8][11][12][2]

Frequently asked questions

What is an amylin drug?
A drug that mimics amylin, a pancreatic hormone released with insulin that slows stomach emptying and helps end meals. Petrelintide, eloralintide and cagrilintide are long-acting, once-weekly versions in testing.[20][5][7][24]
Do amylin drugs work better than Wegovy or Zepbound?
Not on their own, in most trials so far: about 9% to 12% weight loss vs 14.9% for Wegovy and up to 20.9% for Zepbound in their main trials. Eloralintide reached about 20% in one phase 2 trial. These are cross-trial comparisons.[1][11][12][7][15][16]
Do amylin drugs cause less nausea than Ozempic or Wegovy?
Petrelintide reported 20% nausea vs 6% on placebo, below the 44% on Wegovy’s label, but eloralintide reached 64% in one arm. No head-to-head trial has tested it.[1][17][7]
What is the difference between cagrilintide and CagriSema?
Cagrilintide is Novo’s amylin drug. CagriSema is a fixed-dose combination of cagrilintide and semaglutide, the drug in Wegovy.[13]
When will petrelintide be approved?
Not soon. Its phase 3 trials started in September 2026, with main results estimated in late 2028.[5][22]
When will eloralintide be available?
Its phase 3 trials have estimated primary completion from January 2028, so approval would come later.[23]
Is any amylin drug approved for weight loss?
No. Pramlintide (Symlin) is approved only for diabetes alongside mealtime insulin. CagriSema has an FDA decision expected in Q4 2026.[19][13]

Bottom line

Amylin drugs alone have so far produced less weight loss than Wegovy or Zepbound, except in one eloralintide phase 2 trial.[1][11][12][7][15][16] Petrelintide’s early tolerability looks promising, but nausea is still the main side effect, and long-term safety is unknown.[1][3][7] None is approved for weight loss. CagriSema’s FDA decision is expected in Q4 2026, while stand-alone amylin drugs await phase 3 results due from 2027 to 2028.[13][24][23][22]
