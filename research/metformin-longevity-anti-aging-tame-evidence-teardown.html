Study teardown · Metformin for longevity and the TAME trial · The mouse lifespan studies, the observational survival studies, the large randomized trials and every 2025-2026 trial in older adults that we found, the US drug label, registry records and PubMed counts · Sources checked October 26, 2026

Published October 26, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026

Metformin for longevity: does the diabetes drug slow aging, and is it safe? No human trial has tested whether it extends life, and the TAME trial proposed in 2016 has no registry record we could find. Mouse results are mixed, the famous survival data are observational and disputed, and most recent trials in older adults missed their main goals. It carries a boxed warning for lactic acidosis.

Metformin is a decades-old prescription drug for type 2 diabetes, and researchers have proposed it as the first drug to be tested against aging itself.[1][2] In 2016, a perspective paper proposed doing that in a trial called TAME (Targeting Aging with Metformin).[3] Does it work? In mice, results are mixed: one 2013 NIA lab study found longer life in male mice, but the multi-site NIA testing program found metformin alone “did not significantly extend lifespan.”[4][5] In people, no trial has measured lifespan, and we found no ClinicalTrials.gov record for TAME.[25][24] The survival data that made metformin famous are observational and conflicting.[7][8] Of the five randomized trials in older adults in our chart, four missed their main goals or showed no benefit, and the fifth, posted as a preprint, had mixed results.[12][14][15][17][19] Is it safe? It has a boxed warning for lactic acidosis, can lower vitamin B12, and commonly causes stomach side effects.[1]

Why this matters now

2026 has brought a run of metformin-in-aging results, and most are negative or mixed. On July 30, 2026, The Lancet Healthy Longevity published DEMFOS, a 114-person randomized trial in adults aged 65-85 with obesity. Adding metformin to an intensive lifestyle program did not improve physical function, and the authors wrote that the results “support prioritising lifestyle-based strategies.”[15] An accompanying commentary was titled “Adjunctive metformin for obesity and frailty in older people-puncturing inflated expectations?”[16] In April, the METFORAGING pilot in older people with HIV missed its main goal on epigenetic age.[17] In July, a 145-person frailty trial was posted as a preprint, not yet peer reviewed: metformin improved one frailty measure but not the trial’s registered main measure.[19] Small, short trials and a meta-analysis reported some positive marker or test results.[20][21] A September 2026 review still describes “limited randomized clinical evidence.”[22] Our analysis: the evidence that metformin slows aging in people without diabetes has not grown stronger this year.

What metformin is

Metformin is a prescription tablet approved in the US “as an adjunct to diet and exercise to improve glycemic control in adults and pediatric patients 10 years of age and older with type 2 diabetes mellitus.”[1] The aging idea comes from its effects on metabolism: a 2013 mouse study reported that it “mimics some of the benefits of calorie restriction,” and a small crossover trial in about 70-year-olds found it changed the activity of metabolic and other genes in muscle and fat.[4][13] Researchers have called it “the first drug tested to target aging” in people, while noting that it is not clear whether its benefits apply to people without age-related disease.[2]

The evidence, rung by rung

  • Mice. A 2013 NIA lab study found metformin from middle age extended healthspan and lifespan in male mice.[4] The NIA Interventions Testing Program, which tests compounds at three sites in genetically varied mice, found metformin alone did not significantly extend lifespan; metformin plus rapamycin did, but that comparison was against earlier rapamycin-only studies.[5] A 2024 reanalysis using a test more sensitive to earlier deaths reported that metformin increased survival in males only.[6]
  • People: lifespan. We found no human trial that measured whether metformin extends life. A PubMed search for randomized trials of metformin mentioning lifespan or longevity returned 5 records: a mouse study, a trial design paper and three studies of blood or tissue markers.[25]
  • Survival studies (observational). A 2014 UK records study of 78,241 people on metformin found they had longer survival than matched people without diabetes.[7] A 2022 Danish register study, including twin pairs, found higher mortality with metformin than without diabetes and concluded it “was not associated with survival equal or superior” to the general population without diabetes.[8] A 2025 analysis of 438 matched women aged 60 or older found lower risk of death before 90 on metformin than on a sulfonylurea, but the authors write that “causality cannot be inferred.”[9]
  • Muscle and physical function in older adults. In MASTERS, placebo users gained more lean mass and thigh muscle from strength training than metformin users.[12] MET-PREVENT, in 72 people with a mean age of 80, found no difference in walk speed and more hospital admissions on metformin.[14] DEMFOS, in 114 older adults with obesity, found no added benefit over lifestyle change.[15]
  • Frailty. In a 145-person, two-year trial posted as a preprint, metformin improved a frailty index but not the Fried frailty score, which the registry lists as the main outcome; the Fried score worsened in year 1, which the authors attribute to weight loss.[19]
  • Aging biomarkers. METFORAGING, in 40 older people with HIV, found no significant difference in epigenetic age, its main goal.[17] A secondary analysis found metformin users lost 2.8 kg more than placebo users, and their weight drifted back toward baseline after stopping.[18] The frailty preprint reported lower readings on two epigenetic clocks, and a 24-week Indian trial in 127 people with prediabetes reported telomere lengthening that the authors say “requires cautious interpretation.”[19][20]
  • Brain. A 2026 meta-analysis of five studies reported “a small but positive effect” on overall cognitive function in older adults.[21] Our analysis: five pooled studies is a thin base.
  • Diabetes and cancer. Metformin prevents some new cases of type 2 diabetes, though the DPP lifestyle program did better.[10] In the MA.32 trial in 3,649 people with breast cancer, adding metformin did not improve disease-free survival.[11]

The key trials, in one chart

Our analysis: the two large trials were not aging trials. The DPP was about preventing diabetes and MA.32 about breast cancer, and only the DPP met its main goal.[10][11] The five trials aimed at aging-related outcomes were small, and none met a main goal cleanly.[12][14][15][17][19]

The case for metformin, in fairness

Metformin has been used for decades, and its main risks are well described on its label.[1] It prevents some new diabetes, and diabetes speeds many age-related diseases.[10][22] Some mouse data, the 2024 reanalysis and some observational studies point toward longer survival.[4][6][7][9] In 2026, small trials reported improvements in a frailty index, epigenetic clocks, telomeres and, in a meta-analysis, cognition.[19][20][21] Large trials are still running: VA-IMPACT plans to enroll an estimated 7,410 people with prediabetes and artery disease, and ANTHEM has finished and posted results.[23] Our analysis: these are reasons to keep testing, not evidence that metformin slows aging in healthy people.

Safety

  • Lactic acidosis. The label carries a boxed warning: postmarketing cases “have resulted in death, hypothermia, hypotension, and resistant bradyarrhythmias.” Listed risk factors include kidney impairment, age 65 or older, contrast imaging, surgery, heavy drinking and liver impairment.[1]
  • Vitamin B12. “Metformin may lower vitamin B12 levels,” the label says.[1]
  • Common side effects. The most common are diarrhea, nausea and vomiting, gas, weakness, indigestion, abdominal discomfort and headache. Low blood sugar risk rises when it is combined with insulin or drugs that increase insulin release.[1]
  • In older trial participants. In MET-PREVENT, adverse events were more common on metformin (108 vs 77), and 34% on metformin had hospital admissions versus 8% on placebo; the authors call it “poorly tolerated in this population.”[14]
  • Exercise. MASTERS suggests metformin may blunt muscle gains from strength training in healthy older people.[12]

Legal status

In the US, metformin is a prescription drug approved for type 2 diabetes. It is not approved to slow aging, prevent frailty or extend life, so prescribing it for those purposes would be off-label.[1] We do not name or rate sellers, pharmacies or clinics.

What a careful reader should notice

These points are our analysis of the sources cited.

  1. “Extends lifespan” is a mouse claim, and even there the NIA’s multi-site program did not confirm it for metformin alone.[4][5]
  2. The famous human survival result is observational. A later study including twins did not reproduce it.[7][8]
  3. TAME has been discussed since 2016, but we found no ClinicalTrials.gov record for it, so there are no TAME results to cite.[3][24]
  4. Check the main goal. MET-PREVENT, DEMFOS and METFORAGING missed theirs, and the frailty preprint’s positive finding was on a different measure than its registered main outcome.[14][15][17][19]
  5. A preprint is not peer reviewed. Its findings may change.[19]
  6. Biomarkers are not outcomes. Lower clock readings or longer telomeres in short trials do not show that people live longer or healthier.[19][20][17]
  7. Read the disclosures. An author of MET-PREVENT and the DEMFOS commentary discloses consultancy fees from companies including Rejuvenate Biomed; the DEMFOS authors declare none. That does not make either paper wrong.[14][16][15]

Frequently asked questions

Does metformin slow aging?
Not shown in people. No human trial has measured lifespan, and recent trials in older adults mostly missed their main goals.[25][12][14][15][17]
What is the TAME trial?
TAME (Targeting Aging with Metformin) was proposed in 2016 as a trial of metformin against aging. We found no ClinicalTrials.gov record for it, and it has no published results.[3][24]
Does metformin extend lifespan in mice?
Mixed. A 2013 NIA lab study found longer life in male mice, but the multi-site NIA testing program found no significant gain from metformin alone; a 2024 reanalysis reported a male-only effect.[4][5][6]
Do people with diabetes on metformin live longer than people without diabetes?
A 2014 UK study suggested so, but a 2022 Danish study including twins did not find it. Both are observational.[7][8]
Does metformin interfere with exercise?
In the MASTERS trial, healthy people 65 and older on metformin gained less muscle from strength training than those on placebo.[12]
Is metformin safe?
It has a boxed warning for lactic acidosis, which can be fatal, can lower vitamin B12 and commonly causes stomach side effects. In one trial in frail older people it was poorly tolerated.[1][14]
Is metformin approved for anti-aging?
No. In the US it is approved for type 2 diabetes; use for aging would be off-label.[1]

Bottom line

Metformin is a useful diabetes drug, but the case that it slows aging rests on mixed mouse data and disputed observational studies.[4][5][7][8] No human trial has tested lifespan, TAME has no registry record we could find, and 2025-2026 trials in older adults mostly missed their main goals.[25][24][14][15][17][19] It carries a boxed warning for lactic acidosis.[1]
