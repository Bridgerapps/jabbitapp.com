Study teardown · Tirzepatide (Zepbound, Mounjaro) vs semaglutide (Wegovy, Ozempic) · Head-to-head trials, meta-analyses, health records, FDA labels, a court docket and news reports · Sources checked October 26, 2026

Published October 26, 2026 · Updated October 26, 2026

Zepbound vs Wegovy: which works better? In the only head-to-head obesity trial, people on Zepbound lost 20.2% of their weight vs 13.7% on Wegovy over 72 weeks, and pooled studies point the same way, with smaller gaps in real-world records. Serious side effects may be somewhat more common on tirzepatide. On October 26, 2026, a federal judge declined to block Lilly’s comparison ads while Novo Nordisk’s lawsuit goes on, Reuters reported. No trial has compared Zepbound with higher-dose Wegovy HD, or compared heart attacks or deaths.

Tirzepatide (sold as Zepbound for weight loss and Mounjaro for diabetes) and semaglutide (Wegovy and Ozempic) are the two leading drugs in the newest class of weight-loss medicines, and only one randomized trial has compared them head to head in people with obesity. In that trial, SURMOUNT-5, 751 adults without diabetes lost 20.2% of their body weight on tirzepatide vs 13.7% on semaglutide over 72 weeks.[3] The trial was open-label and funded by Lilly, which makes Zepbound.[3] A 2026 meta-analysis of 10 head-to-head studies with 41,381 people found 4.28 percentage points more weight loss with tirzepatide, and more serious side effects.[6] Real-world health records show smaller losses on both drugs, with median use of only about 4.6 months.[10] On October 26, 2026, Reuters reported that a federal judge in New Jersey denied Novo Nordisk’s request for a preliminary injunction against Lilly’s comparison ads; the lawsuit continues.[1][2]

Why this matters now

Reuters reported on October 26, 2026 that the judge found Novo Nordisk had not shown that Lilly’s ads “threatened sales, undermined its goodwill, or caused lasting damage to its clinical reputation,” the showing needed for a preliminary injunction.[1] Novo’s July lawsuit says the Zepbound and Mounjaro ads compared higher doses of Lilly’s drugs with lower doses of Wegovy and Ozempic, according to Reuters; one ad said Zepbound users lost 50 pounds on average vs 33 on Wegovy.[1] Lilly has denied wrongdoing and said it was pleased with the decision; Novo and its lawyers did not immediately respond, Reuters reported.[1] Our analysis: denying a preliminary injunction is not a final ruling that the ads are true or false. It means the court did not order them stopped while the case goes on.[1][2] For anyone choosing between the two drugs, the ruling changes nothing about the evidence, so we set out what the trials actually show.

How the two drugs differ

Semaglutide mimics one gut hormone, GLP-1. Tirzepatide acts on two, GLP-1 and GIP; Lilly describes it as a dual receptor agonist.[4] Both are weekly injections; Wegovy also comes as a tablet.[18] Both carry the same boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents, and both labels say it is unknown whether the drug causes these tumors in people.[17][18]

The evidence, rung by rung

  • The head-to-head trial. SURMOUNT-5 randomized 751 adults with obesity, or overweight plus a weight-related condition, without diabetes, in the US and Puerto Rico. Each group took its drug’s highest tolerated dose for 72 weeks. Weight fell 20.2% on tirzepatide vs 13.7% on semaglutide, and waist size fell 18.4 cm vs 13.0 cm.[3][4]
  • Diabetes. In SURPASS-2, all three tirzepatide doses beat the semaglutide dose tested for blood sugar, and people lost more weight.[5] Novo’s court motion lists the SURPASS-2 paper and FDA approval letters for higher Wegovy and Ozempic doses among its exhibits, the docket shows.[2]
  • Pooled studies. A 2026 meta-analysis of 10 head-to-head studies (41,381 people, including observational studies) found 4.28 percentage points more weight loss on tirzepatide.[6] An updated review of 38 randomized trials reported weight loss beyond placebo of up to 19.0% for tirzepatide and 14.8% for injected semaglutide, but said differences between trials prevented pooling the numbers.[7] A network meta-analysis noted “limited head-to-head evidence, and residual uncertainty.”[8] An indirect comparison by Lilly employees also favored tirzepatide.[9]
  • Subgroups. SURMOUNT-5 analyses found similar gaps in 383 people meeting Japan’s obesity-disease criteria (20.1% vs 12.9%), and more people reaching 15% weight loss by week 24 on tirzepatide (44% vs 21%).[13][12]
  • Real life. In US health records covering over a million people with anti-obesity drug use, weight loss reached up to 9.6% on tirzepatide and 6.2% on semaglutide; median use was about 4.6 months, far shorter than the trials.[10]

SURMOUNT-5, in one chart

Our analysis: the 50 and 33 pound figures Reuters describes appear to match the average losses Lilly reported for SURMOUNT-5, 50.3 and 33.1 pounds, a result Lilly’s release footnotes as not controlled for multiple testing.[1][4] The percentage results, the trial’s main goal, were tested formally.[3] Whether the ads fairly describe the comparison is for the court to decide, and we take no view on it.

Wegovy HD: the comparison no one has run

SURMOUNT-5 was run before the FDA approved Wegovy HD in March 2026.[3][16] In Novo’s STEP UP trial, the higher dose produced 18.7% weight loss vs 15.6% for the standard dose over 72 weeks, with more stomach side effects and more altered skin sensation (dysaesthesia).[15] The FDA said the skin-sensation reports were common, more frequent at the higher dose, and under further investigation.[16] Our analysis: STEP UP and SURMOUNT-5 enrolled different people, in different countries, with different blinding, so their numbers cannot be lined up to say how Wegovy HD compares with Zepbound. Only a head-to-head trial can answer that, and we found none.[15][3]

The case for Wegovy, in fairness

Wegovy’s label includes reducing the risk of heart attack, stroke and cardiovascular death in adults with established heart disease and obesity or overweight, and a liver indication (MASH); Zepbound’s label lists sleep apnea and does not list a heart-outcome indication.[18][17] Serious side effects were slightly less common on semaglutide in both head-to-head trials and in pooled studies.[3][5][6] Our analysis: for someone with heart disease, the outcome evidence on the label can matter as much as extra weight loss.

Safety

  • Serious side effects. In SURMOUNT-5, 4.8% on tirzepatide vs 3.5% on semaglutide had a serious adverse event, with no deaths in either group; in SURPASS-2 it was 5 to 7% vs 3%.[3][5] The 2026 meta-analysis found serious adverse events more frequent with tirzepatide (relative risk 1.83), with overall and stomach side effects similar.[6]
  • Stopping because of side effects. 6.1% vs 8.0% in SURMOUNT-5; no significant difference in pooled studies.[3][6]
  • Stomach and skin. Nausea was about equal (43.6% vs 44.4%); vomiting was less common on tirzepatide (15.0% vs 21.3%), and injection-site reactions more common (8.6% vs 0.3%).[3]
  • Reports to the FDA. A 2026 analysis of FDA adverse-event reports found different reporting patterns for the two drugs, including more low-blood-sugar reports with tirzepatide; the authors call all findings hypothesis-generating.[14]
  • Thyroid. Both labels carry a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents.[17][18]

Healthy aging: what is and isn’t known

In a SURMOUNT-5 analysis of 425 people with prediabetes, 89.9% on tirzepatide vs 76.2% on semaglutide returned to normal blood sugar.[11] Our analysis: that is a promising sign for diabetes prevention, but it is a post hoc analysis of a Lilly-funded trial. No trial has compared the two drugs on heart attacks, strokes or deaths. Lilly’s SURMOUNT-MMO outcome trial of tirzepatide, with 15,374 people, lists primary completion in October 2027.[19]

Legal status

Zepbound and Wegovy are FDA-approved prescription drugs; this page does not compare prices or sellers.[17][18] The lawsuit, Novo Nordisk Inc. v. Eli Lilly and Company, No. 3:26-cv-09027 in the District of New Jersey, is a false-advertising case under the Lanham Act; according to Reuters, Lilly denies wrongdoing.[2][1] When we checked, the October 26 decision was not yet posted on the public docket, so the details here come from Reuters.[2][1]

What a careful reader should notice

These points are our analysis of the sources cited.

  1. One trial carries the head-to-head case in obesity. It was open-label and Lilly-funded, though its results fit the pooled studies.[3][6]
  2. A preliminary-injunction ruling is about harm while the case is pending, not about who is right.[1]
  3. As Reuters describes it, the dispute turns on comparing higher doses of Lilly’s drugs with lower doses of Novo’s. Wegovy HD has not been tested against Zepbound.[1][16][15]
  4. Real-world weight loss is much smaller than in trials, likely in part because people in health records stayed on the drugs for a median of only about 4.6 months.[10]
  5. The serious side effect difference is small in absolute terms, but it points the same way in both head-to-head trials and the pooled studies.[3][5][6] Quitting for side effects does not show the same pattern.[3][6]

Frequently asked questions

Is Zepbound better than Wegovy for weight loss?
On average, yes in the only head-to-head obesity trial: 20.2% vs 13.7% weight loss over 72 weeks. The trial was open-label and Lilly-funded, and individual results vary.[3]
What did the judge decide about Lilly’s Zepbound ads?
Reuters reported on October 26, 2026 that a federal judge denied Novo Nordisk’s request for a preliminary injunction. That is not a final ruling on the ads, and the case continues; Lilly denies wrongdoing.[1][2]
Where do the 50 vs 33 pound figures come from?
They appear to match the SURMOUNT-5 averages Lilly reported in 2025, 50.3 vs 33.1 pounds, a result Lilly footnoted as not controlled for multiple testing.[1][4]
Is Zepbound or Wegovy safer?
Neither is clearly safer. Serious side effects were somewhat more common on tirzepatide in pooled studies, while slightly fewer people quit it for side effects in SURMOUNT-5.[6][3]
How does Wegovy HD compare with Zepbound?
No trial has compared them. Wegovy HD beat the standard Wegovy dose in STEP UP, but comparing across trials is unreliable.[15][16]
Does either drug lower heart attack risk?
Wegovy’s label includes reducing major cardiovascular events in adults with heart disease and obesity or overweight. Zepbound’s does not; Lilly’s outcome trial lists completion in 2027.[18][17][19]
Why do people lose less weight in real life?
In US health records, median use was about 4.6 months, and losses reached up to 9.6% on tirzepatide and 6.2% on semaglutide.[10]

Bottom line

The trial evidence consistently favors tirzepatide (Zepbound) over standard-dose semaglutide (Wegovy) for weight loss, by about 4 to 7 percentage points, with serious side effects possibly a little more common.[3][6][5] Wegovy has a heart-outcome indication on its label that Zepbound lacks, and higher-dose Wegovy HD has never been tested against Zepbound.[18][17][16] The October 26 ruling left Lilly’s ads in place for now; it did not settle the science or the lawsuit.[1][2]
