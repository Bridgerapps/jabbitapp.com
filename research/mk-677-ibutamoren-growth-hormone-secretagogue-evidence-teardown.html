Study teardown · MK-677 (ibutamoren, MK-0677, LUM-201; sold online as an oral “growth hormone peptide”) · 30 years of records from Merck’s 1990s program to 2026: the short hormone studies, six longer placebo-controlled trials, the heart-failure signal, today’s pediatric and obesity trials, FDA’s safety-risk listing, product-testing studies, WADA, registry, FAERS and PubMed counts · Sources checked October 25, 2026

Published October 25, 2026 · Updated October 30, 2026

MK-677 (ibutamoren): does the oral “growth hormone peptide” work, and is it safe? It is not a peptide, it reliably raises growth hormone and IGF-1, and in Merck’s trials it added about a kilogram of lean mass, but it did not improve strength, function, Alzheimer’s disease or most bone sites, it raised blood sugar, one trial was stopped early for a heart-failure signal, and FDA lists it as a compounding safety risk.

MK-677, also called ibutamoren, is a small synthetic molecule taken by mouth that makes the pituitary gland release more growth hormone.[1][11] It is often sold alongside injectable peptides, but it is not a peptide: it copies the action of ghrelin, the body’s hunger hormone, at the same receptor.[2] Merck’s researchers described it in 1995, and in trials over the next 15 years it reliably raised growth hormone and IGF-1.[1][3][13][14] The outcomes people buy it for mostly did not follow: in healthy older adults it added about a kilogram of lean mass over a year without improving strength or function, it did not reduce body fat, it did not slow Alzheimer’s disease, and it raised blood sugar.[13][8][14] A trial in hip-fracture patients was stopped early for a heart-failure signal, and FDA now lists it among compounding ingredients that may present significant safety risks.[15][32] One company is still testing it as a prescription drug candidate, under the name LUM-201, for children with growth hormone deficiency.[18][20]

Why this matters now

MK-677 turns up often in the illegal performance-product market: in a five-year study by European official medicines control laboratories of 324 suspected illegal performance products, ibutamoren was listed first among the five most frequently reported molecules, and 24% of the samples were presented as dietary supplements.[25] A 2026 sports-medicine review of popular “peptides” singled out MK-677 as associated with significant risks including congestive heart failure.[31] Four of the five bulk-powder listings for ibutamoren in FDA’s drug directory have 2026 marketing start dates, which shows raw material is being registered, not that any product is approved.[37] Meanwhile, a phase 3 trial in children began recruiting in May 2026, and in August 2026 a phase 2 trial pairing it with oral semaglutide in older adults with obesity was registered.[20][21] FDA’s interim peptide compounding policy cleared White House review on October 27, 2026; the version HHS made public on October 28 lists six peptides, and ibutamoren is not among them.[35] For where every peptide stands, see our FDA peptide compounding status tracker.

What MK-677 is

Ibutamoren is a “nonpeptide” growth hormone secretagogue: a drug-like molecule, not a chain of amino acids, designed to be taken by mouth once a day.[11][2] It acts on the growth hormone secretagogue receptor, the same receptor that ghrelin uses, which is why researchers call it a ghrelin mimetic; mice lacking that receptor confirmed this.[2][13] That is a different route from sermorelin or CJC-1295, which act on the receptor for growth hormone-releasing hormone, but its action was described as indistinguishable from that of the peptide GHRP-6.[1] Because it works through the pituitary, it raises the body’s own growth hormone pulses rather than replacing the hormone: in young men, the total amount of growth hormone was similar to placebo but pulses were more frequent, while IGF-1 rose.[4] It also acts like ghrelin on appetite: increased appetite was the most common side effect in the 2-year trial.[13]

The evidence, rung by rung

  • Hormone levels. In 32 healthy adults aged 64 to 81, four weeks of treatment raised IGF-1 into the range of young adults.[3] IGF-1 rose in every longer trial too: by 84% in hip-fracture patients and by 72.9% in Alzheimer’s patients.[12][14]
  • Lean mass and fat. In 65 healthy adults aged 60 to 81 followed for up to 2 years, fat-free mass rose 1.1 kg on MK-677 and fell 0.5 kg on placebo, with no difference in belly or total fat; limb fat and body weight rose more on the drug.[13] In 24 obese men over 8 weeks, fat-free mass rose but total and visceral fat did not change.[8] In calorie-restricted volunteers, a week of treatment reversed protein (nitrogen) loss.[7]
  • Strength and function. In the 2-year trial, “Increased fat-free mass did not result in changes in strength or function,” though the authors note the study was too small to judge function.[13] After hip fracture, a 161-patient trial found no significant functional differences; a later trial saw better gait speed but not stair-climbing power, and it was stopped early.[12][15]
  • Sleep. One small study reported about 50% more deep (stage IV) sleep and over 20% more REM sleep in 8 young adults after a week, and nearly 50% more REM sleep in 6 older adults.[5] We found no larger trial with sleep as its main outcome.[38][23]
  • Bone. It raised markers of bone turnover in 187 older adults.[9] In 292 women with osteoporosis, adding it to alendronate raised femoral-neck density more than alendronate alone (4.2% vs. 2.5%) but not spine, total hip or whole-body density, which the authors called “a concern when weighed against the potential side effects.”[10]
  • Brain. In 563 people with Alzheimer’s disease, 12 months of treatment “was ineffective at slowing the rate of progression.”[14] In Alzheimer’s-model mice it did not prevent plaques or memory loss and raised mortality at one dose.[16]
  • Liver fat. A 12-person open-label pilot at Massachusetts General Hospital found no significant change in liver fat after 6 months.[22]

The longer trials, in one chart

Our analysis: across 1,228 people in these six trials, the drug did what it was designed to do to hormone levels every time, but the clinical outcomes it was tested for did not clearly improve, apart from modest lean-mass and single-site bone gains.[13][8][12][15][10][14] Registry enrollment figures differ from some published ones, so we use the numbers in the papers.[23]

Where development stands: LUM-201

Lumos Pharma is developing ibutamoren as LUM-201 for children whose pituitary still works partly.[18][17] Its analysis of an earlier completed trial in 68 children proposed two screening tests to pick likely responders.[17] In its phase 2 trial of 104 children selected that way, average height growth over the first 6 months, expressed per year, was 6.8 to 8.2 cm across the LUM-201 groups versus 10.6 cm with daily growth hormone injections, according to the results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov.[19] A placebo-controlled phase 3 trial of about 150 children began recruiting in May 2026.[20] Separately, a phase 2 trial registered in August 2026 plans to test LUM-201 plus oral semaglutide against semaglutide alone in 202 adults aged 60 to 84 with obesity, measuring physical function.[21] Our analysis: these are company-sponsored trials still in progress, with no results for adults yet, so they do not show that MK-677 bought online works or is safe for anyone.[20][21]

Safety

  • Heart failure signal. The phase 2b hip-fracture trial “was terminated early due to a safety signal of congestive heart failure in a limited number of patients,” and its authors concluded the drug “has an unfavorable safety profile in this patient population.”[15] FDA cites that trial in listing ibutamoren as a significant safety risk.[32]
  • Blood sugar. Fasting glucose rose from 5.4 to 6.8 mmol/L after 4 weeks in older adults; glucose tolerance worsened in obese men; and over 2 years, fasting glucose rose and insulin sensitivity fell.[3][8][13]
  • Other effects in trials. More appetite that faded after a few months, short-lived mild leg swelling, and muscle pain were the most frequent in the 2-year trial, which also saw weight gain, a rise in cortisol and a small drop in LDL cholesterol.[13]
  • Case reports. Single case reports describe liver inflammation after 2 months of use that resolved after stopping, gynecomastia in a man whose supplements also contained other drugs and hormones, and a ruptured spleen in a man also using a SARM, where the authors call any role of the compounds “potential and speculative.”[28][29][30] Case reports cannot show cause.
  • What is in the bottle. Testing studies have found ibutamoren in products sold as other drugs or as supplements, sometimes not on the label, and mislabeling was common; doping researchers describe it as “not available therapeutically” yet widely sold online as “supplement” products.[24][26][25][27]
  • Reports. FAERS holds 14 reports naming ibutamoren or MK-677; such reports are unverified and do not show cause.[36]

Legal status

No ibutamoren drug is approved by FDA.[36] It is on FDA’s list of bulk substances that may present significant safety risks in compounding, for outsourcing facilities since December 29, 2022 and for pharmacies since September 29, 2023, and FDA’s May 2026 list places it in category 2, substances that raise significant safety risks.[32][33] FDA’s interim peptide policy, made public by HHS on October 28, lists six peptides, and ibutamoren, which is not a peptide, is not one of them.[35][2] Five bulk-powder listings exist in FDA’s NDC directory, and a listing is not approval.[37] In sport, WADA’s 2026 list names “ibutamoren (MK-677)” among growth hormone secretagogues prohibited at all times.[34]

What a careful reader should notice

These points are our analysis of the sources cited.

  1. Raising IGF-1 is not the same as a benefit. MK-677 raised it in every trial, yet the strength, function, bone and brain outcomes mostly did not follow.[13][12][10][14]
  2. The lean-mass gain was about a kilogram over a year in older adults, and fat did not fall. “Recomp” claims are not what the trials showed.[13][8]
  3. The best data come from older or sick people. Young, healthy gym users, the main online market, were barely studied beyond a week or two.[4][5][13]
  4. The heart-failure signal came in frail patients, and its size is not reported in the abstract, but FDA treats it as serious enough to list the drug as a safety risk.[15][32]
  5. “Peptide” is a marketing label here. Ibutamoren is a small molecule, which also means peptide-specific policies may not apply to it.[11][35]

Frequently asked questions

Does MK-677 work?
It reliably raises growth hormone and IGF-1. In trials it added about a kilogram of lean mass over a year in healthy older adults, but it did not improve strength or physical function, did not reduce body fat, did not slow Alzheimer’s disease, and improved bone density at only one of four sites.[3][13][8][14][10]
Is MK-677 safe?
Trials found higher blood sugar and lower insulin sensitivity, plus appetite, swelling and muscle pain, and one trial in hip-fracture patients was stopped early for a heart-failure signal. FDA lists it as a significant safety risk for compounding, and products sold online have often been mislabeled.[3][13][15][32][24]
Is MK-677 a peptide?
No. It is a small, nonpeptide molecule taken by mouth that mimics ghrelin at the growth hormone secretagogue receptor.[11][2]
Is MK-677 FDA approved?
No. There is no approved ibutamoren product, and FDA places it in category 2 for compounding because of safety risks. A company is testing it as LUM-201 in children with growth hormone deficiency.[36][33][20]
Does MK-677 improve sleep?
One small 1997 study in 14 people reported more deep and REM sleep over 1 to 2 weeks. That has not been confirmed in larger trials that we found.[5][38]
Does MK-677 raise blood sugar?
Yes, in several trials: fasting glucose rose in older adults, glucose tolerance worsened in obese men, and insulin sensitivity fell over 2 years.[3][8][13]
Is MK-677 banned in sport?
Yes. WADA’s 2026 Prohibited List names ibutamoren (MK-677) among growth hormone secretagogues, prohibited at all times.[34]

Bottom line

MK-677 had a long, well-funded drug program behind it, and the record is clear on one thing: it raises growth hormone and IGF-1.[1][13][14] What the trials did not show is that this translates into more strength, less fat, better recovery after a fracture, or protection against Alzheimer’s disease, while blood sugar rose and one trial was stopped for a heart-failure signal.[13][8][12][14][15] It is not approved, FDA lists it as a compounding safety risk, and it is banned in sport.[36][32][34] People concerned about low growth hormone, muscle loss or sleep can ask a doctor about tested, approved options; see our sermorelin and CJC-1295 and ipamorelin teardowns for the related growth-hormone peptides.
