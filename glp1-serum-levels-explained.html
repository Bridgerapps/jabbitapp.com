Semaglutide & tirzepatide half-life: what a level model can—and cannot—show

A pharmacokinetic curve is an estimate, not a blood test or a symptom forecast. Current U.S. labels give useful population facts about time to maximum concentration, steady state, and elimination. They do not establish that appetite or side effects peak on a particular day of the week. [1] [2] [3] [4]

This page is educational. It does not recommend a dose, injection time, titration, switch, or stop date. Use the current label for the exact product and take schedule questions to the prescriber.

Three PK terms that are not interchangeable

Time to maximum concentration

When the population curve peaks

For injected semaglutide, the Ozempic and Wegovy labels say maximum concentration is reached one to three days after a dose. [1] [2] For injected tirzepatide, Mounjaro gives an 8-to-72-hour range; Zepbound gives a 24-hour median with the same range. [3] [4]

Elimination half-life

A decay parameter, not a “gone” date

The labels give semaglutide an approximate one-week half-life. Mounjaro gives tirzepatide an approximate five-day half-life; Zepbound gives about five to six days in its described populations. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Steady state

Repeated-dose context

The Ozempic label says steady-state exposure is reached after four to five weeks of once-weekly use. The Mounjaro and Zepbound labels say steady-state plasma concentrations are reached after four weeks of once-weekly use. These are label population estimates, not a personal concentration measurement. [1] [3] [4]

Model literacy

How to read a logged-dose model

This is a conceptual model-literacy frame, not a specification of any app’s algorithm.

1 · Logged input

An administration record

A logged-dose model starts with an administration record. A missing or incorrect entry means the model has a missing or incorrect input.

2 · Population parameter

A pharmacokinetic rule

The model applies an assumed pharmacokinetic parameter such as half-life. That parameter is an input, not a personal assay.

3 · Estimated output

A current and projected curve

The output estimates a trajectory. It can preserve timing context without claiming to measure blood or predict symptoms.

What the curve cannot prove

It is not a measurement

  • It does not report an assayed blood concentration.
  • It does not establish a personal therapeutic threshold.
  • It does not show that a medicine is effective or ineffective.

It is not a symptom clock

  • It cannot prove why hunger, nausea, fatigue, or another event happened.
  • It cannot turn time to maximum concentration into a “strongest effects” day.
  • It cannot choose a dose, titration, injection time, or schedule.

Injection site does not supply a shortcut

All four labels say overall exposure was similar after subcutaneous administration in the abdomen, thigh, or upper arm. That does not support using this page to claim that one of those sites produces a reliably faster personal peak. Follow the product’s administration instructions and take site questions to the prescriber. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Why “day 3” is not a symptom rule

A semaglutide label range of one to three days describes when maximum concentration is reached in pharmacokinetic data. It does not say appetite suppression, nausea, or any other effect must be strongest on day three. The tirzepatide labels likewise provide concentration timing—not a symptom schedule. Timing can be recorded as context, but proximity to a modeled peak does not establish cause. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Build a timing record a prescriber can read

Keep the observation separate from the interpretation. A concise record can preserve:

Exact product and formulation

Record the brand and whether the product is injected or oral. Do not substitute a class name for the exact label.

What actually happened

Keep the administration date and time, the prescribed amount as recorded, and any missed or changed event.

Neutral symptom timing

Write what was noticed and when. “Nausea began Tuesday morning” preserves sequence without declaring the cause.

The unresolved question

Keep “Is this timing meaningful?” as a question for the prescriber, not a conclusion produced by the graph.

Frequently asked questions

How long does semaglutide stay in the body?

The Ozempic label gives semaglutide an elimination half-life of about one week and says it remains in circulation for about five weeks after the last dose. The current Wegovy label also gives a roughly one-week half-life and says it remains in circulation for about five to seven weeks after the last listed injectable dose. Half-life is not the same as a personal stop date or a dosing instruction. [1] [2]

How long does tirzepatide stay in the body?

The Mounjaro label gives tirzepatide an approximate five-day elimination half-life. The Zepbound label gives about five to six days in the populations it describes. A half-life does not mean the medicine is fully gone after that interval. [3] [4]

Does a serum-level chart measure my blood?

No. A pharmacokinetic chart generated from logged administrations and population parameters is a model, not a blood draw or a measured personal concentration.

Does the highest modeled level predict the strongest appetite effect or side effects?

No. The labels report time-to-maximum-concentration ranges, but they do not establish a personal day-of-week rule for appetite, nausea, or other symptoms. Timing near a modeled peak does not prove causation. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Can a modeled curve tell me when to change a dose or schedule?

No. A modeled curve can organize timing context, but it cannot choose a dose, change a schedule, or determine whether a medicine is working. Those decisions belong with the current label and the prescriber.
