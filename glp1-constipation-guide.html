Constipation is one of the most common side effects of GLP-1 medications like Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound. While it can be uncomfortable, understanding why it happens and how to manage it can make your GLP-1 journey much smoother.

Why GLP-1 Medications Cause Constipation

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) medications work by slowing down gastric emptying—the process where food moves from your stomach to your small intestine. This slowdown has several effects:

  • Reduced intestinal motility: Your digestive system moves more slowly, allowing more water to be absorbed from waste, making stools harder and drier
  • Decreased appetite: You eat less, which means less bulk to stimulate bowel movements
  • Altered hormone signaling: GLP-1 affects gut hormones that regulate digestion
  • Dehydration risk: Some people drink less due to reduced thirst sensation
How common is it? Studies show 20-40% of people taking GLP-1 medications experience constipation. It's more common when starting or increasing doses.

Prevention Strategies

The best approach is to start preventive measures before constipation becomes a problem:

1. Start Fiber Early

Begin increasing your fiber intake before starting your GLP-1 medication or at the very beginning. Aim for 25-35 grams daily from whole foods.

2. Prioritize Hydration

Drink at least 8-10 glasses of water daily. GLP-1 medications can reduce your sensation of thirst, so set reminders to drink.

3. Establish a Routine

Try to have a consistent time each day to sit on the toilet. The gastrocolic reflex (bowel movement urge after eating) is strongest in the morning and after meals.

4. Stay Active

Regular physical activity helps stimulate intestinal motility. Even a 15-20 minute walk can make a difference.

Relief & Treatment Options

Over-the-Counter Solutions

  • Stool softeners: Docusate sodium helps soften stool making it easier to pass
  • Osmotic laxatives: Polyethylene glycol (Miralax) draws water into the intestines
  • Fiber supplements: Psyllium husk (Metamucil) or methylcellulose (Citrucel)
  • Senna: Natural stimulant laxative for short-term use
  • Magnesium citrate: Helps relax bowels and draw in water

Hydration Guidelines for GLP-1 Users

Proper hydration is crucial when on GLP-1 medications:

  • Aim for 64-100 oz (2-3 liters) of water daily
  • Spread intake throughout the day
  • Increase water during exercise or hot weather
  • Avoid excessive caffeine (can be dehydrating)
  • Limit carbonated beverages (can cause bloating)
Pro tip: Carry a reusable water bottle and set hourly reminders to drink. Your body may not send clear thirst signals while on GLP-1s.

Fiber Guidelines

Fiber is your friend, but there's a right way to add it:

  • Increase gradually over 1-2 weeks
  • Drink plenty of water with fiber supplements
  • Good sources: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes
  • Aim for variety—different fibers feed different gut bacteria
Don't suddenly eat tons of fiber: This can cause bloating, gas, and even worsen constipation. Slow and steady wins the race.

When to See Your Doctor

While constipation is common, certain symptoms warrant medical attention:

  • No bowel movement for 5+ days despite treatment
  • Severe abdominal pain or distension
  • Blood in stool
  • Vomiting
  • Signs of dehydration (dark urine, dizziness)
  • Hemorrhoids or anal tears
Don't suffer in silence: Your prescriber has many tools in their toolkit—from dosage adjustments to prescription medications—to help manage side effects.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does constipation last on GLP-1s?

It varies. Some people experience it only during the first few weeks (titration period), while others may have ongoing issues. Most find that their body adjusts over time.

Will constipation go away on its own?

Often yes, as your body adjusts to the medication. However, for some people it persists and requires ongoing management strategies.

Can I take laxatives with GLP-1 medications?

Yes, most over-the-counter laxatives are safe. Start with gentle options like stool softeners or Miralax. Avoid regular use of stimulant laxatives without medical guidance.

Does constipation mean the medication isn't working?

No. Constipation is a side effect, not an indicator of effectiveness. Many people lose weight successfully without experiencing constipation.

Can I prevent constipation by changing when I take my GLP-1?

Taking your injection in the morning may help—you'll be more likely to stay hydrated and active during the day. Some people find this reduces digestive discomfort.

What foods should I avoid?

Limit processed foods, dairy (if sensitive), red meat, and foods high in fat. These can slow digestion further. Also reduce cheese and bread which can be binding.
