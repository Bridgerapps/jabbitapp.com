Progesterone in oil (Progesterone Injection, USP): what the US label says

Progesterone in oil is progesterone dissolved in sesame oil for intramuscular injection. Its US label lists one use: amenorrhea and abnormal uterine bleeding due to hormonal imbalance. Luteal-phase support in IVF isn’t on its US label.[1] If your clinic prescribes it, your clinic’s instructions are the ones to follow. This page sets out what the Progesterone Injection, USP label (Fresenius Kabi, revised October 2016) says.

What it is

The label says: “Each mL contains: Progesterone 50 mg, benzyl alcohol 10% as preservative in sesame oil q.s.”[1]

What it’s approved for in the US

The label’s indication, quoted: “This drug is indicated in amenorrhea and abnormal uterine bleeding due to hormonal imbalance in the absence of organic pathology, such as submucous fibroids or uterine cancer.”[1] IVF, including support of the luteal phase, isn’t among its US indications, and the label’s dosing section covers only amenorrhea and functional uterine bleeding. It gives no dosing for IVF.[1]

Route and how it’s supplied

The label says: “For intramuscular use only.” It is supplied as “Progesterone Injection, USP, 500 mg per 10 mL, (50 mg per mL) multiple dose vial, packaged individually.”[1] This page doesn’t cover injection sites or technique; ask your clinic about those.

Does progesterone in oil need to be refrigerated?

No, according to this label: “Store at 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [see USP Controlled Room Temperature].”[1] If your vial came from a different labeler, follow the label that came with it.

Side effects on the label

The label lists these adverse reactions without rates:[1]

  • Breakthrough bleeding, spotting, change in menstrual flow, amenorrhea
  • Edema; change in weight (increase or decrease)
  • Changes in cervical erosion and cervical secretions
  • Cholestatic jaundice
  • Breast tenderness and galactorrhea
  • Pain, irritation and/or redness at the injection area
  • Skin sensitivity reactions consisting of urticaria, pruritus, edema and generalized rash; acne, alopecia and hirsutism; rash (allergic) with and without pruritus
  • Anaphylactoid reactions
  • Mental depression, insomnia, somnolence
  • Pyrexia (fever) and nausea

Warnings on the label

The label says: “The physician should be alert to the earliest manifestations of thrombotic disorders (thrombophlebitis, cerebrovascular disorders, pulmonary embolism, and retinal thrombosis). Should any of these occur or be suspected, the drug should be discontinued immediately.”[1]

Who the label says shouldn’t use it

  • Current or past history of thrombophlebitis, thromboembolic disorders, or cerebral apoplexy[1]
  • Liver dysfunction or disease[1]
  • Known or suspected malignancy of breast or genital organs[1]
  • Undiagnosed vaginal bleeding[1]
  • Missed abortion[1]
  • Known sensitivity to progesterone injection[1]

FAQ

Is progesterone in oil FDA-approved for IVF?
Not on the US label we checked. Progesterone Injection, USP is indicated for amenorrhea and abnormal uterine bleeding due to hormonal imbalance, and its dosing section covers only those uses. IVF luteal-phase support isn’t on its US label; your clinic decides whether and how you use it.
Does progesterone in oil need to be refrigerated?
No. The Fresenius Kabi label says to store it at 20–25°C (68–77°F), USP controlled room temperature.
What oil is it in?
The label says each mL contains progesterone 50 mg and benzyl alcohol 10% as preservative in sesame oil. It comes in a 10 mL multiple-dose vial (500 mg per 10 mL).
What are the side effects of progesterone in oil?
The label lists, without rates, changes in menstrual bleeding, edema, weight change, breast tenderness, pain, irritation and/or redness at the injection area, skin reactions, anaphylactoid reactions, depression, insomnia, somnolence, fever and nausea, among others. It warns about thrombotic disorders.
