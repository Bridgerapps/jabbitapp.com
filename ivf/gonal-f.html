Follitropin alfa (Gonal-f and Gonal-f RFF Redi-ject): what the US labels say

Follitropin alfa is a recombinant human follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) made in a Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cell line. In the US it’s sold as Gonal-f, a multi-dose vial of powder, and Gonal-f RFF Redi-ject, a prefilled pen. This page sets out what their US prescribing information (both revised March 2025) says about approved uses, supply, storage, side effects and warnings.[1][2]

What the labels say it is

The Redi-ject label says: “Follicle stimulating hormone (FSH), the active component in GONAL-f RFF Redi-ject, is required for normal follicular growth, follicular maturation, and gonadal steroid production.”[2] The Gonal-f label says it stimulates ovarian follicular growth in women who do not have primary ovarian failure, and that to bring about final maturation of the follicle and ovulation without an LH surge, hCG must be given after Gonal-f when monitoring shows sufficient follicular development. In men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, given with hCG, it stimulates spermatogenesis.[1]

What it’s approved for in the US

  • Induction of ovulation and pregnancy in oligo-anovulatory infertile women for whom the cause of infertility is functional and not due to primary ovarian failure (Gonal-f and Gonal-f RFF Redi-ject).[1][2]
  • Development of multiple follicles in ovulatory infertile women as part of an assisted reproductive technology (ART) cycle (Gonal-f and Gonal-f RFF Redi-ject).[1][2]
  • Induction of spermatogenesis in infertile men with primary and secondary hypogonadotropic hypogonadism for whom the cause of infertility is not due to primary testicular failure (Gonal-f multi-dose vial only).[1]

How it’s supplied

  • Gonal-f multi-dose vial: powder for subcutaneous injection, mixed before use with Bacteriostatic Water for Injection, USP (0.9% benzyl alcohol), which is supplied in a prefilled syringe. The label describes 450 International Units and 1050 International Units vials.[1] Your clinic tells you how to prepare it; this page doesn’t give mixing instructions.
  • Gonal-f RFF Redi-ject: a prefilled pen for subcutaneous injection that is ready to use and isn’t mixed. The label says not to mix other medications inside the pen.[2]

Does Gonal-f need to be refrigerated?

It depends on the product, according to the labels:

  • Gonal-f multi-dose vials: refrigerated at 2–8°C (36–46°F) or at room temperature, 20–25°C (68–77°F), protected from light. After mixing, the solution can be kept refrigerated or at room temperature, protected from light; the label says to discard any unused portion after 28 days.[1]
  • Gonal-f RFF Redi-ject: the pharmacy keeps it refrigerated at 2–8°C until dispensed. After that, refrigerate it until the expiration date, or store it at 20–25°C for up to three months or until the expiration date, whichever comes first. Keep it in the original carton to protect it from light, and don’t freeze it. After the first injection, the pen can be kept refrigerated or at room temperature for up to 28 days with the cap on; discard unused material after 28 days.[2]

The labels’ serious risks are: hypersensitivity reactions and anaphylaxis (dyspnea, facial edema, pruritus, urticaria); ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS); pulmonary complications (atelectasis, acute respiratory distress syndrome, exacerbation of asthma); venous or arterial thromboembolic events; ovarian torsion; abnormal ovarian enlargement; multi-fetal gestation and birth; ectopic pregnancy; spontaneous abortion; and ovarian neoplasms, for which a causal relationship has not been established.[1][2]

Warnings on the labels

  • Only physicians experienced in infertility treatment should treat women with these products; gonadotropin therapy requires appropriate monitoring facilities.[1][2]
  • OHSS may progress rapidly to a serious medical event, and it can develop after treatment stops. The labels say hCG is withheld if monitoring suggests an increased risk of OHSS.[1][2]
  • Serious systemic hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis, have been reported.[1][2]
  • Serious pulmonary conditions and venous or arterial thromboembolic events have been reported; in rare cases they have resulted in death.[1][2]
  • Ovarian torsion has been reported. If the ovaries are abnormally enlarged on the last day of therapy, hCG is not given.[1][2]
  • Multi-fetal gestation: in clinical trials, multiple births occurred in 20% of live births with ovulation induction and 35.1% of live births with ART.[1][2]
  • Congenital malformations after IVF or ICSI may be slightly higher than after spontaneous conception, and ectopic pregnancy may be increased in women undergoing ART. The risk of spontaneous abortion is increased with gonadotropin products; causality has not been established.[1][2]
  • Gonal-f is not indicated in pregnant women, and a negative pregnancy test is verified before use. The label advises women not to breastfeed during treatment.[1]
  • If a dose is missed, the labels say not to double the next dose and to call the healthcare provider.[1][2]

Who the labels say shouldn’t use it

The contraindications are: prior hypersensitivity to recombinant FSH products or one of their excipients (reactions have included anaphylaxis); high levels of FSH indicating primary gonadal failure; uncontrolled non-gonadal endocrinopathies (for example thyroid, adrenal or pituitary disorders); sex hormone dependent tumors of the reproductive tract and accessory organs; tumors of the pituitary gland or hypothalamus; and, in women, abnormal uterine bleeding of undetermined origin and ovarian cyst or enlargement of undetermined origin (the Redi-ject label adds: not due to polycystic ovary syndrome).[1][2]

What the label says about dosing

The Gonal-f label’s dosing statement for ART cycles, quoted:

“Beginning on cycle day 2 or 3, administer subcutaneously a starting dose of 150 International Units of GONAL-f daily until sufficient follicular development ... is attained.”

Gonal-f prescribing information, Dosage and Administration[1]

FAQ

What is Gonal-f?
Gonal-f is a brand name for follitropin alfa, a recombinant human FSH. Its US labels approve it for ovulation induction in oligo-anovulatory women and for developing multiple follicles in ART cycles; the multi-dose vial is also approved for inducing spermatogenesis in men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
Does Gonal-f need to be refrigerated?
The multi-dose vials may be kept refrigerated (2–8°C) or at room temperature (20–25°C), protected from light. The Gonal-f RFF Redi-ject pen is refrigerated until the expiration date or kept at 20–25°C for up to three months or until expiration, whichever comes first; after the first injection, it can be kept refrigerated or at room temperature for up to 28 days.
What’s the difference between Gonal-f and Gonal-f RFF Redi-ject?
Both are follitropin alfa. Gonal-f is a multi-dose vial of powder mixed with the supplied bacteriostatic water; Gonal-f RFF Redi-ject is a ready-to-use prefilled pen. Only the multi-dose vial’s label includes the spermatogenesis indication in men, and each label has its own storage and dosing sections.
