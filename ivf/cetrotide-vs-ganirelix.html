Cetrorelix (Cetrotide) vs ganirelix: what the two US labels say, side by side

Cetrorelix acetate (sold as Cetrotide) and ganirelix acetate are both GnRH antagonists. Their US labels list the same purpose in almost the same words: inhibiting premature LH surges in women undergoing controlled ovarian stimulation (Cetrotide’s label) or controlled ovarian hyperstimulation (ganirelix’s label).[1][2] This page sets the two labels’ facts side by side. It doesn’t recommend one over the other; your clinic chooses the medicine.

Reading the table (our notes)

  • Different trials. Each label reports its own studies (949 people for Cetrotide, 794 for ganirelix), run in different settings. A lower rate on one label doesn’t show that medicine causes fewer side effects. These notes are ours, not label statements.
  • Supply and storage are the clearest differences. The Cetrotide label describes a vial mixed with the supplied water and kept refrigerated; the ganirelix label we checked describes a ready-to-use prefilled syringe kept at room temperature.[1][2]
  • No recommendation. This page doesn’t rank them. Your clinic chooses the medicine and gives you the dose and the times.

FAQ

Is Cetrotide the same as ganirelix?
No. Both are GnRH antagonists whose US labels list inhibiting premature LH surges during controlled ovarian stimulation, but they are different medicines with different labels, supply and storage.
Which is better, Cetrotide or ganirelix?
This page doesn’t rank them. Each label reports its own trials, so their side-effect rates can’t be compared directly. Your clinic chooses the medicine.
Does Cetrotide or ganirelix need to be refrigerated?
The Cetrotide label says to store it refrigerated at 2–8°C. The Organon ganirelix label we checked says to store it at 25°C, with excursions permitted to 15–30°C. Both say to protect from light.
Does either need to be mixed?
Cetrotide is mixed with the supplied 1 mL of Sterile Water for Injection and used immediately. The ganirelix label we checked describes a ready-to-use prefilled syringe.
