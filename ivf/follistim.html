Follitropin beta (Follistim AQ Cartridge): what the US label says

Follitropin beta is a recombinant human follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) produced in a Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell line. In the US it’s sold as Follistim AQ Cartridge, a ready-to-use solution for the Follistim Pen. This page sets out what its US prescribing information (revised February 2026) says about approved uses, supply, storage, side effects and warnings.[1]

What the label says it is

The label says: “Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), the active component in FOLLISTIM AQ Cartridge, is required for normal follicular growth, maturation, and gonadal steroid production.” It stimulates ovarian follicular growth in women who do not have primary ovarian failure, and the label says hCG must be given afterwards to bring about final follicle maturation, resumption of meiosis and rupture of the follicle when there is no LH surge. In men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, given with hCG, it stimulates spermatogenesis.[1]

What it’s approved for in the US

  • Induction of ovulation and pregnancy in anovulatory infertile women in whom the cause of infertility is functional and not due to primary ovarian failure.[1]
  • Pregnancy in normal ovulatory women undergoing controlled ovarian stimulation as part of an in vitro fertilization (IVF) or intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) cycle.[1]
  • Induction of spermatogenesis in men with primary and secondary hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (HH) in whom the cause of infertility is not due to primary testicular failure.[1]

How it’s supplied

Follistim AQ Cartridge is a clear, colorless solution in a single-patient-use cartridge, for subcutaneous use only with the Follistim Pen. It isn’t mixed. The label says not to add any other medicines into the cartridge, and not to use it if the solution isn’t clear and colorless or has particles.[1]

Does Follistim need to be refrigerated?

  • At the pharmacy: refrigerated at 2–8°C (36–46°F) until dispensed; don’t freeze.[1]
  • At home, before first use: refrigerated at 2–8°C until the expiration date, or at room temperature up to 25°C (77°F) for 3 months or until the expiration date, whichever comes first.[1]
  • After first use: store at 2–25°C (36–77°F) and discard after 28 days.[1]
  • Keep it in the original carton to protect it from light, and don’t freeze it.[1]

The label’s serious risks are: ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome; abnormal ovarian enlargement; atelectasis and acute respiratory distress syndrome; thromboembolism; ovarian torsion; multi-fetal gestation and birth; ectopic pregnancy; spontaneous abortion; ovarian neoplasms (causal relationship not established); and hypersensitivity reactions including anaphylaxis, reported after marketing.[1]

Warnings on the label

  • It should be used only by physicians experienced in infertility treatment; gonadotropin therapy requires appropriate monitoring facilities.[1]
  • The cartridge with the pen injector delivers on average an 18% higher amount of follitropin beta than reconstituted Follistim given with a conventional syringe. The label says switching brands, types or methods of administration may require a dose adjustment, which is a decision for your clinic.[1]
  • OHSS may progress rapidly to a serious condition. In clinical trials it occurred in 7.6% of 105 women (ovulation induction) and 6.4% of 751 women (IVF or ICSI). The label says hCG must be withheld if there is a risk of OHSS.[1]
  • Serious pulmonary conditions and venous or arterial thromboembolic reactions have been reported with gonadotropins; in rare cases they have resulted in death.[1]
  • Ovarian torsion has been reported. If the ovaries are abnormally enlarged on the last day of therapy, the label says hCG should not be given.[1]
  • Multi-fetal gestation and births have been reported with all gonadotropin treatments.[1]
  • Congenital malformations after IVF or ICSI may be slightly higher than after spontaneous conception, and ectopic pregnancy may be increased. The risk of spontaneous abortion is increased with gonadotropin products; causality has not been established.[1]
  • The Follistim Pen is not recommended for the blind or visually impaired without the help of a person with good vision trained in its use.[1]
  • If a dose is missed, the label says not to double the next dose and to call the healthcare provider.[1]

Who the label says shouldn’t use it

The contraindications are: prior hypersensitivity to recombinant hFSH products or any component of Follistim AQ Cartridge; high levels of FSH indicating primary gonadal failure; uncontrolled non-gonadal endocrinopathies (for example thyroid, adrenal or pituitary disorders); hypersensitivity to streptomycin or neomycin (the product may contain traces of these antibiotics); tumors of the ovary, breast, uterus, testis, hypothalamus or pituitary gland; and, in women, pregnancy, heavy or irregular vaginal bleeding of undetermined origin, and ovarian cysts or enlargement not due to polycystic ovary syndrome.[1]

What the label says about dosing

The label’s dosing statement for IVF or ICSI cycles, quoted:

“A starting dose of 200 international units (actual cartridge doses) of FOLLISTIM AQ Cartridge is administered ... subcutaneously daily for at least the first 7 days of treatment.”

Follistim AQ Cartridge prescribing information, Dosage and Administration[1]

FAQ

What is Follistim AQ?
Follistim AQ Cartridge is the brand name for follitropin beta, a recombinant human FSH in a ready-to-use cartridge for the Follistim Pen. Its US label approves it for ovulation induction, for controlled ovarian stimulation in IVF or ICSI cycles, and for inducing spermatogenesis in men with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
Does Follistim need to be refrigerated?
Before first use, the label says to keep it refrigerated (2–8°C) until the expiration date, or at room temperature up to 25°C for 3 months or until expiration, whichever comes first. After first use, store it at 2–25°C and discard it after 28 days.
Does Follistim AQ need to be mixed?
No. The label describes a ready-to-use solution in a cartridge that is used only with the Follistim Pen, and says not to add any other medicines into the cartridge.
Is Follistim the same as Gonal-f?
Both are recombinant FSH, but they are different molecules from different labels: Follistim AQ is follitropin beta and Gonal-f is follitropin alfa. The Follistim label says switching brands, types or methods of administration may require a dose adjustment; only your clinic can decide that.
