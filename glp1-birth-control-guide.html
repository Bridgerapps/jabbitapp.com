GLP-1 medications and birth control: what the labels actually say

Direct answer: Mounjaro and Zepbound tell clinicians to advise a non-oral method, or an added barrier method, for 4 weeks after the medicine is started and for 4 weeks after each dose escalation, if the person is using an oral hormonal contraceptive. Ozempic and Wegovy do not contain that instruction. Pregnancy language is a separate question, and it is not the same on all four labels. [1] [2] [3] [4]

These are four labels, not one class rule

Mounjaro and Zepbound are both tirzepatide. Ozempic and Wegovy are both semaglutide. The shared ingredient does not make the pregnancy or contraception text interchangeable. This page stays inside the current US prescribing information for those four brand names: Mounjaro revised 8/2026, Zepbound revised 8/2026, Ozempic revised 5/2026, and Wegovy revised 6/2026. The Wegovy label that was opened covers Wegovy injection and Wegovy tablets. Compounded products are outside this page. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Oral hormonal contraceptives

The useful split is not "GLP-1s and birth control." It is whether the opened label contains a specific oral-contraceptive instruction.

Recognized pregnancy and a planned pregnancy are different sentences

A contraception paragraph does not answer what a label says about pregnancy. Zepbound's reason for stopping in a recognized pregnancy is that weight loss offers no benefit and may cause fetal harm. Mounjaro and Ozempic use a benefit-risk sentence and describe risks of poorly controlled diabetes. Wegovy's wording depends on the labeled use, and the highlights do not match the full section word for word.

What this page will not decide

It will not choose a contraceptive, interpret a pregnancy test, or tell anyone to start, stop, or time a dose. The 4-week and 2-month figures above are quotations from specific labels, not a schedule written for the person reading this. A missed dose, a switch between Wegovy injection and tablets, or a change in contraceptive method needs the prescriber and the current label for the exact product in hand.

Animal reproduction findings on these labels coincided with effects on maternal weight or food consumption. That does not turn an animal study into a human risk number, and the labels say the human pregnancy data are limited or insufficient. [1] [2] [3] [4]

Dates worth bringing to the prescriber

The Mounjaro and Zepbound oral-contraceptive sentences are anchored to two events: the day the medicine is started, and each day the dose is escalated. A prescriber can apply the label only if those dates, and the contraceptive actually in use, are written down. Blank and "unknown" are valid. Do not guess a date to fill a box. [1] [2]

Frequently asked questions

What do the Mounjaro and Zepbound labels say about oral hormonal contraceptives?

Both current labels say use may reduce the efficacy of oral hormonal contraceptives because of delayed gastric emptying. That delay is largest after the first dose and diminishes over time. Both tell clinicians to advise patients using oral hormonal contraceptives to switch to a non-oral contraceptive method or add a barrier method for 4 weeks after initiation and for 4 weeks after each dose escalation. Both say hormonal contraceptives that are not administered orally should not be affected. [1] [2]

Do the Ozempic and Wegovy labels give that same 4-week instruction?

No. The Ozempic and Wegovy labels opened for this page do not tell clinicians to switch contraceptive methods or add a barrier method. Ozempic says semaglutide did not affect absorption of orally administered medications to any clinically relevant degree in clinical pharmacology trials, and still says to use caution with oral medications. Its pharmacokinetics section says no clinically relevant interaction was observed for the evaluated medicines, including an oral contraceptive assessed at steady state, at semaglutide 1 mg steady-state exposure. Wegovy says it delays gastric emptying and may affect oral medications. It reports that semaglutide 1 mg once-weekly injection did not affect absorption of orally administered medications, and that the oral-tablet studies found no clinically significant pharmacokinetic difference for ethinyl estradiol or levonorgestrel. [3] [4]

What do the labels say if a pregnancy is recognized?

Zepbound says to discontinue Zepbound when a pregnancy is recognized, because weight loss offers no benefit to a pregnant patient and may cause fetal harm. Mounjaro and Ozempic do not use that sentence. Each says to use the medicine during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus, and each describes risks of poorly controlled diabetes in pregnancy. Wegovy's highlights say to discontinue Wegovy when pregnancy is recognized for CV risk reduction or weight reduction, and to use a benefit-risk standard for MASH. The full Wegovy pregnancy section says to discontinue Wegovy in pregnant patients using it for weight reduction. [1] [2] [3] [4]

What do the labels say about a planned pregnancy?

Ozempic says to discontinue Ozempic in women at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy because of semaglutide's long washout. Wegovy says to discontinue Wegovy at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy because of semaglutide's long half-life. The highlights add an indication qualifier that the full section 8.3 sentence does not. The Mounjaro and Zepbound labels opened for this page do not contain a 2-month pre-pregnancy instruction. Timing still belongs with the prescriber and the current label for the exact product. [1] [2] [3] [4]

What is worth writing down before that conversation?

The tirzepatide labels attach the oral-contraceptive advice to the start of treatment and to each dose escalation. A useful record is the exact product, the dates those events happened, and whether the contraceptive in use is oral. It does not choose a contraceptive or decide whether to stop a medicine. [1] [2]
