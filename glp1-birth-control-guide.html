GLP-1 and Birth Control: Interactions & What You Need to Know

If you're taking GLP-1 medications like Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, or Zepbound and use birth control, you may wonder about potential interactions. Here's what the research and medical guidance tell us.

Key Takeaways

  • GLP-1 medications are not known to directly interact with hormonal birth control
  • However, GLP-1s can cause nausea and vomiting, which may affect oral contraceptive absorption
  • Weight loss from GLP-1s may require dose adjustments of some contraceptives
  • Always discuss medication interactions with your healthcare provider

How GLP-1 Medications Work

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) receptor agonists work by:

  • Slowing gastric emptying (how quickly food leaves your stomach)
  • Reducing appetite and promoting feelings of fullness
  • Improving insulin sensitivity
  • Helping regulate blood sugar levels

GLP-1s and Hormonal Birth Control: The Research

What We Know

Current clinical trials and FDA documentation do not list hormonal contraceptives as having significant interactions with GLP-1 medications. This includes:

  • Birth control pills (combined estrogen-progestin and progestin-only)
  • Contraceptive patches
  • Vaginal rings
  • Intrauterine devices (IUDs)
  • Contraceptive implants

The Nausea Factor

While GLP-1s don't directly interact with birth control, they commonly cause nausea—especially during the first few weeks. If you experience vomiting after taking your oral contraceptive:

  • The pill may not be fully absorbed
  • Effectiveness could be reduced
  • Consider using backup contraception during nausea episodes
  • Talk to your provider about alternative birth control methods

Weight Loss and Birth Control Considerations

Significant weight loss can affect how your body processes medications. Some considerations:

Dose Adjustments

If you lose a substantial amount of weight, discuss with your provider whether your current contraceptive dose needs adjustment. Some birth control methods are weight-based.

Non-Hormonal Options

Some women prefer non-hormonal options while on GLP-1 therapy to eliminate any potential variables. These include:

  • Copper IUDs (non-hormonal)
  • Barrier methods (condoms, diaphragms)
  • Fertility awareness methods

What the Medications Say

Official prescribing information for GLP-1 medications notes that clinical trials did not show interactions with oral contraceptives. However, manufacturers recommend:

  • Taking birth control at a different time than your GLP-1 injection
  • Using backup contraception if you experience significant GI side effects
  • Discussing any concerns with your prescribing provider

Talking to Your Provider

Before starting GLP-1 therapy, discuss these questions with your healthcare provider:

  1. Will my current birth control method still be effective?
  2. Should I change when I take my contraceptive relative to my GLP-1 injection?
  3. What should I do if I experience vomiting after taking my pill?
  4. Do I need to use backup contraception during the initial titration period?
  5. Would a non-hormonal IUD be a better option for me?

Bottom Line

GLP-1 medications are not known to chemically interact with hormonal birth control. The main concern is gastrointestinal side effects (nausea, vomiting) that could affect absorption of oral contraceptives. If you experience these side effects, use backup contraception and discuss alternatives with your provider.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Ozempic and Wegovy make birth control less effective?

No direct interaction exists, but if you experience vomiting or diarrhea (common side effects), your oral contraceptive may not be fully absorbed. Use backup protection during GI symptoms.

Can I take birth control pills with Mounjaro?

Yes, clinical trials showed no direct interaction. Take them at different times of day and use backup contraception if you have nausea or vomiting.

Should I switch to a non-hormonal birth control method?

This is a personal decision. Many women successfully use hormonal birth control with GLP-1 medications. Discuss with your provider to determine what's best for your situation.

What if I become pregnant on GLP-1s?

GLP-1 medications are not recommended during pregnancy. If you become pregnant, contact your healthcare provider immediately. Discontinue GLP-1 therapy and work with your provider on a safe transition.

Do I need to use two forms of birth control?

Current guidance doesn't require dual methods, but using backup contraception during the initial GLP-1 titration period (when side effects are most common) is prudent.
